Title: The Sun in (Al-A'la, At-Tin, Ash-Shams, Al-Layl, and Ad-Duha)
Excerpt: There is a complete, overall correspondence between Surat Al-A'la, Ash-Shams, and Al-Layl, namely that they move step by step from the creation of the heavens and the earth to the creation of man to the human soul, then its splitting into two kinds according to its striving in purification, then the clarification of the two paths of guidance and misguidance, and the outcome of each path in this world and the Hereafter.

There is a complete, overall correspondence between Surat Al-A'la, Ash-Shams, and Al-Layl, namely that they move step by step from the creation of the heavens and the earth to the creation of man to the human soul, then its splitting into two kinds according to its striving in purification, then the clarification of the two paths of guidance and misguidance, and the outcome of each path in this world and the Hereafter.

Except that Surat Al-A'la is more general than Ash-Shams, and Ash-Shams is more general than Surat Al-Layl, and Surat Ad-Duha is more specific than both, and At-Tin is more specific still.

So observe with me, may Allah grant you the enjoyment of your sight:

(Surat Al-A'la)

Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High (1) Who created and proportioned (2) And Who decreed and then guided (3)

(Surat Ash-Shams)

By the sun and its morning brightness (1) And by the moon when it follows it (2) And by the day when it displays it (3) And by the night when it covers it (4) And by the sky and He who built it (5) And by the earth and He who spread it (6) And by the soul and He who proportioned it (7) And inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness (8) He has succeeded who purifies it (9) And he has failed who corrupts it (10).

(Surat Al-Layl)

By the night when it covers (1) And by the day when it appears (2) And by He who created the male and the female (3) Indeed, your efforts are diverse (4)

In Surat Al-A'la He made clear the Creator behind every creation, so He began with glorifying and exalting Him. Then His creating everything, of heavens and earth, animals, humans, and plants, in that He proportioned it in the best form, then decreed for it its measures and provisions, or in the nature of its creation, then guided it. So it is more summary, encompassing every creation and every kind of guidance, to the point that He guides the ant in carrying the grain on top of it.

And in Surat Ash-Shams He began with the creation of the day and the night, the sun and the moon, the sky and the earth, then the creation of the soul and its kinds, and that its purpose is striving to perfect it and attain its righteousness.

And likewise in Surat Al-Layl, along the same lines: in the surahs there is a wonderful order, even in terms of time and history, with the creation of the heavens and the earth, then the creation of man and his soul, and the goal of creation: his striving for guidance, and that there is no perfection for the soul except by perfecting guidance.

*Taghshiyah* (covering) and *tasjiyah* (shrouding):

In Ash-Shams and Al-Layl He used with the night the description of covering (And by the night when it covers it) (By the night when it covers).

And in Ad-Duha He used shrouding (And by the night when it grows still and dark).

And the reason -and Allah knows best- is that *ghishā'* (covering) is used for complete and total covering, while *tasjiyah* (shrouding) is used for intensity, and it was borrowed here with the night to show the intensity of the time.

Astronomers say that when evening falls, the sun does not set completely! It does not set completely until 8 p.m., and in some countries at eleven at night, and it varies with the seasons of winter and summer and with countries.

So the meaning may be: when the night covers, with the sun's complete disappearance from it, not merely its apparent setting to the naked eye.

And when the day appears, with the moon's complete disappearance from the daytime sky. So it is an expression of the completeness of the day and the completeness of the night, and of each being truly distinct from the other, not of an illusion of the eye.

As for *tasjiyah*: it is from tightly binding the shroud of the dead and covering him with cloth and covering. For the dead may be covered and fully shrouded, yet not tightly bound and fastened. Hence (And by the night when it grows still and dark) is the night's intensifying at its end, at the time of *suḥūr*.

Here the wonderful astronomical order of the surahs becomes clear to you! They are in an organized, precise orbit of time; Surat Ash-Shams and Al-Layl begin with the complete covering of night and day -let's suppose it is eleven o'clock- and Surat Ad-Duha is late at night, like three in the morning, for example.

Except that the meaning of Surat Ad-Duha is its clarifying the most intense times for purifying and refining the soul, as though Surat Ash-Shams poses a riddle: how is the soul purified, and when?

And Surat Ad-Duha answers that: it is when the time is most intense! And these are the voluntary prayers: the voluntary prayer of the day is the Duha prayer, and the voluntary prayer of the night is more effective for concurrence and more suitable for words.

The heavy can only be dealt with by something as heavy or heavier, so it makes no sense for a broken-down truck to be towed by a light bicycle! That is foolishness. Rather, it is the fullness of knowledge to bring a truck like it or heavier to tow it, move it, and bring it back to life again; and so is the soul, weighed down by desires, and these are what stop a person and hold him back from moving quickly and lightly and keep goodness and blessing from him, so there had to be something heavier than it to lead it, and that was the two revelations, the Quran and the Sunnah (Allah wants to accept your repentance, but those who follow their passions want you to deviate into a great deviation (27) Allah wants to lighten for you, and mankind was created weak (28) (An-Nisa)

Lighten what for us? The desires you read about in the verse before it!

And it is absurd to rely on the self alone in straightening and correcting it, through man-made Western methods and approaches; this is like a bicycle towing a truck! Either the rope between them snaps, or the bicycle rider's back gives way and breaks.

The approaches of the Quran and the Sunnah are indispensable in changing the human soul, in addition to human research and approaches, Western or Eastern, if their ideas are balanced and sound, for they complement one another, except that the two revelations are heavier and better.

We mentioned that the night and the day are meant in the sense of their completeness when they arrive and in their time; (And by He who created the male and the female) likewise: just as He told us about the completeness of the day and the night, He went on to tell us about the completeness of the creation of the male and the female, by each being completely distinct from the other in make-up.

*Tazkiyah* (purifying) and *tadsiyah* (corrupting)

(And by the soul and He who proportioned it (7) And inspired it with its wickedness and its righteousness (8) He has succeeded who purifies it (9) And he has failed who corrupts it (10)

We mentioned that He completed the creation of the night and the day and the creation of the bodily structure of the male and the female, and He also completed the creation of the human soul by granting it the gift of thought and feeling, and proportioned it in the best proportion and did not wrong it; yet it remains man's task to perfect his soul and proportion it until its darkness is distinct from its light, and that is by bringing it out, through the path of purification, from the darknesses into the clear light!

By earnest, serious striving toward guidance (Indeed, your efforts are diverse); and *sa'y* (striving) is faster than walking, as though He is saying: if you could attain guidance in a day and a night, then hasten to do it! Of course this is not possible, but because of the importance of achieving guidance and righteousness, so that his creation is completed by the completeness of his character, and this is the trust that man must fulfill.

Just as He completed the creation of the heavens and the earth for you and completed your creation in the best stage, the recompense for this divine goodness is that you complete the purification of your soul and perfect your righteousness (Is the reward for good anything but good?).

The wretched soul and the happy soul

Thamud denied in their transgression (11) When the most wretched of them was sent forth (12) And the messenger of Allah said to them: The she-camel of Allah, and her drink (13) But they denied him and hamstrung her, so their Lord brought down upon them destruction for their sin and leveled it (14) And He does not fear the consequence thereof (15).

When He finished mentioning the divisions of the happy and the wretched soul (And he has failed who corrupts it), He brought a real example of a particular wretched soul, namely the wretched one of the people of Thamud, and that this is the outcome of whoever does not strive for guidance and purify himself: his soul leads him astray until it makes him slip, seduces him, and makes him transgress.

So if you ask me: And why did He mention in Surat Ash-Shams the story of Thamud and not mention Pharaoh, for example, who was more wretched than him?

The surah is brief, so brevity suits it, and sufficing with one example of the wretched soul that did not purify itself; and whoever disobeyed Allah and His Messenger, this was his outcome in this world before the Hereafter.

And why has He not mentioned an example of the happy soul that purified itself?

It is in the nature of the human mind that when we have two opposite ideas, if we give an example of the worse, we automatically understand the example of the better; and this is intelligence, eloquence, and mastery of brevity.

The happy soul and the wretched soul: if I give you an example of the wretched soul and its consequences, you automatically understand from it the happy soul and its end.

And the meaning: if this is the outcome of the wretched soul in this world before the Hereafter, then likewise the happy soul, the one that purified itself, strove for guidance, and obeyed Allah and His Messenger, is happy in this world before the Hereafter.

So if you ask me: If He has made clear the worldly punishment of wretchedness, then where is the one of the Hereafter!?

We say that He has made it clear in Surat Al-A'la:

But the most wretched one will avoid it (11) He who will burn in the greatest Fire (12) Then he will neither die therein nor live (13) He has certainly succeeded who purifies himself (14) And mentions the name of his Lord and prays (15).

So the traits of the wretched soul in Ash-Shams are detailed (When the most wretched of them was sent forth), and his recompense in the Hereafter in Surat Al-A'la is clear (But the most wretched one will avoid it). Hence Surat Al-A'la is more general than Ash-Shams.

That whoever does not put his wretched soul to death here by striving and struggle, and bring the good soul to life by purification and give it a noble, good life, and does not believe in the best reward, will have a Fire in which he neither dies nor lives; and whoever brings his soul to life by reviving the night and the Quran and the voluntary prayers will have two lives = together they make a good life in this world and the Hereafter, and his life is the life of a nation, (and whoever saves one, it is as if he had saved mankind entirely), and is the life of the soul anything but by the Quran and the night prayer?

So He mentioned success in this world and the Hereafter for the one who purified his soul; *zakāh* includes the meaning of the purification of souls, as Ibn Kathir mentioned in his tafsir of verse 156 of Surat Al-A'raf).

So we glean from it that *ṣadaqah* (charity) -and Allah knows best- falls within the meaning of *taṣdīq* (believing) and *ṣiddīq* (the truthful one).

But you prefer the worldly life (16) While the Hereafter is better and more enduring (17) Indeed, this is in the former scriptures (18) The scriptures of Abraham and Moses (19) (Al-A'la)

And since some souls are in doubt about this talk, that Allah has a firm, fixed way in correcting, straightening, and guiding the soul, He sealed it with the firmest thing the soul trusts, namely writing, scriptures, and history; and that these are Allah's ways in straightening and balancing souls and in the good life, so much so that He mentioned them in the scriptures of Abraham (peace be upon him) and the Torah of Moses (peace be upon him), and that just as it is mentioned there, We mention it again here in the Quran, as confirmation of this truth and principle, so that it is confirmed to you that the truest approach to human change toward the highest and most complete is the Quran and the Sunnah and what they contain (So if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to you, then ask those who have been reading the Scripture before you. The truth has certainly come to you from your Lord, so never be among the doubters (94)

So the mention of the scriptures was not for rhyme in speech, far be it from that; rather, it was mentioned for whoever doubts these ways and laws, just as you say to someone who is in doubt about your words: If you don't believe me, ask so-and-so, he is the witness to what I'm saying.

(Surat At-Tin)

By the fig and the olive (1) And by Mount Sinai (2) And by this secure city (3) We have certainly created man in the best of stature (4) Then We return him to the lowest of the low (5) Except for those who believe and do righteous deeds, for they will have a reward uninterrupted (6) So what yet causes you to deny the Recompense? (7) Is not Allah the most just of judges? (8).

Like the pairs of night and day, male and female, and the wretched and the happy soul, here too He paired the fig and the olive; and what is the connection!?

If the limit of man's senses with the sun and the moon is sight, then the fig and the olive are within reach of the full range of senses, even the two most intimate senses: smell and taste, and that may be what is meant here!

The taste of the fig is sweet, and the taste of the olive is bitter.

So the pair of bitterness and sweetness is completed for us, and that the human soul tastes its misguidance as bitterness, pain, and misery, and tastes its guidance as the sweetness of faith, joy, and delight.

And Mount Sinai: because He revealed the Torah to Moses (peace be upon him) there. And this secure city: because revelation came down in it, and because Abraham (peace be upon him) built the Kaaba, may Allah honor it.

These two places were honored only because revelation came down in them, so what about a heart in which the Quran came down, and which memorized it, acted on it, and understood it! Would it not be the most honored heart and the most complete soul!

We have certainly created man in the best of stature: in body and soul,

But there are impurities and defects in it (Then We return him to the lowest of the low) except for whoever purifies it and cleanses it of them.

The most just of judges: the question of divine decree is not why Allah made this one rich and that one well-provided, for much of that is understood, but why did He guide this one and leave that one astray? So He directed us to the fact that decree belongs to Him (And Who decreed and then guided), and because all judgment and knowledge belong to Him; and just as we have accepted the other questions of decree, we must here likewise accept; do you not see that Noah (peace be upon him) called upon his Lord by this name (And Noah called to his Lord and said: My Lord, indeed my son is of my family, and indeed Your promise is true, and You are the most just of judges (45) (Hud)

So the Most Just of Judges guides whom He wills and leaves astray whom He wills.

The secret of complete contentment

He who gives his wealth to purify himself (18) And not for anyone who has a favor with him to be repaid (19) But only seeking the face of his Lord, Most High (20) And he is going to be satisfied (21).(Al-Layl)

And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied (5) (Ad-Duha)

Sincerity in individual worship is easy, but in communal acts of worship, and especially giving, it is very hard, except for whom Allah makes it easy; one must have sincerity when giving, by giving for Allah and withholding for Allah, and whoever chases after people in his giving, his soul burns out and he dies of grief. So we have found nothing more restful for the soul than giving for Allah; because this is what brings you contentment.

And this is frequent in the Quran:

And the example of those who spend their wealth seeking the pleasure of Allah … (265) Al-Baqarah

No good is there in much of their private conversation, except for one who enjoins charity or what is right or reconciliation between people. And whoever does that seeking the pleasure of Allah, We will give him a great reward (114) (An-Nisa)

And all of these are good deeds toward people; therefore He made clear that whoever's concern is the pleasure of Allah and not of people will have a great reward, which is being pleased with him and showering him with provision and gifts until he is satisfied; because the recompense is of the same kind as the deed: he gave only seeking the pleasure of Allah, so the recompense was that Allah gives to him until He satisfies him, and his soul is cooled.

(And Who brings out the pasture (4) And then makes it dark stubble (5) We will make you recite, and you will not forget (6) Except what Allah wills. Indeed, He knows what is declared and what is hidden (7) And We will ease you toward ease (8).(Al-A'la)

(And Who brings out the pasture and then makes it dark stubble) this suits this world and its states, but this is not the state of one who recites the Quran, for he is in a perpetual spring; hence after it came (We will make you recite, and you will not forget)

And this is the secret of the saying of the Chosen One ﷺ: "O Allah, make the Quran the spring of my heart", and he did not say the plant of my heart or the flower! Because of the property of withering, whereas spring is a season that lasts through days and months.

And whoever keeps carrying the Quran, this is always his state: in a joy that does not stop and a happiness that does not perish.

Then He made clear His knowledge of what is declared and what is hidden of the soul's hidden schemes; hence Surat Al-A'la is general, and Surat Ash-Shams is more specific than it, and so on with the rest of the surahs.

All of them are favors, except the Quran: (the favor of your Lord), an attribution of honor and glorification to the Mighty Lord.

And the favor of your Lord: for gentleness and reassurance.
