PubMed Central (PMC), the research archive of the U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH), has published a study that brings together strong, reliable evidence on the effect of the Noble Quran on mental and physical health. It is titled:

The Impact of Listening to, Reciting, or Memorizing the Quran on Physical and Mental Health of Muslims: Evidence From Systematic Review

In it, the researchers gathered 230 studies published in major, high-ranking scientific databases, such as PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar. Nor did they take just any study: they selected them only after assessing their methodology and analyzing their data, to make sure the results were not the product of chance or bias. Then they organized the evidence and findings, and the results were astonishing:

- Listening to Surah Ar-Rahman did much to help manage depression in pregnant women, and eased their anxiety about childbirth.

- Listening to Surah Ya-Sin has a profound psychological effect on one's sense of calm and tranquility.

- Listening to the Noble Quran in general reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety in kidney patients and heart patients.

- Listening to the Quran improves sleep quality

- Remarkably, when intensive care patients heard the Noble Quran, their vital-sign readings fell and their level of consciousness rose!

- Listening to the Quran improves physiological markers (regulating heart rate, breathing..)

- Listening to the Quran improves quality of life and average lifespan.

- The Quran gives the mind bright, hopeful beliefs about life and about the events and hardships that befall it, making it steadier and stronger.

- The more of the Quran one memorizes (surahs, juz', methods of review, and so on), the higher one's IQ.

- Memorizing the Quran has a bearing on brain atrophy: gray matter, white matter, and total brain volume were larger in those who had memorized the whole Quran than in those who had not memorized it, or had memorized only part of it. The explanation is that the one who memorizes keeps using and stimulating brain tissue, which helps preserve it.
