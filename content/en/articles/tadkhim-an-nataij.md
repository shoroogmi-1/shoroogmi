The cognitive nature of human beings dictates that we magnify the negative outcomes of the bad experiences that happen to us.

And this is a huge psychological trap!

And if you knew that this cognitive mindset was behind the collapse of giant companies, you wouldn't take lightly this nature you already know about.

Now, you can't change this nature.

- Got it!

But there are tactics you can use and force yourself into to break this cognitive nature: magnifying bad outcomes.

- What are they?

First- Just be aware of it, understand it, and recognize it when it shows up. Don't do anything else. This is a huge step forward.

- Okay, what's next!

Second- Create a happiness file! Digital or paper, whichever you like, and put in it the most important good news, kind comments, and positive moments in your life, and collect them in it!

And at the times when your brain naturally leans toward magnifying the negatives, force yourself, really force yourself, even if it doesn't want to, to open this file and read something from it!

You'll find that your sharp bad mood has broken, or let's say the magnifying of negatives in it has dropped from 100% to 70–60%

And what happens here is that you've broken this sharpness and bad magnification, which makes you more rational afterward in making decisions, so you don't act rashly and, blindly and heedlessly, make harsh decisions based on a sharp, elevated negative mood.

Always look for any enjoyable positive technique that lowers this sharpness (it might be an outing or a walk, or a talk with an old friend, or telling jokes and joking around with your colleague at work,, anything that improves your mood) and force yourself into it even if you don't want it, and say: O self, I want what's good for you! You must look at this file so you don't make reckless decisions and hurt people who've done nothing wrong because of a bad, high, sharp cognitive magnification!
