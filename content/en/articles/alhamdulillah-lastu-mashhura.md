Title: Praise Be to Allah That I Am Not Famous
Excerpt: A reflection on how obscurity can be a blessing, and how fame may sometimes bring regret and sorrow.

Praise be to Allah that I am not famous!

Some forms of fame lead to injustice: a person becomes involved in a case in which they are wronged—or become the one who wrongs others.

Someone may become famous, loved, and surrounded by admirers, only for a misstep to be recorded and spread everywhere. They regret it and grieve, then people abandon them as they would a carcass.

Some fame is built on ignorance, so the person’s words and actions are ignorant too. No matter how many millions it brings, it will soon fade away.

Perhaps someone becomes famous through a scandal—we ask Allah for well-being. So praise Him for concealing, protecting, and keeping you hidden from people, and for veiling your faults from them.

A person may enjoy great fame, numbers, and wealth in youth, then suffer a spectacular fall in their mid-thirties, cursed by people as their numbers decline after once being celebrated. It is as though they have been given a death sentence before death.

Another person may remain highly regarded by people throughout life, only to be exposed after death; their fame then becomes a curse. Ask Allah to grant us and you concealment at death, after death, and before death, and sincere repentance before it is too late.

Perhaps great fame brings regret, sorrow, bitter disappointment, and a depression that imprisons a person until they say, “If only I had been dust.”

These are distressing scenes, I know. But remember that you are in a state of blessing: let go of the desire to be seen, and recognize obscurity as a tremendous gift.
