Title: What Remains for a Person in His Hardship?
Excerpt: So I wanted to pass it on, with a comment on it:

So I wanted to pass it on, with a comment on it:

Dr. Muhammad Hamza memorized the Quran and was then put into prisons, where they were forbidden to speak. He says he memorized and taught the prisoners the Quran by writing the page with soap on black clothing, so that the white soap foam would show clearly against the black, and not on colored clothing; and that the writer and television screenwriter Adnan al-Zira’i was with him in the cell and memorized ten juz’ of the Book of Allah this way, until he was martyred.

Then he told that in the last years of torture his nerves had been worn out and he was close to death and illness, so his mental review of the Quran from memory began to take time: al-Baqarah, which used to take him half an hour, began to take him four or five hours, and he felt weakness and frailty in his memory. Yet despite this, he says he insisted on working his memory and reviewing his daily portion, even if it took time and effort.

And when he reached the saying of the Exalted ﴿And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits, but give good tidings to the patient, (155) Who, when disaster strikes them, say, “Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return.” (156) Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is those who are the [rightly] guided. (157) [Al-Baqarah: 155-157]

the jailer came to him, called his name, and gave him the good news of release, and he left the prison after six lean years spent in it.

The amazing thing is that this surgeon used to treat patients and perform surgeries, and he is also a teacher of the Quran who has memorized it.

I don’t know what to say as I listen to his story! Except that I understood one of the meanings of life and of existence within it, which is that when a person is cut off from everything, besieged by time, and fought from every side, denied his food, his drink, his speech, and his people, what remains with him!? Nothing but sorrows, and a memory busy recalling pains, except for the one who has memorized the Quran. Because he reviews his daily portion of the Quran by heart, he crowds out, with the words of the Quran, the words of hatred, insult, and abuse that come his way, so he does not feel pain and does not care much about what is directed at him. That is why he easily forgets painful memories, and his thinking and mind are absorbed in recalling the Quran. And memory, if you do not exhaust it with memorizing and reviewing, will exhaust you with memorizing sorrows and fears.

And from here I came to know the root of the modern problem (overthinking): it is a memory empty of reviewing the daily portion of the Quran, so it became busy recalling the past and the future, and ruminating. Indeed, I came across the stories of many who, after memorizing the Quran and keeping up with it in review and recitation, mentioned an astonishing fact: the whisperings, thoughts, and negative feelings disappeared completely!

Then I knew what remains for a person in his hardship: his chest remains with him, and the verses and recitations he has entrusted to it, which heal him, treat him, and bless his life for him. So what remains for one who has not yet memorized it! And what is he waiting for before he memorizes it!?

As for those who argue (understanding comes before memorizing), I say: there are mathematical laws you do not understand unless you repeat them and memorize them, and with memorizing you understand them; and so it is with the Quran.

And I remember the woman who published a post on Facebook mentioning that she had resolved to memorize the Quran, and her friend said to her: Why are you memorizing it? Reflection is more important than memorization. She says: And now the years have passed, and I have neither memorized it nor reflected on it.

So, my friend, no companion stays with you in hardship except it; it is the spring and it is the rain, so memorize it, and you will find companionship and happiness in it. I ask Allah the Exalted to make the Quran the spring of our hearts, and to make memorizing it easy for me and for you, in pardon and well-being, peace and safety.

From the book (Ya Bushra Hadha Ghulam)
