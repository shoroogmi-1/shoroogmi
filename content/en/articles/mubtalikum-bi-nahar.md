Title: (Indeed, Allah will be testing you with a river)
Excerpt: (He said, "Indeed, Allah will be testing you with a river") and they split into three groups:

(He said, "Indeed, Allah will be testing you with a river") and they split into three groups:

The disobedient: they scooped up more than one handful.

Those who believed: they scooped up a single handful.

The God-fearing: they did not drink at all.

Disobedience screened the first group from even setting their hearts on obedience (obeying the king placed over them, and fighting alongside him in the cause of Allah); it sapped their resolve and drained their aspiration. And so this world teaches us that loving it is the lowest share of anything there is: whoever's aspiration stops at this point, you will find his strength feeble and his spirit defeated; he never learned that there are higher things, the highest of them al-Firdaws al-A'la, and above it the Throne of the Most Merciful.

This is well known even in psychology: whoever sets himself a low goal comes to believe his abilities allow him no more than that, and walks away with poor, feeble results — what they call low competence.

But whoever makes the Hereafter his concern — you will find his aspiration soaring high, like one who has stretched his abilities to their limit and given his horse free rein; he will not settle for the lesser and the lowly. He wants the highest in everything: in worship, in knowledge, in his specialty, in serving his country,,.

And people will differ in their ranks in the Hereafter as their aspirations differed in seeking knowledge in this world; so look well at what you choose, and see clearly.

The second group: those who believed with him; they scooped up a handful, taking what was permissible, yet the abundance of permissible things and distractions weighed them down from marching and fighting. The intention to fight did stir in them; they marched with him a little, and then they stopped (Then when he had crossed it along with those who believed with him, they said, "There is no power for us today against Goliath and his soldiers.") Patience whets the will for the long road, and he meant to keep them from the river not to punish them, but to test them and to train them in endurance.

The third group: those who feared Allah and were patient -and can there be God-fearing without patience?- through thirst and fasting, through renunciation and scrupulousness, through drought and deprivation and want, through *al-ba'sā'*: poverty, and *al-ḍarrā'*: sickness. These are the ones who marched to the very end, and fought on with valor, with skill, and with humble pleading to Allah (they said, "Our Lord, pour upon us patience and plant firmly our feet and give us victory over the disbelieving people.")

(But they drank from it, except a [very] few of them) and these few, who did not drink and held back out of scrupulousness, are the ones who said:

(Those who were certain that they would meet Allah said, "How many a small company has overcome a large company by permission of Allah. And Allah is with the patient.")

Then look at His eloquent description of what lay behind their lofty aspiration, their steadfastness and their endurance: (those who were certain that they would meet Allah)

Their aspirations were bound to the Hereafter; (*yaẓunnūn*: *yūqinūn*, they were certain) of meeting Allah, and they chose what is better over what is lower, so they finished the march, and they persevered and were patient (So they defeated them by permission of Allah).

The story tells us that whoever prefers the Hereafter to this world, his aspiration climbs and rises and towers like the mountains; that whoever triumphs over the whims and cravings of the self will endure and triumph in the battles of this world — and whoever has not triumphed here will not triumph there. True kingship is to be king over the kingdom of your own self. And whoever hopes for Allah and for meeting Him sees nothing of this world in front of him but he casts it aside, so nothing of this world will meet him except what he left behind him!, and what is behind him is behind him! Because the meaning is greater, the concern for the Hereafter weighs heavier, and Allah is more to be hoped for than any good or any hope.

&#42; And what is behind him is behind him!: meaning, what he put behind him he will find before him, and this is known in the Quran (Before him is Hell): *warā'ihi* ("behind him") means: before him.
