Islam has a wonderful system of rewards, motivation, encouragement and hope; part of it is the feast, the sharing of food, however little, at every joy that comes to completion or begins. Among these: the wedding feast (*walīmat al-ʿurs*, a firmly established Sunnah); the *ʿaqīqah* at a birth, boy or girl (Sunnah); the *shundukhiyyah*, at the betrothal and the marriage contract (the beginning of a marriage); the *wakīrah*, food for a newly built home; the *naqīʿah*, food for the return of one who was away; and the *mishdākh*, when a reciter completes the Quran.

A feast for funerals and condolences, however, is disliked, except for food made for the family of the deceased alone, so that feasting never becomes bound up with grief. And so, when we look at what Islam legislates about feasts, we find them bound up with joy.

And at every stage where a person moves on and changes for the better

Food is shared, even a little; let it be intended as charity and as thanks to Allah, for thanks draws down more, and as joy in Allah's bounty and blessing. {Say, "In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy - in that let them rejoice"} that one should rejoice that the blessing came from Allah, not from oneself. So do not keep the reward for yourself; make it charity, joy and thanks to Allah, and it will mean so much more. For “Once the Prophet ﷺ distributed dates among his companions and gave each one seven dates” (narrated by al-Bukhari). Nor do I agree with those who say: “Graduation parties, and feasts for studies, for a new job, for a betrothal or a new home, are innovations, novelties alien to our society!”

Anyone who reads the Shari'ah of Islam deeply knows that it encourages such things, for they fall under charity, feeding others and gratitude. And I can only see the one who says this (that they are new and an innovation) as either stingy, or blind to the occasions for charity and for feeding others, a stranger to the occasions for gratitude, or else a gloomy soul who has never valued a blessing as it deserves.

So when someone becomes skilled at something and masters it, let him give something in charity, however small: Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal handed out walnuts to the boys, five apiece, when his son al-Hasan mastered his lessons.

And on completing a stage of learning (even if other stages remain): as Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) and his son did when they slaughtered a camel upon learning Surat al-Baqarah.

And whoever has memorized 114 surahs may, without any blame, hold 114 celebrations! And so it is with every other craft, science and art.
