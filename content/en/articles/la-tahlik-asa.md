Title: “They say, ‘Do not perish of grief; be steadfast’”| Reflections on the Prohibition of Killing Oneself with Grief
Excerpt: The Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful opens Surah Ash-Shu'ara with the most dazzling of openings:

The Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful opens Surah Ash-Shu'ara with the most dazzling of openings:

(Ta, Seen, Meem (1) These are the verses of the clear Book (2) Perhaps you would kill yourself with grief that they will not be believers (3)

He made clear that the disbelievers did not benefit from the Book, so do not be like them by killing yourself with sorrow over their lack of faith, for even grief has no benefit in it. Just as they did not benefit from the shining truth, so too killing yourself with sorrow has no benefit in it. *Bākhiʿ*: one who kills, from *bakhaʿa*: to reach, in slaughtering, the *bikhāʿ*, a vein at the nape of the neck, which is the furthest limit of slaughter; when it is reached, the soul departs. (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

And Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, says: (And do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you (29) (An-Nisa) And perhaps among the forms of killing oneself is weighing it down with gloom and grief until one no longer joins in the joy of life again or returns to what used to make one happy.

So if He has forbidden you from killing it with grief, then it means you must rouse yourself with every permissible delight so that you regain happiness and joy!

There are two paths: either you kill yourself with grief and intense sorrow over the worries of life and concern for people, or you bring joy to yourself and restore to it its lost right to joy, and its missing provision of everything delightful and pleasing.

And since we are still in Surah Ash-Shu'ara (“The Poets”), the poets have unmatched marvels on this meaning, like the one who recited:

O you whom longing is killing with grief

for something the Decrees have turned away from his hands

He calls him with a vague address: O you who kill yourself with grief over something that the Decrees *naḥathu* (turned away) from him.

(Then perhaps you would kill yourself over their traces if they do not believe in this message, out of sorrow) (Al-Kahf)

What is upon you: to do what you are commanded of obligations and to take the means, striving toward the goals; then if the outcomes prove impossible and the efforts fall short of reaching them, and the Decrees turn them away, then there is no need to force yourself to grieve over them

“In this verse and others like it there is a lesson; for the one commanded to call creation to Allah is obliged to convey and to strive by every means that leads to guidance, and to block the paths of misguidance and error, to the utmost of his ability, while relying upon Allah in that; then if they are guided, that is excellent, and if not, let him not grieve or sorrow; for that weakens the soul and destroys one's strength, and there is no benefit in it; rather he should carry on with the action he has been charged with and directed toward, and whatever is beyond that is outside his power” (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

The meaning: there is no point in the intense grief with which you kill yourself, bury your thoughts and creativity alive, and end your life over what has passed and gone. Destroying it with excessive gloom and depriving it of life's joy and pleasures for the sake of this sorrow has no meaning and no benefit, except that it produces more of the very same sorrow! Sorrow produces sorrow, and so on in a continuous circle that never ends.

So forcing yourself to be patient is better for you than forcing yourself to grieve! And pressing it toward the joy of life and moving it to its pleasures (even if it does not want to) is better than its remaining in a sorrow that brings nothing desired and accomplishes nothing sought. Moving forward in joy, such as being busy with a beneficial hobby or beneficial knowledge, work, and conversation with people, opens sails of happiness by which the ships travel, and perhaps they will carry you to an island you never expected.

For joy strengthens the soul and resolve, so it becomes strong enough to work, moves with enthusiasm and energy, and the sails of opportunity open for it

As for sorrow, what does it do?! It produces: nothing. Helplessness and laziness. It is the killing of life and the slaughter of the soul. Dead land, man revives it; and your own soul, you are the one responsible for reviving it, and you are also the one who slaughters it with intense grief.

And Imru' al-Qais says:

My companions halting their mounts there over me ... saying, “Do not perish of grief; bear it with grace”

And it is as if I imagine Jibril (peace be upon him) standing over him! Consoling him not to kill it with worry (Perhaps you would kill yourself..)

“He says: they halted over me, meaning: for my sake, or over my head while I sat by their saddled camels and mounts, saying to me: Do not perish from excessive grief and intense distress, and adorn yourself with patience. The summary of the meaning: they halted their mounts over him, commanding him to be patient and forbidding him from distress.” (Sharh al-Zawzani)

And look at his use of the word *tajammal*! It comes from bearing up, and also from forcing oneself into beauty and everything that delights the soul and brings happiness into it: that he force himself to this even if he does not want to open his closed-off soul, even if it sees nothing but its own closing.

And ordinary people say: “*Tajammal*, there's nothing left!” That is when the effort is exhausted, the mind is pushed to toil, and fatigue and weariness climb over the walls; they say it as encouragement: You have reached your goal! You've arrived at it, so why have you turned back? Won't you bear up for the last step? Do anything until you reach it!

Put on a brave face, be patient, bear it, anything! Just get it done!

And the English have something similar in a beautiful expression; they say:

“Burn the midnight oil”

Meaning: if your work is nearly finished, stay up over it late into the night and complete it fully, even at the cost of sleeplessness and fatigue.

And our fellow says: Lift the curtain of night and undo its braid, and do not rest except on the tops of the high peaks”

Tarafa ibn al-Abd says:

My companions halting their mounts there over me ... saying, “Do not perish of grief; be steadfast”

“*Tajallud*: forcing oneself to be hardy, which is forcing oneself to be patient.” (al-Zawzani)

And it appears that the poets use (do not perish) at the parting of loved ones, while the Quran is different: it uses (*bākhiʿ*) and begins it with giving a reason, and it guards the soul of the Prophet ﷺ from being demeaned, so it does not direct perishing at his person as the poets do (so do not perish of grief); rather it guards his noble soul ﷺ and uses the style of giving a reason instead of a direct prohibition (perhaps you), and it uses *bākhiʿ*, an active participle, which is the one to whom the act of *bakhʿ* is attributed: killing oneself with grief. And it uses the active participle to hasten the good news and remove the illusion!

Yes, because intense sorrow comes from illusion, not reality; because the extra sorrow in it does not match reality and what happened. Intense grief contains an exaggerated and excessive interpretation of what occurred; it is an extremist interpretation of events!

So let us fight extremism in thought and feeling!

As for hastening the good news, it is like saying: Blessing has arrived, the companion has arrived. And here He brought out the active participle (*bākhiʿ*) to forbid the soul and keep it far from harmful sorrow, to expose its reality, and to remove Iblis's deception in saddening the believer's heart with what is not real, and which has no benefit in it, rather it is very harmful, except that Allah permits (Secret talk is only from Satan, so that he may grieve those who believe, yet he cannot harm them in the least except by Allah's leave; and in Allah let the believers put their trust (10) (Al-Mujadila)

Iblis: derived from *iblās*, which is intense despair; because he despaired of Allah's mercy forever, and we have been forbidden (and the knowledge is with You) from resembling and following him. So keep yourself far from intense worry, for it is Iblis's step toward other steps.

And know that Satan's ways of saddening the believers are many, because it is a goal for Satan to bring grief into the believer so that it weakens his strength for worship and his motivation to give good, and to achieve this goal he uses any means

And it is not limited to two people whispering together; al-Mawardi said in explaining the meaning of secret talk from Satan: “that it is the dreams a person sees in his sleep” “And whoever brings grief into the believers has a resemblance to Satan (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

So the believer follows the example of the Quran in its being good news for the believers: the believer brings happiness into the hearts of the believers and spreads good omens, and whoever resembles Satan spreads gloom and brings grief into people's hearts.

“He, glorified is He, likened that to a man whose loved ones and dear ones have left him, so he is falling apart with regrets over their traces, and killing himself with longing for them and yearning over their parting; and He brought this unique image to guard His comparison from being demeaned; for Allah the Exalted wanted to console His Prophet ﷺ and to lighten for him the worry and pain that had befallen him, so He presented the situation in the form of hope, ‘then perhaps you’, even though what is meant by it is prohibition, i.e.: do not kill yourself and do not destroy it because of your sorrow over their lack of faith” (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

Because killing oneself with intense sorrow weakens it, and if the soul weakens it becomes of no benefit: it neither worships Allah with contentment and happiness, but rather with reluctance and pain, nor does it benefit itself and people! “And al-Kisa'i said: *bakhaʿtu al-arḍ* with farming, if you made it weak through continuous plowing, and a man *bakhaʿa* himself if he wore it out; and on this basis is the meaning of: ﴿*bākhiʿun nafsaka*﴾, i.e. wearing it out and straining it until you destroy it” al-Razi.

For intense grief (may the Most Kind, the Most Merciful watch over you) weakens your soul even from worship, dampens resolve, and stands between it and gaining good deeds and rising in ranks.

Over their traces:

*Āthār* (traces) is used for the past, and these two poets stood over the ruins of the past, weeping.

And it is well known among ordinary people: someone who died *ʿalā athar* (on the traces of) a loved one! Meaning that when the first person died, his traces and signs remained: his clothes and his perfume, so the other stood grieving for a time over those traces until he followed him in death, and the latter perished!

“It is said: so-and-so died *ʿalā athar* so-and-so, i.e. after him. And the origin of this is that when a person dies, his signs and traces remain after his death for a while, then they fade away and he is erased entirely; so if his death is close to the death of the first, his death has occurred while the traces of the first still remain, so it is correct to say: so-and-so died *ʿalā athar* so-and-so.” (al-Razi)

The Quran urges you to leave a trace, instead of weeping over a trace!

Because in the first is your life, and in the second your death. Have you not heard what the Lord of Might said?!

(It is We who give life to the dead, and We write down what they sent ahead and their traces; and everything We have recorded in a clear record (12) (Ya-Sin)

Their traces: We record what they did and what they established of a good or bad practice in their lives, and after their lives people followed it, so it is either a reward for them or a burden upon them.

So always give yourself (may the Most Merciful watch over you) this choice in the face of every killing grief:

1- Either I waste my time weeping over a dead trace (of no benefit at all)

2- Or I make the most of my life by building up the earth and filling it with what is beneficial for myself and others, and leave good traces after death that return to me as rivers of reward and the most abundant good deeds, so I am made happy by them.

So choose for yourself; you are free now. I entrust you to Allah.
