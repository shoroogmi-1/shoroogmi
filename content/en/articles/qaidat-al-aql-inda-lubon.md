Title: The Rule of the Human Mind According to Gustave Le Bon
Excerpt: For Gustave Le Bon, the rule of the human mind is to lead it from the composite to the abstract.

For Gustave Le Bon, the rule of the human mind is to lead it from the composite to the abstract.

And so, in his view, a child's upbringing must rest on what can be touched and seen, not on the abstract.

My objection: this is simply not so!

For a child, by his very nature, can grasp the most abstract of ideas, just as he believes in Allah by instinct. And even if he does not understand them now, hearing them again and again until he comes of age makes them easy for him to understand and to call to mind.

Teach him the rules of grammar, and he may not understand them as a child; but at least the abstract notions will have passed before him: subject and object, declinable and indeclinable... Then, when he learns them again as a grown man, he grasps them clearly and easily, and imagines that age is what made the difference. True, in one sense; yet in another, it is the repetition, from childhood into adulthood, that made it.

"O boy! I will teach you a statement: Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you. Be mindful of Allah and you will find Him before you. When you ask, ask Allah, and when you seek aid, seek Allah's aid..."

Dr. Said al-Kamali commented on this hadith: "What would a child know of such vast meanings? We do not suppose that Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), as a boy, understood them. But this is no reason not to teach a child great ideas; he will understand them later, when he comes of age."

What is more, if we built children's education on Le Bon's method, it would be a disaster! For, quietly and unseen, it raises the child on what is experimental and tangible alone, and a child raised this way will never be able to plant faith and values deep in his spirit and soul.

The child must be taught what is abstract, but simply and gently, through similes and metaphors wherever we can.
