Leave them to do what they do: the blocking, the rebuffing, the scheming, the shutting out — all by the will of Allah! And none can lift it but Him!

![The Let Them Theory by Mel Robbins](the-let-them-theory.jpg)

Because every attempt to correct them, change them, convince them, win them over, falls under "the desire to control" — and the only thing you can ever control is yourself.

That is why there is nothing to be gained from fretting over them!

{ And thus We have made for every prophet an enemy — devils from mankind and jinn, inspiring to one another decorative speech in delusion. But if your Lord had willed, they would not have done it, so leave them and that which they invent } [Surat al-An'am: 112]

Had your Lord willed, they would have been on your side, and dear to you! But He willed, in His wisdom, that they be your enemies. So can you change a thing Allah has written for you? No.

So the best way to handle it: leave them to lie! Let them scheme! And let them mock you.

Leave them! Because letting go is not leaving your life in chaos; it is freeing yourself to put your own life in order, instead of being consumed by that terrible, overwhelming chaos the hotheads leave in their wake — a chaos you can never, however hard you try, tidy, make sense of, or set right again!

"Letting go" does not simply mean letting things run their course; it is a conscious choice to free yourself from this primal urge to control" (Mel Robbins, p. 55)

So letting go does not mean you are weak. That comes from the illusion of being in control {You are not over them a controller }

[Surat al-Ghashiyah: 22]

Because feeling weak and helpless is a negative feeling about something you cannot fix or handle; what it really means is that you leave others as they are! (Mel Robbins)

Leave them! They are the ones who chose loss, and they are the ones who chose to go astray.

So leave them; let the hopes they live in keep them distracted, and let their numbered days dress up what they are in, until they see it as everything, the greatest thing of all!

Leave them! You are not answerable for them, and you will not be called to account for them.

Leave them, for they will be repaid for their evil deeds in this world before the Hereafter; it is only a matter of time, so be patient and let them be. { Let them eat and enjoy themselves and be diverted by [false] hope, for they are going to know } [Surat al-Hijr: 3]

Leave them to delight in their little; ahead of them lies much to keep them busy..

All they are in is a slow drawing-on toward ruin, but their sensors are switched off, so they take it for blessing and breakthrough, for provision and victory. Leave them in their arrogance, swelling until it becomes tyranny, until they rebel, and come to misery.

(But the people divided their religion among them into sects — each faction, in what it has, rejoicing (53) So leave them in their confusion for a time (54) Do they think that what We extend to them of wealth and children (55) Is [because] We hasten for them good things? Rather, they do not perceive (56). (Al-Mu'minun)

Leave them to think their tyranny is judgment, and their ignorance knowledge (their confusion, *ghamratihim*: their misguidance and their ignorance).

I left them to themselves — but until when?!

Until the Day of Resurrection!

{ So leave them to converse vainly and amuse themselves until they meet their Day which they are promised } [Surat az-Zukhruf: 83]

So turn away from them entirely, and busy yourself with the worship of your Lord, with your work, your learning, your life; they are not worth the waste of your time, your thoughts, or your feelings.

Your deferred revenge:

And what is a deferred victory but a deferred revenge on those who set themselves against you?! { So leave them until they meet their Day in which they will be struck insensible } [Surat at-Tur: 45]

{ And whoever is patient and forgives — indeed, that is of the matters [requiring] determination }

[Surat ash-Shura: 43]

Forgiveness makes sense here, since the verses speak of the wronged taking back their right — but why patience?

Because pardon takes a great deal of patience: swallowing your anger, reining your impulses back, even while you have it in your power to take revenge and press on toward justice!

Letting go is a choice and a decision, because you have the very same power, the very same choice, to punish!

They are two options, equally within your power and reach (pardon = justice)

"Someone once said to me: it's as if you are "allowing it," without actually allowing it." (Mel Robbins)

Think how many times you have plotted punishment: I'll do this, I swear, and I'll say that.

Then,

you do,

nothing.

How much time have you burned thinking, and how many nerves have you frayed! And in the end you choose to let it go, because you know it is the wisest course, the most mature, the most complete, the surest shield against childish slips like flaring up in anger — which hardly suit someone as grown-up as you !

Tell me: when was the last time you lost your temper, frayed every nerve, and it actually worked?! Rarely, very rarely.

Do you remember the Facebook message you fired off, boiling over with rage? And the voice note where you went through the roof?

Tell me, honestly, by God: didn't you regret it, even a little!

Admit it — admitting feels good, and it helps us make conscious decisions, and better ones in the future.

For the grudge before you let go, and the anger after it, are of no use at all: a waste of time, and a squandering of the standing you hold.
