Title: Surat an-Nur Shows Us How to Reach the Light
Excerpt: To have the light of Allah reach the heart of His believing servant, you do not need endless prayer and fasting; it is simpler than that. These three are enough for you:

To have the light of Allah reach the heart of His believing servant, you do not need endless prayer and fasting; it is simpler than that. These three are enough for you:

1- Purifying your hearing

2- Purifying your tongue

3- Purifying your sight

And the light will come, if Allah wills!

The surah opens with just two verses on the familiar indecency of the deed (verses 2-3), and twenty-three on the many kinds of indecency of the tongue! (4-26)

As if to tell us that the indecency of the tongue is far graver than we imagine: we make light of it, but Allah says it is tremendous!

(When you received it with your tongues and said with your mouths that of which you had no knowledge and thought it was insignificant while it was, in the sight of Allah, tremendous (15) [An-Nur]

The kinds of indecency of the tongue in the surah:

1- Accusing chaste, unsuspecting women, and likewise chaste, unsuspecting believing men, with no proof and no witnesses.

2- Spouses accusing one another with no proof and no witnesses.

3- The lying slander, the fabrications against the honor of people who are wholly innocent.

4- Thinking ill of believing men and women and casting doubt on their honor without evidence or witness, out of nothing but whim; and this Allah the Exalted called a manifest slander.

5- Wading into people's honor even when there is proof and evidence and the thing truly happened. Are you a judge? Are you a witness holding evidence? The answer is no! Then why wade into their honor, even if it happened and the news is true? What business is it of yours? Look to your own soul and weep over your own sin. We have known people who waded into the honor of others, gloating and mocking, only to fall into the very thing they had fallen into. So take heed, and beware: this is not the character of one who comes to his Lord with a sound heart.

"By Allah, O Hazzal, had you covered him with your garment, it would have been better than what you did to him" Narrated by Imam Ahmad

Imam Malik said:

"In this town (meaning Madinah) I knew people who had no faults; they found fault with others, and so came to have faults. And in this town I knew people who had faults; they kept silent about the faults of others, and so their own faults were forgotten"

So cover the faults of Muslim men and women, and Allah will cover yours in this world and the Hereafter.

The cure lies in what the verse says (And why, when you heard it, did you not say, "It is not for us to speak of this. Exalted are You, [O Allah]; this is a great slander."?) [An-Nur]

Its meaning: why, when you heard such reports, did you not hold your peace and keep out of them, and say: Glory be to You! This is false talk; it is not for us to let our tongues speak of it, or our suspicions turn it over, or to mention it to anyone. Glory be to You, our Lord; we declare You far above this monstrous lie!

Ibn Kathir said: (This is a second lesson in manners after the first, which was the command to think well of others: that is, when something unbecoming is said about the best of people, then all the more should one think well of them, and not let oneself feel anything else; and if something of it should still cling to the soul (a whisper or a passing fancy), one should not speak of it).

So the verses teach us to purify our hearing from listening to such rumors and reports (even when they are true): not to browse them, not to comment on them, but to pass them by with dignity, for this is a trait of the servants of the Most Merciful (and when they pass near ill speech, they pass by with dignity); and to purify the tongue and our suspicions, speaking of believing men and women only with good, and thinking well of them.

6- Swearing too much! A person should not swear oaths often, and should rid himself of idle oaths as far as he can; it is disliked, and some even counted it a mark of hypocrisy. My father once told us of a friend of his who had gone many years without swearing by Allah even once! I said: now that is true honor!

(And let not those of virtue among you and wealth swear..) (An-Nur)

*Lā yaʾtali*: let him not swear.

7- Pardoning and overlooking whoever has assailed others' honor, if he repents, turns back, and does not persist in what he said (And let not those of virtue among you and wealth swear not to give [aid] to their relatives and the needy and the emigrants for the cause of Allah, and let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you? And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful (22)

The outcome:

(Evil words are for evil men, and evil men are [subjected] to evil words. And good words are for good men, and good men are [an object] of good words. Those [good people] are declared innocent of what the slanderers say. For them is forgiveness and noble provision.) [An-Nur]

The more a person cleanses himself of foul speech and purifies his hearing and his tongue, the purer and more chaste he becomes, and the more worthy of chastity and goodness (The ones whom the angels take in death, [being] good and pure). They kept their speech and their hearing clean of filth and of trespassing on people's honor, so Allah made their words good and sweet at the hour of death; these are the ones who truly deserve to be called good. And it is this goodness that makes him fit for the light to enter the depths of his heart and his innermost being, even if he never toiled at endless prayer and fasting

“They said: O Messenger of Allah, so-and-so fasts by day and prays by night, but she harms her neighbors? He said: She is in the Fire. They said: O Messenger of Allah, so-and-so prays only the obligatory prayers and gives in charity pieces of dried curd, but she does not harm her neighbors? He said: She is in Paradise” - Sahih

And this shows that whoever thinks ill of people, wades into their honor, listens to such talk, believes it and repeats it, is foul, be it a foul man or a foul woman.

"This is proof that Allah, Glorified and Exalted, clears the family of a good and chaste man of foulness; for good women are for good men and good men for good women. And this is part of the wisdom of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: the more good, clean and pure a person is, the more Allah, Glorified and Exalted, prepares for him a family of the same standing, a fitting recompense; and the reverse holds if he is foul, especially in matters of chastity. And this is what we usually see in reality: a man of foul character has a family like him, because he did not guard himself, so Allah, Mighty and Majestic, did not guard him"

(al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

"Evil words are for evil men, and evil men are for evil words; and good words are for good men, and good men are for good words. That is: the evil words the hypocrites attributed to Aisha are more fitting for them, and she is more fitting for innocence and purity than they are; and so the Exalted said: Those [good people] are declared innocent of what the slanderers say [An-Nur: 26]," (Ibn Kathir).

And part of guarding the hearing, the tongue and the sight is that He laid down the etiquette of asking permission before entering, so that a person's eye does not fall on what he would hate to see, of his own family or of others.

Allah the Exalted does not only forbid the act itself; He forbids even drawing near to it (But do not approach this tree) (And do not approach the orphan's property..) (which is abrogated), (And do not approach unlawful sexual intercourse..).

(But do not approach this tree): He stressed the prohibition and made it all the stronger by first forbidding them to go near it, before forbidding them the act itself (the eating).

For merely drawing near, seeing and hearing, is enough to make the sin look lovely in the heart and make a person fond of it.

He forbade drawing near, and forbade every path that leads to sin; a person wrongs himself simply by drawing near it, by nothing more than drawing near

"Nearness to a thing breeds an urge and an inclination that seize the whole heart; this is what familiarity brings, and familiarity invites love, so that one sees nothing ugly and hears no prohibition, and it distracts him from what revelation and reason require. For He made their approaching the tree a cause of their being among the wrongdoers; and a cause that invites to evil is forbidden, just as a cause that leads to good is commanded" (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

3- Purifying the sight: He commanded believing men and women to lower their gaze, for this is a means of attaining goodness and chastity.

And He gave believing women etiquettes that help keep the gaze, the speech and the hearing pure. As for hearing: "let them not stamp their feet to make known what they conceal of their adornment", that is, a woman striking the anklet on her foot so that a man will hear it. And He commanded her to wear the hijab, which keeps the gaze pure.

And He commanded whoever cannot find the means to marry to stay chaste and pure until Allah enriches him and provides for him. He must take up the means of chastity and strive in them: fasting often, lowering the gaze and all that was mentioned before, and filling his time with knowledge and useful pursuits instead of dwelling on this matter, not letting it occupy his mind too much. Haven't you heard that Imam al-Nawawi was asked: When will you marry? And he said: I forgot all about it! Meaning, it was simply not on his mind, so absorbed was he in knowledge. And Ibn Kathir said about the words of the Exalted (But let them who find not [the means for] marriage abstain [from sexual relations] until Allah enriches them from His bounty)

this is the man who has no wife: "let him gaze upon the dominion of the heavens and the earth until Allah enriches him."!

So when he has done all this, purified his hearing, his sight and his tongue, and kept chaste, his heart is cleansed and becomes more fit for the light of Allah to enter it. And what is the light of Allah? It is the Quran!

(And We have certainly sent down to you distinct verses and examples from those who passed on before you and an admonition for those who fear Allah (34) Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The example of His light is like a niche within which is a lamp, the lamp is within glass, the glass as if it were a pearly [white] star lit from [the oil of] a blessed olive tree, neither of the east nor of the west, whose oil would almost glow even if untouched by fire. Light upon light. Allah guides to His light whom He wills. And Allah presents examples for the people, and Allah is Knowing of all things (35))

Admonition: that which holds one back from sin.

Emptying comes before adorning: once a person has emptied himself, purified and cleansed himself of the causes of indecency in word and deed, and kept away from them, he is the most worthy of the light of Allah and of holding fast to the Quran.

And once the light has settled in his heart, he spreads light wherever he goes: ([Such niches are] in mosques which Allah has ordered to be raised and that His name be mentioned therein; exalting Him within them in the morning and the evenings [are] men whom neither commerce nor sale distracts from the remembrance of Allah and performance of prayer and giving of zakah. They fear a Day in which the hearts and eyes will [fearfully] turn about) (An-Nur)

The verses show the fruit of this light: the believer now prefers the Hereafter to this world, his heart grows tender, humble and lowly before Allah the Exalted, and fear, awe and hope come to dwell in it. These lofty qualities are reached in no other way! Purity of hearing, sight and tongue, then the light of Allah, the Quran, until he reaches the fruits of the light and spreads it among people (And is one who was dead and We gave him life and made for him light by which to walk among the people). This light is the Quran: it lit up his heart, and then it shone on those around him.

So the believer's light is inward, real and deep-rooted, rising from his heart and then lighting up all around him; while the hypocrite's light is outward, with no root in the heart, as the verses of Surat al-Baqarah show (Their example is that of one who kindled a fire, but when it illuminated what was around him). The hypocrite borrowed from another to kindle his light, and it lit up the outside, not his heart, as al-Tafsir al-Muharrar explains.

Then He describes the state of the one who is its very opposite: (Or [they are] like darknesses within an unfathomable sea which is covered by waves, upon which are waves, over which are clouds - darknesses, some of them upon others. When one puts out his hand [therein], he can hardly see it. And he to whom Allah has not granted light - for him there is no light (40) (An-Nur)

Whoever is foul in speech, in hearing and in sight, the very opposite of those qualities, lives in darkness of heart upon darkness of place, spreading foul words. How would Allah grant light to one who never took up the means of light!

Whoever reflects on Surat an-Nur will find it full of words kin to light!

Flash, lightning, sight (The flash of its lightening almost takes away the eyesight. Allah alternates the night and the day. Indeed in that is a lesson for those who have vision). Allah is able to bring the servant out of darkness into light, and the reverse, according to whether he takes up or abandons the means of light. And there are means of steadfastness the servant must strive for, so that his heart is not turned about as the night and the day are turned about: “A man would be a believer in the morning and an unbeliever in the evening, or he would be a believer in the evening and an unbeliever in the morning, and would sell his faith for a paltry worldly gain”

Then He crowns all this with obedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ and contentment with their rulings, while the hypocrite is the very opposite; and whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger and lives in awe of Him has won the prize (And whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger and fears Allah and is conscious of Him - it is those who are the attainers (52) (An-Nur)

And once he has passed through these stages, Allah, by His leave, establishes him in the land:

(Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [to authority] upon the earth just as He granted it to those before them and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has preferred for them and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security, [for] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that - then those are the defiantly disobedient (55) (An-Nur)

How could he be established in the land when he has not cleansed himself of foul speech and of a roaming gaze? He will be someone others follow, and such traits do not befit him. The cleansing must come first, so that the establishing may come after!

And notice that He calls whoever goes back to the darkness after being established in the land *fāsiq*, defiantly disobedient! “Abusing a Muslim is *fusūq*.”

Purify the inner self through the hearing, the tongue and the sight; then the light settles in the heart and he holds fast to the Quran; then he spreads the light, and stands firm in obedience to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ; and so he is established in the land, if Allah wills.

Whoever reflects on the surah will find it repeating again and again (And Allah is Hearing and Knowing) (And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing), driving home that Allah hears your words; so beware of what you say and what you do, for He hears it all and knows it all, and nothing of it is hidden from Him. And whoever keeps watch over himself before the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing receives a fitting reward: the All-Encompassing, the All-Knowing widens for him his religion and his world, and increases him in light.
