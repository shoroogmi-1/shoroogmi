Title: Praise Be to Allah That I Am Not Famous
Excerpt: Many a fame has bequeathed injustice: one gets into a dispute in which he is wronged or does wrong.

Many a fame has bequeathed injustice: one gets into a dispute in which he is wronged or does wrong.

Or he becomes famous, loved, and his admirers multiply across the earth, and it bequeaths a slip whose scenes are filmed and spread, so he regrets and grieves, and is abandoned the way carrion is abandoned.

And fame built on ignorance, so his actions and words are ignorant, and this, however many millions it makes, fades away before long.

Perhaps fame was written for someone through a scandal -we ask Allah the Exalted for well-being- so praise Him that He has covered us, kept us safe, hidden us from the creation, and concealed our faults from them.

High fame in his youth, numbers and money; then in his mid-thirties he takes a resounding fall, the creatures curse him and the numbers drop, after he had been so-and-so and so-and-so. So it is sealed for him with death before death.

And perhaps his standing stayed high among the creation his whole life; and after death he was exposed and disgraced, so his fame was a curse upon him. So I ask Allah for us and for you a covering at death, after death, and before death, and a sincere repentance before it is too late.

And perhaps a high fame bequeathed regret, grief, bitter disappointment, and a depression in which a person is imprisoned until he says/ I wish I were dust.

These are scenes that tighten the chest; I know that, but so that you know you are in a blessed state, and so that you renounce the love of showing yourself, and see obscurity as a great blessing.
