Title: Grace and Courtesy in Listening to the Quran
Excerpt: She tells me she was lying in a hospital bed, and that the woman beside her turned her Bluetooth speaker up loud with Surah Al-Baqarah and the ruqyah, and that for two, three days she could not sleep because of it.

She tells me she was lying in a hospital bed, and that the woman beside her turned her Bluetooth speaker up loud with Surah Al-Baqarah and the ruqyah, and that for two, three days she could not sleep because of it.

I told her: that woman has no sense of grace! Perhaps she never heard how he ﷺ would lower his voice with the Quran, for fear of waking his family.

And likewise the one who turns the Quran up loud at a traffic light with his window rolled down, forgetting that among people there are those suffering from ear and eye infections and other ailments that sharp, loud sound aggravates, and those with pressing needs, and the elderly.

“It has been narrated to us that the Prophet ﷺ heard a man reciting aloud, and he called out to him: O so-and-so, let Allah hear you, and do not make me hear you” (Qut al-Qulub)

For no one stands more in need of this Quran's admonitions and warnings than you, O listener, O reciter. As for people, they have a Lord who will guide them; what business is it of yours? Let yourself hear, and set the volume to your own measure, not to the measure of all creation.

(And do not recite [too] loudly in your prayer or [too] quietly but seek between that an [intermediate] way (110) (Al-Isra)

Reciting aloud means that your own ears hear you. Reciting silently means that you do not hear yourself at all, a mere murmur of the lips. And it used to be said: whatever your ear hears is not silent recitation. “Whoever lets his own ears hear has not recited silently” (al-Tabari)

“I was told of a good and pious man who said: I was reciting Surah Ta-Ha before dawn, in a room of mine that opened onto the street, and when I finished it I dozed off. I saw a figure descend from the sky, holding a white scroll, and he spread it out before me; and there was Surah Ta-Ha, and beneath every word ten good deeds were recorded, except for a single word: in its place I saw an erasure, and nothing beneath it. That grieved me, and I said: By Allah, I did recite this word, yet I see no reward for it, nor do I see it recorded! The figure said: You are right, you recited it and we wrote it down for you, but we heard a caller cry out: Erase it, and strike out its reward! So we erased it. I wept in my sleep and said: Why did you do that? They said: A man passed by, and you raised your voice with it for his sake, so we erased it”(Qut al-Qulub)

Linguists say that words alone can never express everything in the mind.

Even now, as I talk with you and write, there are so many feelings and thoughts these words of mine cannot carry to you; language is not a pipe that delivers everything.

Here, I paused the podcast and began to ponder his words ﷺ: “When one of you rises at night to pray, let him use the siwak, for when one of you recites in his prayer, an angel places its mouth upon his mouth, and nothing leaves his mouth but it enters the angel's mouth” (its narrators are trustworthy)

Did I perhaps understand, then, why the angel places its mouth upon the reciter's mouth as the Quran is recited? Why not sit at some distance from him, say?! Would that not be enough?

I think, though true knowledge is with Allah, that it is because along with the verses it takes in the reciter's thoughts, feelings, intentions, supplications, and requests! It is not merely a matter of taking in the letters; for if it were only the letters, how would the sincere be told apart from the one who shows off? So it takes in everything from the reciter: his intentions, his thoughts, and his feelings as he recites -and Allah knows best-.

(And [by] the witness and what is witnessed) Among the meanings I reflected on in this verse: the witness and the witnessed may be the Quran itself. It is a witness for you or against you; it is witnessed in this world, the angels and the believers bear witness to it, and witnessed in the Hereafter, on the day the reciter of the Quran is told: Recite, and rise up.

So make it a witness for you, not against you, and do not make it a means of harming people and the sick. Mind public decency and good manners, and let us hold fast to sincerity, for it travels along with our reciting and our listening - and I am the first of those in need of this reminder. O Allah, forgive us, have mercy on us, and grant us sincerity from Yourself-.
