Title: The Sprout: On the Means of Holding Firm to Guidance Until Death —by the Leave of Allah the Exalted—

I know! You live in dread of slipping after you have walked straight; it buzzes in your head like a bee, on and on… When that thought comes, do nothing and do not overthink it; just say: “O Changer of the hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion.”

Leave guidance to its Owner, hand the keys to its Keeper, and what an excellent Keeper He is.

Yes, steadfastness comes from Allah the Exalted, yet the servant must take up its means #strive:

Do not sit with the people of falsehood who make you doubt the truth of faith in Allah and the Last Day. Do not listen to them. Do not say, “It won’t affect me.” Oh, it will: your certainty in the Hereafter slips a notch or two, and then you can barely lift it back up.

So are you ready to lift it again after every single drop!?

I am not ready!

Then do not listen to them.

If you come across lines that cast doubt on the foundations of the faith, do not play the virtuoso and say (I’ll take it on, let me read what it says! Why all these restrictions? I can refute it..)

We have been told of a man who knew whole texts of fiqh by heart and could recite them without pause; he exposed himself to the doubts of the atheists, and they lodged in his heart and would not leave, until he became an atheist, after having been a proud believer in the One God —O Allah, we ask You for safety—.

So here is advice from the heart: do not take them on, so long as this is not your field.

I am no specialist in answering doubts; so why would I expose myself to them!?

Why, when the daylight is plain before him, does he ask like a fool: Does the sun exist or not?

Do not turn your faith into a playground for every passerby to toy with as he pleases, wrecking years of certainty that you built and labored over!

Have you ever seen a man with wealth and standing who, in a single second, tore down everything he had built, until nothing was left! Such is his likeness

(Whoever makes his religion a target for disputes will keep shifting from one thing to another)

A man came to al-Hasan al-Basri and said: O Abu Sa‘id, I want to dispute with you. He said: (Keep away from me; I know my religion. It is only one who doubts his religion who would dispute with you)

And Malik ibn Anas, whenever one of the people of whims came to him, would say: (As for me, I stand on clear proof from my Lord; as for you, you are in doubt, so go to a doubter like yourself and dispute with him) A man from among the people of innovation came to debate him, and Malik said: (They confuse themselves, then go looking for someone to set them straight) (*Al-Aqidah al-Tahawiyyah*, commentary by Prof. Dr. Khalid al-Muslih)

(See an earlier article of ours explaining the words of Allah the Exalted (And recite to them, [O Muhammad], the news of him to whom we gave [knowledge of] Our signs, but he detached himself from them..)

And among the causes of swerving after guidance:

1- Putting reason ahead of revealed knowledge and faith.

2- Personal vanity, and a haughty refusal to accept facts, evidence, and proofs.

3- Drifting back and forth in doubt without taking up the means of certainty. Among what increases certainty: revealed knowledge; learning the deeds and the understanding of the heart, so that faith and devotion to Allah take root; and humility before the truth when it appears.

4- Being dazzled by the adornment of this worldly life. Keep the world in your hand (by taking the means and making the effort required) and not in your heart, lest you cling to it and crave it. This is the finest definition of *zuhd*, and the sign that the heart has renounced the world is that losing something does not grieve you, nor does its arrival make you rejoice; rather, falling short in obedience grieves you, and fulfilling it brings you joy.

And the answer: No, you are not shielded from influence the way the angels are; you are a human being, who moves others and is moved by them.

So do not say: My mind can handle hearing the stories and the news, watching the pictures and the videos, and none of it touches me. Oh, it does; your mind is touched without even knowing it.

Do not say, “I love to confront, not to run away”; sometimes running is the only way a drowning man escapes the water, and we have been commanded to flee from the lion: “one should run away from the leper as one runs away from a lion.” How many a man was undone by his own courage! {And do not kill yourselves}.

Have you not seen that the master of the brave, Muhammad, upon him be blessings and peace, was commanded to keep away from sitting with the people of falsehood, and this though he was protected from error! He did not say, “I am a Prophet; it won’t affect me.” {And when you see those who engage in [offensive] discourse concerning Our verses, then turn away from them until they enter into another conversation}.

Have you not seen that Yusuf, Prophet and Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him), fled from the wife of al-Aziz? {And they both raced to the door}.

We were told of a righteous man who heard the story of someone falling into a sin, and it gripped him so fiercely that he committed the sin himself! And all it was, was a story, something he heard!

{And [it is] so the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter will incline toward it and that they will be satisfied with it and that they will commit that which they are committing} Dr. Ahmad Abdel-Moneim said: This verse lays out the stages of people’s fall into temptation —the steps of being influenced—:

1- Inclining (listening)

2- Being satisfied with what they heard, with no rejection of it in the heart

3- The act and the deed.

I say: the stronger one’s faith in Allah and in the unseen, the stronger the heart stands when it hears temptation, and the firmer it holds against doubt and desire. Yet however high faith may climb, one must still flee and not listen.

Have you not seen that even the heroes and fearless champions of war are commanded to lower their gaze! And is lowering the gaze not a way of running away!

And so the question was: Can a person resist being influenced?
