Title: (And their hearts absorbed [the worship of] the calf)
Excerpt: The love of the calf mingled with the very lining of their hearts, as water seeps into the depths of the body; and this because they denied the truth.

The love of the calf mingled with the very lining of their hearts, as water seeps into the depths of the body; and this because they denied the truth.

*Ushribū* (“they were made to drink”): as though someone else had made them drink the love of the calf, as though it had carried them to a point where they no longer had any choice. Here we learn that clinging to this world, and craving it, robs a person of freedom of spirit and of will: he becomes its slave, until it takes from him the most precious thing he owns, his freedom. The believer, you will find, is free, because he has broken loose from his desires and from his attachment to this world, and so he can say no to the forbidden; while the one without faith, whom the world has mastered, says yes to everything in it, out of his attachment to it and his bondage to doubts and forbidden desires. There is an eloquent image here, too: their hearts soaked up their love of the world the way the earth soaks up water. Their life and their nourishment came from it, so that their deeds and their disobedience became the fruit of the very water they had been made to drink. We hold fast to Allah, and seek refuge in Him from such a state.

It also shows that sin left unrepented leads to love of this world and all it holds, to clinging to it and craving it. (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)
