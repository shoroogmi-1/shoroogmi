Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (may Allah have mercy on him) said that one of the names of the Quran is “*al-Ḥabl*, the Rope: ﴿And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together﴾ [Al Imran: 103]; in the exegesis, it is the Quran. It was given this name because whoever holds fast to it in the affairs of his religion is delivered by it from the punishment of the Hereafter and the chastisements of this world, just as one who clings to a rope is saved from drowning and from perils. For this reason the Prophet ﷺ called it a safeguard, saying: ‘This Quran is a safeguard for whoever holds fast to it,’ for it safeguards people from sins.”

I say: a rope is for pulling, for pushing, for hauling up; it is used to rescue and to relieve. And the Quran pulls you toward Allah, hauls you along, even when a person has no wish for guidance and resists it. Merely holding on to one end of it (even a little reading, a little listening) keeps drawing him up and lifting him out of the depths of the well of sins, darkness, and ignorance, until little by little it raises him to the sky of nearness to Allah and of piety, of complete knowledge, of virtue and high standing, in this life and in the Hereafter.

And this is borne out by the authentic hadith:

“Indeed, I am leaving among you, that which if you hold fast to them, you shall not be misguided after me. One of them is greater than the other: The Book of Allah is a rope extended from the sky to the earth, and my family - the people of my house - and they shall not split until they meet at the Hawd, so look at how you deal with them after me”

And this rope is the means through which one is given victory in this world and the Hereafter. The proof is the word of the Exalted: (Whoever should think that Allah will not support [him] in this world and the Hereafter - let him extend a rope (*sabab*) to the ceiling)

Most exegetes hold that the *sabab* is a rope. And the rope is the Quran: whoever clings to it is given victory in this world and the Hereafter, and whoever cuts it, Allah leaves to go astray.

And reflect on his words “so look at how you deal with them after me (*takhlufūnī*)”: he used the language of succession, of leaving a trust to successors, while the Quran uses the language of inheritance and legacy: (Then we caused to inherit the Book those We have chosen of Our servants).

It is the legacy of prophethood, left in our hands (how you deal with them after me). So look at where you stand with it! And mend it, mend it.
