Title: Whoever Knows Allah, What Has He Failed to Know?
Excerpt: Knowing Allah with the heart is a form of knowledge that leads to faith and certainty.

“Whoever knows Allah, what has he failed to know?
And whoever is ignorant of Allah, what has he truly known?”

To travel the earth, leave behind differing books and conflicting studies, and emerge from your library with a restless mind, seeking knowledge heart to heart—this is a kind of knowing.

“Competition for more distracts you” (Surah At-Takathur). Some scholars said that this can refer to an abundance of books.

“When you hear a narration from me and your hearts recognize it, your skin and hair soften at it, and you feel that it is close to you, then I am the one most entitled to it. But when you hear a narration from me and your hearts reject it, your skin and hair recoil from it, and you feel that it is far from you, then I am the farthest of you from it.”

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ knew that narrations would multiply and knowledge would expand without end. He therefore made knowledge of the heart one of the paths of learning. Faith, too, is a kind of knowledge acknowledged by people of reason.

The strength of knowledge in the heart depends on the strength of faith and its branches, certainty and its shades, reverent fear, humility, submission, and modesty before truth and creation.

Worship and faith are the water the heart drinks; from them emerge knowledge expressed through words, deeds, and states.
