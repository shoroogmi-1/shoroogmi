Title: The Wisdom of Forgetting

(We will make you recite, [O Muhammad], and you will not forget, except what Allah should will): proof that forgetting, and its opposite, remembering, lie within the will of Allah the Exalted and are part of His written decree. It also shows that forgetting is from Allah's decree, so whoever forgets should be content and not lose heart over it.

Forgetting happens for a great wisdom. Part of it is that Allah makes us forget our pain; for if we remembered it all, it would destroy us.

Part of it, too, is that Allah may make us forget a certain piece of knowledge; for if we remembered it and passed it on to someone, it might lead them astray and into temptation rather than guide them!

And the other way around: you may remember a piece of knowledge in front of one person and forget it in front of another, because it will help the one and harm the other. So He reminded you there, and made you forget here.

In the *hasan* hadith: (My Lord, make me forget what You have decreed, and help me with what You have left me). The best way to deal with the past is to forget it, and if you cannot, then ask Allah for it! For none can make us forget but Allah the Exalted.

And the best way to deal with the present is to turn toward what Allah the Exalted has given us.

And Allah has wisdom and knowledge in making him forget what he meant to say.

A person leans on Allah the Exalted in the most delicate workings of the mind, forgetting and remembering, and does not lean on the techniques and mechanisms of memory and the mind's forgetting alone. And part of the wisdom of forgetting is that Allah makes you forget a certain story; for had you told it before a certain gathering of people, you would have opened upon yourself a door of evil and strife that you knew nothing of, and that Allah knew. That is why He made you forget it.

The wisdom of remembering: I asked Allah to make me forget the name of a poem so that I would stop searching for it, yet it kept echoing in my mind, and I wondered why. And the answer came: a test, a trial.

And one should still take the means of remembering, such as digital tools, notes, and calendars, rather than trusting frail human memory, especially where people's rights and promises are concerned: "When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ feared he would forget something he needed, he would tie a thread around his little finger, or around his ring." Even though some have graded this hadith weak, its meaning is sound

"Abdullah al-Lahji said: Forgetting, as some of the gnostics have said, belongs to the perfection of true knowing; for Allah the Exalted declared Himself free of it, and made it part of the very reality of the servant."
