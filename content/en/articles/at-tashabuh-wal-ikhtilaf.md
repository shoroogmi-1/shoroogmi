Title: Similarity and Difference
Excerpt: Similarity wraps us in a sense of familiarity, though in the clever it stirs a faint suspicion; difference, on the other hand, rings far more true.

Similarity wraps us in a sense of familiarity, though in the clever it stirs a faint suspicion; difference, on the other hand, rings far more true.

What I mean is this: the disturbed person is the one who tries to match you in everything. You went through such-and-such? So did I. You live in such-and-such a country? Me too. You love coffee juice? Just like me.

I don't deny that real similarities exist. My point is that, with time, the disturbed person's contradictions come to light: you find out he doesn't read at all, isn't from that country, and has failed the vocabulary test (coffee juice).

And that is why clever people grow wary of similarities.

Difference, though, rings truer. When you disagree with someone, there may be arguments and quarrels between you, even a brief chill, and yet you feel you are talking to a real human being.

We admire the way someone reacts differently from us, and the more we admire, the more we follow their example. We imitate those who differ from us; in those who resemble us we find nothing to imitate, let alone to admire.
