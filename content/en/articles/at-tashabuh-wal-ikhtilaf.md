Title: Similarity and Difference
Excerpt: Similarity gives us a feeling of familiarity, except for intelligent people, in whom it stirs a little suspicion, while difference gives us higher credibility.

Similarity gives us a feeling of familiarity, except for intelligent people, in whom it stirs a little suspicion, while difference gives us higher credibility.

And what I mean is that the psychologically disturbed person is the one who tries to resemble you in everything: you had such-and-such an experience? Me too. You live in such-and-such a country? Me too. You like coffee juice? Same as you.

I don't deny the possibility of similarity, but what I mean is that the disturbed person's contradictions are exposed with time: you discover that he doesn't read at all, is not from such-and-such a country, and failed the vocabulary test (coffee juice).

Which makes intelligent people feel more suspicious toward similarities.

As for difference, its credibility is higher. When you disagree with someone in opinion, as much as arguments and quarrels happen between you and a little aversion for a temporary period, you still feel that you are talking to a real human being.

We admire someone's difference from us in reactions, and in proportion to the admiration, emulation and following grow. We imitate those who differ from us, and we find nothing in those who resemble us to imitate, let alone admire.
