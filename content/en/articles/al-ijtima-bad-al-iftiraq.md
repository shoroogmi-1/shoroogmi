Even the sky dressed itself in the colors of the rainbow and wore the hours of the full moon, and the clouds turned and fled

Look: Yusuf meets his father; he goes out to receive the caravan. Before that he was reunited with his brothers, and before that with his brother.

A bitter parting, a family torn apart, every line of contact cut: that is the first episode of life. They threw him into the depths of the well; then came bitter episodes and tearful nights (And they came to their father at night, weeping). The soul grew weary and gave out, the days and nights turned their backs and fled, the caravans headed home. What is left to wait for!

Wait! The episode isn't over yet,

Then, little by little, the family is gathered back together. He is reunited with his brother Benjamin (he took his brother to himself; he said, “Indeed, I am your brother, so do not despair over what they used to do [to me]”).

And this was a signal to Yusuf: if he could be reunited with one close to him, then his greatest love, his father, he would meet too, but only in the final minutes, when the blessing is complete and old age has devoured the morsel.

Then he is reunited with his brothers: (He said, “No blame will there be upon you. Today Allah will forgive you; and He is the most merciful of the merciful”), following the recitation of Warsh from Nafi', where the pause falls at *ʿalaykum*, “upon you.” So the meaning becomes: the day they are reunited is the day of pardon, of generosity, of sins forgiven.

Then he meets his greatest love: his father (he took his parents to himself)

A small homecoming heralds the greater Eid al-Adha

(And when they entered upon Joseph, he took his brother to himself; he said, “Indeed, I am your brother, so do not despair over what they used to do [to me]”)

(And when they entered upon Joseph, he took his parents to himself and said, “Enter Egypt, Allah willing, safe [and secure]”)

## [Scene Two: Silent, No Script]

She casts him into the river, a nursing infant.. Which will he meet first? The darkness of the pitch-black night, or the darkness of the deadly sea, or the cruelty of a fate that carried him far from his mother's tender breast?

On the shore of safety, he meets his enemy.

Safety and tenderness, both gone.

She wept; her heart cracked and trembled in terror at losing him (And the heart of Moses' mother became empty [of all else]. She was about to disclose [the matter concerning] him had We not bound fast her heart that she would be of the believers)

There is a good promise here, the kind that promises reunion after parting, a true promise, beyond all doubt

And like Yusuf, the infant was first reunited with his sister (And she said to his sister, “Follow him”), as if to say: just as I could bring you someone close to you (when your sister went and said, “Shall I direct you to someone who will be responsible for him?”), so I am able to bring you the dearest heart of all, the closest and nearest to you (So We restored him to his mother that she might be content and not grieve and that she would know that the promise of Allah is true. But most of the people do not know). The very words of the mother are the words of the father! (And when the bearer of good tidings arrived, he cast it over his face, and he returned [once again] seeing. He said, “Did I not tell you that I know from Allah that which you do not know?”)

What did the old man Yaqub, peace be upon him, know? And what did the mother know?

They knew that Allah's promise of reunion is true, even if it comes only in the Gardens, when lifespans and hopes have run out; true, beyond all doubt (Indeed, you are to die, and indeed, they are to die. Then indeed you, on the Day of Resurrection, before your Lord, will dispute)

It may be a reckoning over wrongs, or the reproaches of loved ones.

## [Scene Three / The Hardest Plot]

He falls ill until he is at death's door

And all his loved ones and children are parted from him,

Which will he meet first: the illness that has imprisoned his body, or the loss of his own flesh and blood!

He suffers until it towers over him (*samāhu*) and he gives it a name (*sammāhu*): (adversity) (And [mention] Job, when he called to his Lord, “Indeed, adversity has touched me”)

And it was adversity because everyone had strayed away from him, and he was left alone with his first Beloved: my Lord and his Lord, Allah (And when adversity touches you, lost are [all] those you invoke except for Him)

Then help came, and reunion, and a whole army of family (So We responded to him and removed what afflicted him of adversity. And We gave him [back] his family and the like thereof with them as mercy from Us and a reminder for the worshippers [of Allah]) (And We granted him his family and a like [number] as mercy from Us and a reminder for those of understanding)

A reminder for the people of reason!

And a reminder for the worshippers!

That where parting ends, reunion begins.

This is Allah's way in His creation; but you are hasty.

## (The Parting of the Angry One)

He left his people in anger at them; so he lost land and sea and earth and sank into darkness upon darkness, until his people thought he would never return,

And he returned

The same story as Ayyub's, illness and parting (But We threw him onto the open shore while he was ill); only here it is parting, then illness!

The order doesn't matter, for all of us pass through these decrees.. but knowledge and wisdom depend on which of them came first. What matters is this: tell me, whom do you resemble, Dhun-Nun or Ayyub?

Then came healing and reunion (And We caused to grow over him a gourd vine. And We sent him to [his people of] a hundred thousand or more).

And when the reunion came, it was finer than the parting

For they were better in faith and character than he had left them

Had that parting never happened, faith would have made no difference to them

Perhaps a parting brought about repentance; perhaps a separation brought knowledge to light.

## (Two Partings, or Three, Maybe More / Viewer Warning: Not for the Faint of Heart)

The hardest parting is to be driven away by the one you love,

And for the One you love to command you to part from the one you love,

His father drove him out ([His father] said, “Have you no desire for my gods, O Abraham? If you do not desist, I will surely stone you, so avoid me a prolonged time”)

He left his house, his homeland, his land

Then Allah commands him to kill his son with his own hand!

A parting that comes about without your hand

And a parting that comes about by your hand

Both are bound to come in life (Indeed, this was the clear trial)

The parting that comes without your hand is decree

And the parting that comes by your hand is choice: to choose the righteous and part from the wicked. Not because you are perfect, of course! But a person can barely hold on to his own goodness, so we have no time to argue over a road we have already chosen.

## (The Parting of the Coerced and the Reunion of the Compelled / A Sad Scene)

(and made its people into factions) Pharaoh cut the Children of Israel apart and tore them into groups so that they could not come together, by coercion and force, and so that dominion and sovereignty would never be theirs.

And in the verse there is *iḥtibāk*! That is, leaving out the end because the beginning points to it: the end of the story, their reunion, is left out, and its beginning, their scattering, is mentioned (and made its people into factions).

Meaning: what looks like parting on the outside is, on the inside, the mercy of reunion (So We restored him to his mother that she might be content and not grieve)

## (A Scene That May Be Merged with the Previous One)

Enmities, grudges, anger, hatred, a face that cannot bear the sight of another face. Whoever has insight can see how it ends: (Perhaps Allah will put, between you and those to whom you have been enemies among them, affection. And Allah is competent, and Allah is Forgiving and Merciful)

Every “perhaps” (*ʿasā*) in the Quran is binding: certain to happen and to come true. Let the mind or the soul deny it; it is coming, it is surely coming, because it is recompense (And indeed, the recompense is to occur).

Competent to mend what was severed

And Forgiving and Merciful: He forgives what lies between those at odds, turns to them so that they may turn back to Him, then makes peace between them; (and settlement is best), and is there any good that is not from Allah?

(O Prophet, when you [Muslims] divorce women…) until He said (You know not; perhaps Allah will bring about after that a [different] matter)

You do not know the unseen, O veiled one, nor the decrees it holds, nor what is hidden from eyes behind its curtains. Perhaps a return after a divorce; perhaps a reunion after a parting.

For the whole matter belongs to Allah.
