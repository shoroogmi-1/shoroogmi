Title: (And of the people is he who sells himself, seeking means to the approval of Allah. And Allah is kind to [His] servants)
Excerpt: The believer in this world is like the *mukātab* slave (and we are all slaves of Allah): a slave whose master has agreed with him on a fixed sum of money, and the moment he pays it in full, his master sets him free.

The believer in this world is like the *mukātab* slave (and we are all slaves of Allah): a slave whose master has agreed with him on a fixed sum of money, and the moment he pays it in full, his master sets him free.

So the believer buys himself back, ransoming his soul from doubts, whims and cravings, until not a single debt remains on him, not even an atom's weight of whim, without his having freed himself from it. And the deeper he goes into bondage to Allah the Exalted, the more he is freed from bondage to everything else: the world and all its creatures.

The more complete the servitude, the more beautiful and the sweeter the freedom.. That is true freedom: freedom of the spirit, not of wealth or body or self. A freedom in which a person truly, genuinely chooses what he loves and what he hates; a freedom that cares nothing for people's blame or praise; a freedom that began with the Truth and ends in Him, far removed from creation; a freedom stripped of the garments of fame, praise and blame, of hatred and love. Something from above. And how could what is from above descend, to be grasped by lowly minds that cling to the world, whose souls are bound up in it?

"So when he abandons disbelief and sin and steps forward into faith and obedience, it is as though he has bought himself free of the punishment and the Fire. The believer, then, is like the *mukātab*, who pays a counted number of dirhams and buys himself with them; so too the believer spends a counted number of breaths and with them buys himself forever. But the *mukātab* remains a slave as long as a single dirham is still owed; and likewise the one under obligation is never released from the bondage of servitude as long as he has a single breath left in this world." al-Razi

He closed the verse with kindness to affirm that whoever bears these traits (selling himself, seeking the approval of Allah) will receive Allah's good gifts and His mercies, in this world and the Hereafter

"Kindness here stands, by metonymy, for what it entails, namely the bestowal of good things, just as mercy does

And the apparent sense is that the definite article in His words *al-ʿibād* (“the servants”) is one of totality, for Allah is kind to all His servants, though they differ in their share of it: among them are those whom Allah's kindness reaches in this world and in the Hereafter, in differing measures in each, as Allah's knowledge and wisdom decree" Ibn Ashur
