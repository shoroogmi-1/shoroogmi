Title: Whoever Knows Allah, What Does He Not Know?
Excerpt: "Whoever knows Allah, what is there left for him not to know?

"Whoever knows Allah, what is there left for him not to know?

And whoever does not know Allah, what is it that he knows?"

To strike out across the earth, leaving behind the shelves of books and the clashing studies, to walk out of your library with your hair wild, seeking knowledge with your heart and carrying it to every heart: this, too, is a kind of knowing.

Competition in [worldly] increase diverts you: some scholars said this means the piling up of books.

"When you hear a hadith from me that your hearts recognize, that makes your hair and your skin grow soft, and that you feel to be near to you, then I am nearer to it than any of you. And when you hear a hadith from me that your hearts deny, that makes your hair and your skin shrink back, and that you feel to be far from you, then I am farther from it than any of you."

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ knew that hadiths would multiply and that knowledge would keep spreading and never shrink; so he made the heart's knowing one of the paths of learning. Faith itself is a kind of knowledge that people of reason acknowledge.

And the heart's knowing is only as strong as faith and its branches, certainty and its shade, awe, reverent stillness, and humble lowliness before the truth and before people.

Worship and faith are the water the heart drinks, and from it there springs up knowledge of what to say, what to do, and how to be.
