Title: The Delight of the Art of Listening to the Noble Quran
Excerpt: Quran memorization expert Dr. Saeed Hamza mentions in his book and courses "The Five Fortresses" that, in addition to your daily portion of reading, you must have a daily portion of listening to the Noble Quran of no less than 30 minutes a day, which is equivalent to 41 verses, or let's say four to five pages, or half a juz', or a hizb.

Quran memorization expert Dr. Saeed Hamza mentions in his book and courses "The Five Fortresses" that, in addition to your daily portion of reading, you must have a daily portion of listening to the Noble Quran of no less than 30 minutes a day, which is equivalent to 41 verses, or let's say four to five pages, or half a juz', or a hizb.

Perhaps he derived it from the hadith: “The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to me: (Recite to me Surat an-Nisa'), so I recited until I reached {So how will it be when We bring from every nation a witness and bring you, [O Muhammad], against these as a witness?} [An-Nisa: 41]. He said: Either he nudged me, or I turned, and his eyes were flowing with tears ﷺ”

And know - may Allah protect you - that nothing affects the soul and stirs the heart and feelings like listening, nothing at all, and you cannot imagine its tremendous benefits for renewing presence of mind, softening humility, and sharpening reflection; how many a written reflection had its origin in listening. The soul grows bored with and recoils from a single method, like relying on reading alone.

And don't say: I'm busy! I can barely, barely get through my reading portion, and now you want me to listen too! No, that's too much.

We can find time for anything if we want it (And if they had intended to go forth, they would have prepared for it some preparation). And what is the way to achieve that?

1- Make it a daily habit, put it in your daily plan and schedule.

2- Start with a few minutes, then increase the amount over time (even a 10-second clip, yes, I'm serious)

3- Speed up the recording! -we'll mention the condition later-.

4- Listen to it during routine tasks, like driving the car.

Don't listen to the Quran of prayers!

And why?

Because the imam is a human being, prone to mistakes and slips; he may swap one verse for another, or forget it. So listen to the Quran only from recorded, recited mushafs that have been checked and reviewed, such as the King Fahd Mushaf, or from apps such as the Wahy app.

See the King Fahd Complex apps here:

qurancomplex.gov.sa/apps/

Don't say: But some YouTube channels remove and fix the mistakes.

Don't say that, for I listened to a complete mushaf from the Affairs of the Imams and Muezzins of the Haram in Makkah, and it had 3-4 mistakes the editors didn't catch -once again, it's natural for mistakes to happen; the imams are human like us, and the point is only to alert those who care about listening to the Quran to these observations- sometimes the imam slips and brings the Warsh reading into Hafs! And someone with no knowledge of the qira'at may memorize the pronunciation, think it is the Hafs reading, and recite it and repeat it! And he may think this is part of tajweed and masterful precision! The poor thing doesn't know he has made a mistake!

Verify: if you suspect the imam made a mistake, check the mushaf by reading and correct the verse immediately! So that you don't pick up the wrong reading -and how many times this method has saved me!-.

I still remember memorizing some verses in a sweet voice with one of them from a Taraweeh prayer, then discovering, months after memorizing, that there was a mistake in my memorization. I asked myself what the source of the mistake was, and found it was the clip I had been memorizing from and listening to: he made a mistake, so I made a mistake and memorized the mistake! And the mistake became entrenched in my memorization! So see how much trouble it's been correcting it; to this day I struggle with it and sometimes slip, and I say: if only I had listened to the advice of the adviser from the beginning: listen only to recorded mushafs.

The temptation of prayer recordings is that they have a lofty spirituality without limit or end, and a vocal beauty you cannot find in a recorded mushaf. Listen to them -there's nothing wrong with them- I do this sometimes, but I always find mistakes -I said always, not rarely or sometimes, and that is when listening to a complete surah of medium or long length, except for a few, and from the most renowned for precision-.!

But accustom your ear to the precise recorded mushaf.

Should I use speeding up the recording?

Yes, I have read that speeding up Quran recordings is permissible on condition that it doesn't lead to letters and words running into each other, or to a gabble from which nothing can be understood.

Don't you use hadr (rapid recitation) when reading? Well, this is hadr in listening.

Okay, there's a problem!

Say it!

Sometimes my mind wanders and I get drowsy and don't know what the reciter is saying?

Leave aside the whisperings of Satan and listen even if you didn't focus, reflect and understand. Let's drop the perfectionism! And listen to what Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal said:

He said: I heard Abdullah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal say: I heard my father say: I saw the Lord of Might, the Mighty and Majestic, in a dream, and I said: O Lord, what is the best thing by which those who seek nearness draw near to You? He said: My speech, O Ahmad. He said: I said: O Lord, with understanding or without understanding? He said: With understanding and without understanding.

(Manaqib al-Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn al-Jawzi)

Among the etiquettes of listening to the Quran: the righteous predecessors, after listening to poems, chants and the like, would conclude them with recitation of the Quran, and some of them before and after; perhaps the wisdom in this is that it removes heedlessness, re-energizes the heart and returns it to faith. (See Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din by al-Ghazali - Listening)

Is the listening portion different from the reading portion?

Whatever suits you!

It is better for the listening portion to be different from the reading portion, so that if you stop at a certain minute, you continue tomorrow from where you left off.

And make your reading portion different, so that you have two completions: a reading completion and a listening completion.

And if you want to complete one with the other, there is latitude in the matter!

There is a wonderful idea -though it's hard to apply- which is listening while at the same time reading some tafsir, and so I recommend the Wahy app (31 tafsir encyclopedias, with no internet and no ads. Look up how to download the books and audio)

Don't set ideal, perfect conditions, for they are the reason you postpone listening to the Quran, such as requiring of yourself: to listen only to the mujawwad mushaf, or to a particular reciter with demanding tajweed, or complete focus and attention, or slow listening at speed 1. All of these are obstacles that have stood between you and completing your listening portion, so start now without these conditions. Apply this right this hour; don't say tomorrow, because you'll forget, and when an action follows an affecting word, it lasts longer!

For memorizers: one of the tried-and-tested ways of correcting recitation is to listen and move your lips along without speaking aloud. The aim is to discover your mistakes in memorization, recitation and tajweed; if you raise your voice with the reciter, you may not hear what he said well and may not notice your own mistake, because your voice is louder than his. I have found it to be the best way so far, after a teacher, to discover recitation mistakes and correct them.

If you benefited: then make good supplications for the writer of these words.
