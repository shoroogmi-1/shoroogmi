Educators say: when a child screams, disobeys, and rebels, the best thing you can do is 1- come close to him physically and place your hand on the back of his head (this is for gentleness and to calm the lower brain, which is responsible for emotions and reactions) 2- then call him by his name (this is to show affection and warmth, so he is more inclined to accept the message) 3- then tell him the behavior he should do.

It reminded me of the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ

When Aisha became angry, he would take hold of her nose and say: “O Uwaysh, say: O Allah, Lord of Muhammad, forgive me my sin, remove the rage of my heart, and protect me from the misleading trials” (weak)

And in one wording: “he took hold of my nose”; so he took hold of her nose to calm her.

Then he calls her affectionately by the dearest name (O Uwaysh) (and in another wording of the hadith: O Uwayshah, and Aa’ish), and he did not say “O Aisha”; to give the words gentleness, warmth, love, charm, and wit. Had he called her “Aisha,” there might have been seriousness and sharpness in it, so he wanted to fully soften both the words and the head.

Then he tells her what she should do (“say…”), not forbid her from the action! Don’t get angry, drop it, there’s no need for problems over things that aren’t worth it! Rather, he goes straight into what he wants done.

An analysis of the upbringing of Luqman (peace be upon him):

Luqman called his son three times in succession before each piece of advice on a new topic, but with prohibition he did not use “O my son” except in one place, and we will come to explaining the reasons in their places, Allah willing.

In it he began with command (what should be done) in more than one place, then prohibition (what should not be done), and this is the soundest of educational methods.

And [mention], when Luqman said to his son while he was instructing him, “O my son, do not associate [anything] with Allah. Indeed, association [with Him] is great injustice.” (13)

And after the instruction to honor parents, he said:

(O my son, indeed if wrong should be the weight of a mustard seed and should be within a rock or [anywhere] in the heavens or in the earth, Allah will bring it forth. Indeed, Allah is Subtle and Acquainted. (16) O my son, establish prayer, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and be patient over what befalls you. Indeed, [all] that is of the matters [requiring] determination. (17) And do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful. (18) And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice; indeed, the most disagreeable of sounds is the voice of donkeys. (19)

O my son: he says it out of gentleness and affection toward his son. Had he called him by his name, there would have been a sharpness in it that causes estrangement, and the advice would not enter the heart! Rather, its limit would be the mind, and then it might stay, or it might go out the ear.

And you see that all the prophets (peace be upon them) call their children by sonship, the obedient and the disobedient alike!

Noah (peace be upon him): O my son, come aboard with us (42)

And Abraham and Jacob (peace be upon them): And Abraham instructed his sons [to do the same] and [so did] Jacob, [saying], “O my sons, indeed Allah has chosen for you this religion, so do not die except while you are Muslims.” (132) Al-Baqarah

As for Jacob (peace be upon him), he used it a great deal, even with his sons when they had disobeyed his command and wronged him! (O my sons, go and find out about Joseph and his brother (87)

And with Joseph (peace be upon him): (He said, “O my son, do not relate your vision to your brothers (5)

And this upbringing was reflected in Joseph (peace be upon him) too, for he calls his companions in prison with the gentlest call (O [my] two companions of prison, are separate lords better or Allah, the One, the Prevailing? (39)

And Abraham with his son Ishmael (peace be upon them) (O my son, indeed I have seen in a dream that I [must] sacrifice you.. (102)

And we return once more to Luqman with his son:

With every piece of advice he put “O my son” first. In my view it is possible, and Allah knows best:

1- Either they were pieces of advice he gave him on separate days; that is why he would begin them with “O my son.”

And among the Quran’s rhetorical styles is brevity, so it is not reasonable for it to say with every verse: and on the 11th day of such-and-such month, of such-and-such year, at such-and-such hour, Luqman the Wise said…. and on the … day of such-and-such month…. he said such-and-such!

And this is found in the style of rhetoricians and men of letters when compiling the sayings of the wise: they are gathered by topics and ideas without recording the date and time they were said.

Likewise with any wise man, you find his sayings gathered in one book, like Sahih al-Bukhari for the hadiths of the Messenger ﷺ, and we know for certain that he did not say them in a single day!!

Or the letters and essays of a thinker or philosopher, you find them collected in one book without dates (some of them).

2- Or they were gathered in a single day.

The timing of messages

There are always educational messages you teach your child from time to time, and you always think: what is the lesson I want to teach him?

And it is better to spread them over months and days rather than gather them in one sitting, so that boredom, weariness, and tedium, and then rejection, do not occur. That is why I strongly support the first possibility.

And you see that in the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ: the advice is one line and a few words, unless the Companion says: “Tell me more,” and some, yes, are combined pieces of advice.

Then, in the priorities of educational messages, he began with him 1- with creed and monotheism, 2- then with what grows his faith and his certainty that there is no bad deed and no good deed but that Allah is able to bring it forth, so as to attach his heart more to Allah. And from here we understand that building certainty and thinking well of Allah is a basic and essential educational touchstone, not a luxury! The prophets raised their children and their peoples on it (and if we gathered all the evidence we would lose the topic! But it is plentiful, like Noah commanding his people to seek forgiveness and promising them that He would provide them with wealth and children, and others..)

This foundation is what protects children from psychological disorders, depression, and anxiety, and preserves faith from cracking and splitting apart.

And after the solid roots in building the boy’s identity, he went to the branches 3- establishing prayer, enjoining good and forbidding evil, patience, and urging him toward resolve and lofty matters. Likewise the child must have his self spurred on and encouraged toward lofty things and ambitions, and toward rising above the petty and the trivial. High aspiration is something the young are raised on.

And when he had finished with the roots in forming identity (creed and faith) and the branches of religion (prayer and enjoining good..)

He was ready to receive guidance on dealings and advice on how to treat people, by forbidding him from arrogance. And now notice with me (may Allah bless your hearing and sight) that when forbidding vile things he does not use the call of sonship! He did not say “O my son”! And this is the most perfect in the manners of discipline, disciplining with refinement, the most beautiful style, the fullest tenderness, and the soundest good omen. Had he said “O my son, do not be arrogant,” it would be as though it were said that the trait of arrogance is in his very self. But when you say: do not give arrogance a way over you, you set his self and being apart from the ugly trait, and put a great distance between them so he does not come near it; because you have kept “O my son” away from it!

And notice with me that he did not say to him: do not be arrogant! He did not use the explicit word (arrogance); rather he used allusion! (And do not turn [your cheek]) and he addressed in it the cheek, the voice, and the walk (your cheek, your pace, your voice) because they are real parts of the human self. And he did not address arrogance itself, because it is not original to the self, and so as not to plant it in him unawares, or stubbornness, had he forbidden him with it.

And he also concluded it with advice on refinement and general manners (And be moderate in your pace…), and now they call it by the modern term: etiquette, taste, and manners, and it is fitting to look into it and learn it!

A dilemma!

But why, when he forbade him from shirk, did he say “O my son”!

The answer: because it was the beginning of the advice, so affection was needed at its start; and then because shirk is a great injustice! So rebuke and prohibition of it were necessary.

And it is fitting, in advice on the roots and branches of religion, to use command, and in advice on dealings, to use prohibition. And combining the two makes for the most complete understanding and comprehension.
