It reminded me of the way of the Prophet ﷺ

Whenever Aisha grew angry, he would squeeze her nose and say: “O Uwaysh, say: O Allah, Lord of Muhammad, forgive me my sin, take away the rage of my heart, and protect me from the trials that lead astray” (weak)

And in one wording: “he took hold of my nose”; he took hold of her nose to calm her.

Then he calls her tenderly by the dearest of names (O Uwaysh) (another wording of the hadith has: O Uwayshah, and Aa'ish), and he did not say “O Aisha,” so as to wrap his words in gentleness, warmth, love, charm and playfulness. Had he called her “Aisha,” there might have been a seriousness, an edge, to it; he wanted to soften the words, and the head, completely.

Then he tells her what she should do (“say…”), rather than forbidding her what she was doing! Not: don't be angry, let it go, there's no need to make trouble over things that aren't worth it! He goes straight to what he wants her to do.

An analysis of Luqman's way of raising his son (peace be upon him):

Luqman called his son three times in succession, once before each piece of advice that opened a new subject; yet with prohibition he did not use “O my son” except in one place. We will explain the reasons in their place, Allah willing.

More than once he began with command (what ought to be done), then prohibition (what ought not to be done), and this is the soundest of educational methods.

And [mention, O Muhammad], when Luqman said to his son while he was instructing him, “O my son, do not associate [anything] with Allah. Indeed, association [with him] is great injustice.” (13)

And after the counsel to honor parents, he said:

(O my son, indeed if wrong should be the weight of a mustard seed and should be within a rock or [anywhere] in the heavens or in the earth, Allah will bring it forth. Indeed, Allah is Subtle and Acquainted (16) O my son, establish prayer, enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and be patient over what befalls you. Indeed, [all] that is of the matters [requiring] determination (17) And do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful (18) And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice; indeed, the most disagreeable of sounds is the voice of donkeys (19)

O my son: he says it to be gentle and loving toward his son. Had he called him by his name, there would have been an edge to it that breeds distance, and the counsel would not reach the heart! At most it would reach the mind, where it might stay, or it might go out the other ear.

And you can see that all the prophets, peace be upon them, address their children as “my son,” the obedient and the disobedient alike!

Noah, peace be upon him: O my son, come aboard with us (42)

And Abraham and Jacob, peace be upon them: And Abraham instructed his sons [to do the same] and [so did] Jacob, [saying], “O my sons, indeed Allah has chosen for you this religion, so do not die except while you are Muslims.” (132) Al-Baqarah

As for Jacob, peace be upon him, he said it again and again, even to sons who had disobeyed his command and wronged him! (O my sons, go and find out about Joseph and his brother (87)

And to Joseph, peace be upon him: (He said, “O my son, do not relate your vision to your brothers (5)

And this upbringing shone back from Joseph, peace be upon him, too: he calls to his companions in prison with the gentlest of calls (O [my] two companions of prison, are separate lords better or Allah, the One, the Prevailing? (39)

And Abraham with his son Ishmael, peace be upon them: (O my son, indeed I have seen in a dream that I [must] sacrifice you.. (102)

Now we return once more to Luqman and his son:

With every piece of counsel he put “O my son” first. I see two possibilities, and Allah knows best:

1- Either these were counsels he gave him on different days, which is why he opened each one with “O my son.”

Brevity is one of the Quran's rhetorical ways; it is hardly to be expected that it would say with every verse: and on the 11th of such-and-such month, in such-and-such year, at such-and-such hour, Luqman the Wise said…. and on the … of such-and-such month…. he said such-and-such!

You find the same in the practice of rhetoricians and men of letters when they gather the sayings of the wise: they arrange them by subject and idea, without recording when each was said.

So it is with any sage: you find his sayings gathered into one book, just as Sahih al-Bukhari gathers the hadiths of the Messenger ﷺ, and we know for certain that he did not say them all in a single day!!

Or the letters and essays of a thinker or philosopher: you find them collected in one volume, some of them undated.

2- Or they were all given on a single day.

The timing of the messages

There are always lessons you teach your child from time to time, and you are always asking yourself: what is the lesson I want to teach him?

It is better to spread them over months and days than to pour them all out in one sitting, so that boredom, weariness and tedium, and then rejection, do not set in. That is why I lean strongly toward the first possibility.

You see this in the way of the Prophet ﷺ: a counsel is a single line, a handful of words, unless the Companion says: “Tell me more.” And yes, some are several counsels given together.

Then, in ordering the lessons by priority, he began 1- with creed and the oneness of Allah, 2- then with what grows his faith and his certainty that there is no bad deed and no good deed that Allah cannot bring forth, so as to bind his heart more closely to Allah. From this we understand that building certainty and good expectation of Allah is a basic, essential touchstone of upbringing, not a luxury! The prophets raised their children and their peoples on it (and if we marshalled all the evidence we would lose our subject! But it is plentiful: Noah, for instance, bidding his people seek forgiveness and promising that He would give them increase in wealth and children, and more besides..)

This foundation is what shields children from psychological disorders, depression and anxiety, and keeps their faith from cracking and splitting apart.

After laying solid roots for the boy's identity, he moved on to the branches: 3- establishing prayer, enjoining good and forbidding evil, patience, and spurring him toward resolve and lofty aims. So too a child must have his spirit roused and encouraged toward high things and great ambitions, toward rising above the petty and the trivial. High aspiration is something the young must be raised on.

And once he had finished with the roots that form identity (creed and faith) and the branches of religion (prayer, enjoining good..)

His son was ready to receive guidance on conduct and counsel on dealing with people, beginning with the warning against arrogance. Now notice with me (may Allah bless your hearing and your sight) that when he forbids ugly traits, he does not use the call of sonship! He does not say “O my son”! This is the height of refinement in teaching manners, the most beautiful of styles, the fullest tenderness and the soundest good omen. Had he said “O my son, do not be arrogant,” it would be as if he were saying that arrogance lived in his very self. But when you say: do not give arrogance any way into you, you set his self and his being apart from the ugly trait and put a great distance between them, so that he does not come near it, because you have kept “O my son” well away from it!

And notice with me that he did not say to him: do not be arrogant! He did not use the plain word (arrogance); he used allusion! (And do not turn your cheek). He spoke of the cheek, the voice and the walk (your cheek, your pace, your voice) because they are real parts of the human self. He did not name arrogance itself, because it is not native to the soul, and so as not to plant it in him unawares, or plant stubbornness, by forbidding him in those words.

And he closed with counsels of good taste and general manners (And be moderate in your pace…), which today go by the modern names of etiquette, taste and manners; it is well worth reading up on them and learning them!

A puzzle!

But why, when he forbade him from associating partners with Allah, did he say “O my son”?!

The answer: because it was the opening of his counsel, so it had to begin with tenderness; and then because associating partners with Allah is a great injustice! It had to be rebuked and forbidden.

It is fitting, then, in counsel on the roots and branches of religion, to use command, and in counsel on dealing with people, to use prohibition; and joining the two makes for the fullest understanding and grasp.
