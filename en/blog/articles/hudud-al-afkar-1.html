An Epistle on the Limits of Thoughts / 1

  • 📅 May 24, 2026
  • ⏱️ 5 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Know (may Allah grant us and you success) that hearts have their earnings, and minds have their limit.

The earnings of the heart are its deeds: the passing thoughts it lets settle within it, the delights and the tears it has laid up in store. And this is His saying (but He imposes blame upon you for what your hearts have earned).

And minds have a limit where they must end and halt; were they to cross it, they would destroy crops and animals, (And Allah does not like corruption), not even in the mind does He like it! For the mind is the field where thoughts are sown, the seedbed of deeds, the nursery of the soul, the food of subtle graces, the drawing-near of secrets, the storehouse of tidings. Inspiration from the Lord, the Exalted, is an honour and a mark of His generosity, and it is fitting that it descend only upon pure ground: just as one who remembers Allah ought not to eat garlic and onion, and one who prays may not pray in a place of impurity.

Those who know Allah know that whoever is deprived of subtle graces and insights, whose heart is veiled from them, owes it to a sin his heart and mind have earned, not his limbs. Do you not know that the recompense matches the deed?

For these subtle graces are hidden knowledge, veiled from creation and reserved for the servant; their rapture (wajd) steals into his inner being, and so he finds them (wajadahā). Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (may Allah have mercy on him) said of the words of the Exalted (In their hearts is disease, so Allah has increased their disease): it is the withholding of any further graces.

So what the heart earns either veils understanding, or polishes the inner sight until it understands from the All-Knowing.

This, then, is the sorrow of those who know: states they once harvested, subtle graces they once beheld, then veiled from them by the sins of the heart. This is their sorrow in this world, and nothing besides.

The Sufis went too far, carried to excess in annihilation and eternity, in ceaseless contemplation in the presence of the Eternal Refuge. For a human being, whoever he may be, needs to reflect on his life in this world just as on his life in the next

Have you not heard His words: (that you might give thought to this world and the Hereafter)?

By this He means reflection on the interests and affairs of this world and on its sciences, on how things work and how they are to be managed; for no deed is sound unless the thought behind it is sound. Now that you know this (may Allah keep you safe), let us move on to its limits

The Limits of Reflecting on This World

Whatever is outwardly permissible is inwardly permissible too, that is, it is permissible to think about; and whatever is outwardly forbidden to the limbs, it is forbidden even to think about! Between the two lie doubtful matters, and according to the light of those who know and their degrees of piety, understanding shines forth and scrupulousness grows strong.

Know this, and you will be safe.

(And know that Allah knows what is within yourselves, so beware of Him) "He warned them about what passes through their hearts concerning women, and guided them to harbour good rather than evil; then He did not leave them despairing of His mercy, nor hopeless of His kindness, but said: (And know that Allah is Forgiving and Forbearing)" - Ibn Kathir

This verse comes in the context of proposing marriage; and since most passions fall within that domain, it is cited here.

It is fitting, then, for the resolute and the shrewd to pack away the baggage of his passions and emotions and cut them off, and for the brave and the noble to stand against the current of his nature and master it; for this is the great summit, the great resolve, the farthest reach.

We have said: even if he feels love, no one is held to account for a feeling, so long as he does not drink from the cup of imaginings, nor let himself drift into what feeds them. And the one truly strong and trustworthy, firm of heart, is he who stands guard at the frontiers of imaginings and memories, strips himself of them, and says: "Allah, Allah is my Lord, I do not associate anything as partner with Him." (And take your provision from the skills psychology offers for mastering your thoughts, for they are truly useful; take them in search of purity from passions, and for the journey to the Lord of the worlds)

The Limits of Reflecting on the Hereafter

One reflects on it in submission, humility and awe, not in the manner of those who plunge into the knowledge of the unseen and philosophize about the Lord's attributes (exalted is He, high above what they say).

Rather, let him reflect on Paradise until his soul yearns for it, and picture himself standing before the Lord of the worlds and being questioned; and let him strive to summon fear, humility and love, for the seeker begins by striving and ends by arriving, rightly guided.

And when he slips into thoughts of passion, let him not excuse himself with pleas of weakness and escape. For Yusuf (peace be upon him) had ten excuses counted for him to fall into sin, and still he did not!

He was young, a stranger, unmarried; and she was the queen of the palace, from whom commands and prohibitions flowed. Yet for all that he fled from her and refused her, and so he humbled her, and was himself raised in honour.

So put your excuses aside; even the prophets lived in times of trial, and you are not the only one being tested.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
