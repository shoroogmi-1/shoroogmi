Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

On the Meaning of the Quran Being a Mercy

  • 📅 August 1, 2026
  • ⏱️ 13 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

I have always wondered: what does it mean that Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, describes the Quran again and again as good tidings and mercy? Things are known by their opposites, and the opposite of mercy is torment. In more than one place, Allah the Exalted speaks of the inner torment and pain that befall those who do not follow al-Furqan, the Criterion, the Noble Quran. In Surah Al Imran He says: (And He revealed the Qur'an. Indeed, those who disbelieve in the verses of Allah will have a severe punishment). The exegetes explain that this severe punishment is the torment of the soul that makes the breast grow tight; that is why He called it severe: the torment of the soul, with all its anguish and pain. And in Surah Al-An'am: (So whoever Allah wants to guide - He expands his breast to [contain] Islam; and whoever He wants to misguide - He makes his breast tight and constricted as though he were climbing into the sky. Thus does Allah place defilement upon those who do not believe). As for defilement (al-rijs): some said it is Satan, and this is the stronger view; others said al-rijs is torment, meaning the inner torment of one whose breast never opens, no matter what he does or achieves in this life: there is always that torment, that pain. But whoever follows guidance, believes, and holds fast to the Noble Quran, for him the Quran is what our Lord, Glorified and Exalted is He, said it is: a mercy for the believers, through which the believer is shown mercy.

Yes, I know there are people who turn to the Noble Quran to lift their worries, to ease their way, for provision and for knowledge, and that is good and well. The trouble is that it is a temporary goal. Once the goal of all that reading and all those completions is met (praise be to Allah, my debt was paid after I completed the Quran; praise be to Allah, I got the job after I completed the Quran), fine: then, once you have the job, do you abandon the Quran? Do you abandon the Quran? That is a short-term goal. And if the goal is not met, do you abandon the Quran? Do you abandon the Quran? I remember reading the story of a woman who used to complete the Quran again and again and join completion groups, and then she stopped: after three months she gave up completing it because the goal she wanted had not come true. Nothing happened, she said. That is the problem when we take up the Quran for aims and purposes other than the ones Allah the Exalted revealed it for. Allah the Exalted revealed the Quran as a mercy: so that peace, gladness, and richness of heart and spirit may come, so that they may blend together. It is also a blessing, a means of provision, and a means of multiplying good deeds, and with them, provision in this world. But the problem, as with this woman, was that through all those completions what she wanted never came, so she abandoned the Quran and abandoned the completions. Then she began to feel her heart tighten, and she said: I have lost something I had in the Quran, something that made me happy. And so she realized that its true purpose is happiness and peace, not provision alone, and she returned once more to completing it.

If only the Noble Quran were taken as a cure for the soul and all it holds of desires, doubts, whims, emotions, and the rest; and taken, too, as a fortress for the soul and a source of its strength. This is what the daily portion of the Quran is for: every person must have a daily portion of the Quran, read or listened to in whatever voice he loves, never abandoned, never let go; and if he cannot manage a juz' of the Quran, then a single page a day. I remember one woman who had memorized the Quran telling me the story of how she did it. She said that at first she used to attend Quran classes, but then she spent a whole year reading one juz' every day. And she told me: Before that, I made sure to keep a daily portion of one page, and when I was under pressure, swamped with work and errands, then before I slept I would read even a single verse. And she told me: I really did do that, more than once; I was so pressed that day with work and errands that before I slept I opened the Mushaf, read one verse, closed it, prayed, and slept. So look how Allah the Exalted, when He saw her persevering and keeping up her reading, rewarded her by letting her memorize the Noble Quran; that was her reward. So let no one shortchange the Quran of its due. Let him begin his bond with it by listening or by reciting; he need not start at the beginning and go through to the end. He may read a particular surah he loves and repeat it. That is how the bond with the Quran begins: with something a person loves, so that afterward the Quran grows dearer and dearer to him.

Now let us reflect on the last page of Surah Al-A'raf: (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous). A few verses later, Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, says: (Take what is given freely, enjoin what is good, and turn away from the ignorant). It is related that when Gabriel came down with this verse, the Messenger ﷺ said: I will turn away from the ignorant, but some resentment and anger toward him will still remain inside me. This is the meaning: take what is given freely, that is, take whatever people's characters allow; accept their manners as they are and do not ask for more; enjoin what is good, that is, what is right and known to be right; and turn away from the ignorant. Then the Messenger ﷺ said to Gabriel: But what of the anger, O Gabriel? And Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, revealed: (And if an evil suggestion comes to you from Satan, then seek refuge in Allah. Indeed, He is Hearing and Knowing). There are two views of this suggestion (al-nazgh). The first is that it is the urge toward a wrong: a person sets his mind on a sin, on disobedience; so when this suggestion, this urge, comes to him, let him seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed: I seek refuge in Allah, the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, from Satan, the accursed. And according to most exegetes, al-nazgh here is also anger, so he seeks refuge in Allah the Exalted from it.

Then He says: (Indeed, those who fear Allah - when an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember [Him] and at once they have insight). The impulse (ṭāʾif) here, they said, is a passing thought, an evil thought: evil thoughts that come over a person. They remember: that is, they remember Allah the Exalted and remember the Home of the Hereafter, and seek refuge from Satan, the accursed, and the evil thought is gone. These are Satan's suggestions: hit him, curse her, do this; all of them are the whisperings of Satan, and their cure is seeking refuge and remembering: remembering the Hereafter, and being reminded by the Noble Quran, taking it up so that one holds back from anger, guards one's heart, and heals what is in it: the resentment smoldering in the breast, the anger, the rancor, and all the rest.

Then He says: (But their brothers - the devils increase them in error; then they do not stop short). Their brothers are the human brothers of the devils: Satan comes to a person and whispers in his breast, do this, don't do that, and dresses it up for him, and he gives in and goes astray. Ibn Abbas said: Satan drives him toward sin, harrying him on; he goads him with desires and doubts, with whisperings, worries, and sorrows that goad him on and on into sin and disobedience: do this, say that, curse him, insult her; and so the breast is seething all the time. (In error; then they do not stop short): they increase them, that is, they keep feeding them such thoughts and notions. Then they do not stop short: they never let up, never weary of sin and disobedience, never weary, moving from one desire to the next, from one sin to the next, sinking ever deeper into error. That is the meaning.

Then Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, gives the cure for the soul, for everything in the breast of resentment, anger, rancor, desire, and whim: (And when you, [O Muhammad], do not bring them a sign, they say, "Why have you not contrived it?" Say, "I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord. This [Qur'an] is enlightenment from your Lord and guidance and mercy for a people who believe.") And here is the heart of it: the cure through which a person is shown mercy is the Noble Quran, (that you may receive mercy). Allah the Exalted made it baṣāʾir, insights: (enlightenment from your Lord). Notice: not a single sight, but insights, as though one had more than one sight, more than one eye to see and behold with. And He made its purpose, and repeated it, that it bear the fruit of guidance and mercy: (and guidance and mercy for a people who believe). And then once more: (So when the Qur'an is recited, then listen to it and pay attention that you may receive mercy).

Why? This mercy, the scholars said, is a mercy that reaches the believer in this world, and so does the guidance, just as with the patient: (Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is those who are the [rightly] guided). They said: the blessings are the Lord's praise of them, and it comes in this world; and mercy and guidance come in this world too. And at the top of that same page He says: (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous): whoever takes the Noble Quran to heart as his ally, Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, takes him as His ally, and by His mercy brings him in among the righteous, among His righteous servants and allies, who are shown mercy through this Book.

And the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has His own people among mankind." It was asked: "O Messenger of Allah, who are they?" He said: "The people of the Qur'an, the people of Allah and those who are closest to Him." In explaining this hadith, the scholars said: Allah the Exalted takes care of the people of the Quran as a guardian takes care of his household, or as a shepherd tends his own family; He watches over them in the affairs of this world and of the Hereafter, setting them right, guiding them, and making this Quran a mercy for them. Likewise in Surah Al-A'raf, the Exalted says: ([Allah] said, "My punishment - I afflict with it whom I will, but My mercy encompasses all things." So I will decree it [especially] for those who fear Me and give zakah and those who believe in Our verses - Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet) to the end of the verse, where He says: (and followed the light which was sent down with him - it is those who will be the successful). So He bound success and mercy together: He decreed this mercy for whoever follows the Book and the guidance, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the Sunnah; and likewise success in this world and the Hereafter for whoever follows the Noble Quran and holds to it with strength.

Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, also says in Surah Al-A'raf: (And there followed them successors who inherited the Scripture [while] taking the commodities of this lower life and saying, "It will be forgiven for us." And if an offer like it comes to them, they will [again] take it. Was not the covenant of the Scripture taken from them that they would not say about Allah except the truth, and they studied what was in it? And the home of the Hereafter is better for those who fear Allah, so will you not use reason? But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers). These were a later generation of the Jews who inherited the Scripture, taking the commodities of this lower life, that is, whatever of this world was put before them; they used the Scripture to grab at worldly gain, and lulled themselves with wishful thinking: It will be forgiven for us. And if an offer like it comes to them, they take it, taking what is forbidden; every aim of theirs was turned toward this world, and they were utterly, deeply heedless of the Hereafter. And this though they had studied the Scripture, memorized it, understood it, and read it; but they squandered it in practice, never living by it, as though they had never studied it at all.

Then He mentions a second group, the opposite of the first: (But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers). Some said this is a community among the Jews; others said it is the community that submitted and believed in Muhammad ﷺ; indeed, it is the nation of Muhammad ﷺ. (Indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers): this shows that the Book guides a person, making him righteous in himself and then a reformer of others; and he cannot set others right before he has set himself right. So this Book sets a person right and brings him into righteousness. (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous): by making him righteous in himself. (But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers): by making him a reformer of others.

Again and again, the Quran is spoken of as an inheritance: (Then We caused to inherit the Book those We have chosen of Our servants). And the Prophet ﷺ used a word drawn from the idea of succession: "I am leaving among you, that which if you hold fast to them," meaning the Book of Allah, the rope of Allah extended from the sky, "and my family - the people of my house," which is the Sunnah, "so look at how you deal with them after me (takhlufūnī)." So the Quran is spoken of in terms of succession and inheritance; that is, Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, set the Quran and its worth on a par with an inheritance, as if a person had inherited a treasure, treasures upon treasures, riches; no, it is greater than any treasure. And succession (khilāfah) likewise: as if he possessed the earth. And as I see it, true kingship is for a person to possess himself, and the Noble Quran helps a person to master himself, his mind, and his desires. That is true kingship: to be king over yourself, ruler over yourself. That is true kingship. So part of the greatness of the Noble Quran is that it is described as an inheritance, a legacy, a heritage, and as succession.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
