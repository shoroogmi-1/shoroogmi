Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

On the Meaning of the Quran Being a Mercy

  • 📅 August 1, 2026
  • ⏱️ 13 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

I always ask myself: what does it mean that Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, always describes the Quran as good tidings and mercy? Things are known by their opposites, and so the opposite of mercy is torment. Allah the Exalted has mentioned psychological torment and pain for those who do not follow the Criterion, the Noble Quran, in more than one place. He said in Surah Al Imran: (And He revealed the Criterion. Indeed, those who disbelieve in the verses of Allah will have a severe punishment). In the exegesis: the severe punishment is the psychological torment by which chests become constricted; that is why He called it a severe punishment, and it is the torment of the soul, its torments and its pains. Also in Surah Al-An'am: (So whoever Allah wants to guide - He expands his breast to [contain] Islam; and whoever He wants to misguide - He makes his breast tight and constricted as though he were climbing into the sky. Thus does Allah place defilement upon those who do not believe). Defilement (al-rijs): some said it is Satan, and this is the more likely interpretation; and some said that al-rijs is torment, meaning the psychological torment of one whose chest is not expanded no matter what he does and no matter what he achieves in this life, for there is psychological torment and pain. As for the one who follows guidance, believes and holds fast to the Noble Quran, the Quran, as our Lord, Glorified and Exalted is He, described it, is a mercy for the believers, and the believer is shown mercy through it.

Yes, I know there are those who take up the Noble Quran for relief from worries, for ease, provision and knowledge, and this is good and fine; but the problem is that it is a temporary goal. That is, if the goal of the reading and the completions is achieved: Praise be to Allah, my debt was paid after I completed the Quran; praise be to Allah, I got a job after I completed the Quran. Fine, then once you have the job, do you leave the Quran? Do you leave the Quran? This is a short-term goal. And if it is not achieved, do you leave the Quran? Do you leave the Quran? I remember reading the story of a woman who used to complete readings of the Quran and join Quran-completion groups, then she stopped; after three months she stopped completing because the goal she wanted had not been achieved, and she said: Nothing happened. This is the problem when we take up the Quran for goals and purposes other than those for which Allah the Exalted revealed it. Allah the Exalted revealed the Quran for the sake of mercy, so that tranquility, gladness of heart, and richness of the heart and the soul may be realized, so that they may blend. It is also a blessing, a cause of provision, and a cause of increasing good deeds, and thereby provisions in this world. But the problem, as happened to this woman, was that over the course of the completions what she wanted did not happen, so she left the Quran and left the completions; then she began to feel distressed, and she said: I lost something in the Quran that was a cause of happiness. So she knew that its purpose is happiness and tranquility, not the gaining of provisions alone, and she went back once more to completing it.

If only the Noble Quran were taken as a cure for the soul and what is in it of desires, doubts, whims, emotions and so on, and taken likewise as a fortification and strengthening of the soul. This is the daily portion of the Quran: a person must have a daily portion of the Quran, reading it or listening to it in any voice he loves, never leaving it nor parting from it; and if he cannot manage a juz' of the Quran, then one page a day. I remember one of the women who had memorized the Quran telling me the story of her memorization. She said that in the beginning she used to attend the Quran, but she spent a whole year reading one juz' every day, and she told me: Before that I was keen on a daily portion of one page, and if there were pressures, work and busyness, then before I slept I would read even a single verse. She told me: I actually did that several times; I was pressed that day with busyness and work, so before I slept I opened the Mushaf and read a verse, then closed it, prayed and slept. So look how Allah the Exalted, when He saw her persevering and continuing in her reading, rewarded her by enabling her to memorize the Noble Quran; and this is the reward. So let no one diminish the right of the Quran, but let him begin his relationship with it by listening or recitation; it is not a condition that he start from its beginning to its end; rather, he may read a particular surah that he loves and repeat it. This is how the relationship with the Quran is in the beginning: with something the person loves, so that the Quran becomes more and more beloved to him afterward.

Now let us reflect on the last page of Surah Al-A'raf, its last face: (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous). Then a few verses later Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, says: (Take what is given freely, enjoin what is good, and turn away from the ignorant). It is mentioned that the Messenger ﷺ, when Gabriel came down with this verse, said: I will turn away from the ignorant one, but something of resentment and anger toward him will remain inside me. So this is the meaning: take what is given freely, that is, take what people's characters allow, take their manners from them as they are and do not demand more; and enjoin what is good, that is, what is right; and turn away from the ignorant. Then the Messenger ﷺ said to Gabriel: So what of anger, O Gabriel? And Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, revealed: (And if an evil suggestion comes to you from Satan, then seek refuge in Allah. Indeed, He is Hearing and Knowing). And the suggestion (al-nazgh) has two interpretations: the first is that it is the inclination toward an evil deed, that is, that a person intends an evil deed and a sin; so if this suggestion and this inclination come to him, let him seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed: I seek refuge in Allah, the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, from Satan, the accursed. And al-nazgh here, according to most exegetes, is also anger, so he seeks refuge in Allah the Exalted from it.

Then He said: (Indeed, those who fear Allah - when an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember [Him] and at once they have insight). The impulse (ṭāʾif) here, they said, is the passing thought, the evil thought, that is, that evil thoughts come to a person. They remember: that is, they remember Allah the Exalted and remember the Home of the Hereafter, so they seek refuge from Satan, the accursed, and the evil thought goes away. And the suggestions of Satan: hit this one, insult that one, do such and such; these are all the suggestions of Satan, and their cure is seeking refuge and remembering: remembering the Hereafter, and remembrance and reminding through the Noble Quran, by a person taking it up so that he holds back from anger, protects his heart, and treats what is in it of resentment in the chest, of anger, of hatred and so on.

Then He said: (But their brothers - the devils increase them in error; then they do not stop short). Their brothers: they are the brothers of the devils among mankind, meaning that Satan comes to him and shows him in his chest: do such and such, do not do such and such, and embellishes it for him, so he responds to him and goes astray. And Ibn Abbas said: Satan agitates him toward sin with agitation; he said: he incites him with desires, doubts, whisperings, worries and griefs that incite him to sin and disobedience with incitement: do this, say that, insult this one, curse that one; so the chest is boiling all the time. (In error; then they do not stop short): He said: they increase them, that is, they supply them with such passing thoughts and ideas. Then they do not stop short: meaning they do not desist from that, they do not tire of sin and disobedience, they do not tire, from one desire to another and from one sin to another, in a continuation in error. This is the meaning.

Then Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, mentioned the psychological cure for everything in the chest of resentment, anger, rancor, desires and whims, and said: (And when you, [O Muhammad], do not bring them a sign, they say, "Why have you not contrived it?" Say, "I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord. This [Quran] is enlightenment from your Lord and guidance and mercy for a people who believe.") And here the point is that the cure by which a person is shown mercy is the Noble Quran, (that you may receive mercy). Allah the Exalted made it insights (baṣāʾir): (enlightenment from your Lord); notice: not a single sight but insights, as though he has more than one sight, more than one eye with which he sees and looks. And He made the purpose of that, and repeated it, that it bears the fruit of guidance and mercy: (and guidance and mercy for a people who believe). Then once again: (So when the Quran is recited, then listen to it and pay attention that you may receive mercy).

Why? This mercy, the scholars said, is a mercy that comes to the believer in this world, and guidance likewise, as in the state of the patient: (Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is those who are the [rightly] guided). They said: the blessings are the praise from the Lord, and it happens in this world, and mercy and guidance happen in this world. And at the beginning of the page He also said: (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous), meaning that whoever takes the Noble Quran as his ally, Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, takes him as His ally, and by His mercy admits him among the righteous, among His righteous servants and allies, who are shown mercy through this Book.

And the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has His own people among mankind." It was said: Who are they, O Messenger of Allah? He said: "The people of the Quran; they are the people of Allah and His chosen ones." And the scholars said in explaining the hadith: Allah the Exalted takes charge of the people of the Quran as a guardian takes charge of his household, or as a shepherd takes charge of his household; He takes charge of them in their worldly affairs and in their affairs of the Hereafter, setting them right, guiding them, and making this Quran a mercy for them. Likewise in Surah Al-A'raf the Exalted said: (He said, "My punishment - I afflict with it whom I will, but My mercy encompasses all things." So I will decree it [especially] for those who fear Me and give zakah and those who believe in Our verses - Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet) to the end of the verse, until He said: (and follow the light which was sent down with him - it is those who will be the successful). So He made success and mercy conditional: He decreed this mercy for whoever follows the Book and the guidance and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the Sunnah; and likewise success in this world and the Hereafter for whoever follows the Noble Quran and holds to it with strength.

Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, also says in Surah Al-A'raf: (And there followed them successors who inherited the Scripture [while] taking the commodities of this lower life and saying, "It will be forgiven for us." And if an offer like it comes to them, they will [again] take it. Was not the covenant of the Scripture taken from them that they would not say about Allah except the truth, and they studied what was in it? And the home of the Hereafter is better for those who fear Allah, so will you not use reason? But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers). These are successors from among the Jews who inherited the Scripture, taking the commodities of this lower life, that is, what is offered to them of this world; they take the commodities of this world by means of this Scripture, and are deluded by wishful thinking: It will be forgiven for us; and if an offer like it comes to them, they take it, which is taking what is forbidden; and all their aims are directed to this world, with their complete and severe heedlessness of the Hereafter, even though they studied the Scripture, memorized it, understood it and read it; but they squandered it in practice, not acting upon it, as though they had never studied it.

Then He mentioned the second group, the opposite of the first: (But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers). It was said: it is a community from among the Jews; and it was said: it is the community that submitted and believed in Muhammad ﷺ; indeed, it is the nation of Muhammad ﷺ. (Indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers): and in this is that this Book guides a person, making him righteous in himself, then making him a reformer of others; and he cannot be a reformer of others when he has not reformed himself. So this Book sets a person right and admits him into righteousness. (Indeed, my protector is Allah, who has sent down the Book; and He is an ally to the righteous): by making him righteous in himself; (But those who hold fast to the Book and establish prayer - indeed, We will not allow to be lost the reward of the reformers): by making him a reformer of others.

And the Quran is always expressed in terms of inheritance: (Then We caused to inherit the Book those We have chosen of Our servants). And the Prophet ﷺ used a word and an expression derived from succession: "I have left among you that which, if you hold fast to it," meaning the Book of Allah, the rope of Allah extended from the heaven, "and my family, the people of my house," which is the Sunnah, "so look at how you will succeed me regarding them." So the Quran is expressed in terms of succession and inheritance; that is, Allah, Glorified and Exalted is He, equated the Quran and its value with inheritance, as though a person inherits a treasure, treasures and wealth; indeed, it is greater than treasures. And likewise succession (khilāfah), as though he owns the earth. And I see that true kingship is for a person to own himself, and the Noble Quran helps a person to own himself, his mind and his desire; this is true kingship: for a person to be king over himself, ruler over himself; this is true kingship. So part of the greatness of the Noble Quran is that it is expressed in terms of inheritance, or of legacy and heritage, and of succession.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
