Branches of the Relationship with the Quran

The Delight of the Art of Listening to the Noble Quran

  • 📅 October 4, 2026
  • ⏱️ 7 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Dr. Saeed Hamza, an expert in Quran memorization, says in his book and courses "The Five Fortresses" that alongside your daily portion of reading, you need a daily portion of listening to the Noble Quran: no less than 30 minutes a day, roughly 41 verses, or say four to five pages, half a juz', a hizb.

Perhaps he drew it from the hadith: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to me: (Recite Surat an-Nisa' to me). So I recited until I reached {So how [will it be] when We bring from every nation a witness and we bring you, [O Muhammad] against these [people] as a witness?} [An-Nisa: 41]. He said: Then either he nudged me, or I turned, and there were his eyes, flowing with tears ﷺ"

And know, may Allah keep you, that nothing touches the soul and stirs the heart and the feelings like listening. Nothing at all. You cannot imagine what wonders it works: renewing your presence of heart, softening you into humility, sharpening your reflection. How many a written reflection was born from listening! The soul tires of any single method, such as reading alone, and grows restless with it.

And don't say: I'm busy! I can barely, barely get through my reading portion, and now you want me to listen as well! No, that's too much.

We can find time for anything, if we truly want it (And if they had intended to go forth, they would have prepared for it [some] preparation). So how do we make it happen?

1- Make it a daily habit; build it into your plan and your daily routine.

2- Start with just a few minutes, then let it grow over time (even a 10-second clip; yes, I'm serious)

3- Speed up the recording! -we'll come to the condition later-.

4- Listen during routine tasks, such as driving.

Don't listen to recordings of the Quran from prayers!

Why not?

Because the imam is human, and prone to slips and lapses: he may swap one verse for another, or forget it altogether. So listen to the Quran only from recorded, recited mushafs that have been checked and reviewed, such as the King Fahd Mushaf, or from apps such as the Wahy app.

See the King Fahd Complex apps here:

qurancomplex.gov.sa/apps/

Don't say: but some YouTube channels cut out the mistakes and fix them.

Don't say that. I once listened to a complete mushaf put out by the Affairs of the Imams and Muezzins of the Grand Mosque in Makkah, and it had 3-4 mistakes the editors had missed -again, mistakes are only natural; the imams are human like us, and my only aim is to alert anyone who cares about listening to the Quran- sometimes the imam slips and brings the Warsh reading into Hafs! And someone who knows nothing of the qira'at may memorize that pronunciation, take it for the Hafs reading, and recite it and repeat it! He may even think it is a refinement of tajweed, a mark of masterful precision! The poor soul has no idea he has made a mistake!

Check: if you suspect the imam has made a mistake, look it up in the mushaf and correct the verse right away! That way you won't pick up the wrong reading -and how many times this habit has saved me!-.

I still remember memorizing some verses from a Taraweeh prayer, recited by one imam in a sweet voice, and then discovering, months later, that there was a mistake in what I had memorized. I wondered where the mistake had come from, and found it was the very clip I had been listening to and memorizing from: he slipped, so I slipped, and I memorized the slip! And the mistake took root in my memory! Just imagine the trouble of correcting it: to this day I am still struggling with it, and sometimes I slip again, and I say: if only I had heeded the advice from the start: listen only to recorded mushafs.

The pull of prayer recordings is their soaring spirituality, boundless and endless, and a beauty of voice you will never find in a recorded mushaf. Listen to them -there's no harm in it- I do so myself at times, but I always find mistakes -I said always, not rarely or sometimes, that is, when listening to a whole surah of medium or long length, apart from a few, and those from the most renowned for precision-.!

But train your ear on the precise, recorded mushaf.

Should I speed up the recording?

Yes. I have read that speeding up Quran recordings is permissible, on condition that the letters and words do not run into one another, or blur into a babble from which nothing can be understood.

Don't you use hadr, rapid recitation, when you read? Well, this is hadr for listening.

All right, but there's a problem!

Go on!

Sometimes my mind wanders, I get drowsy, and I have no idea what the reciter is saying?

Pay no mind to the whisperings of Satan, and keep listening even if you didn't focus, reflect, or understand. Let's leave perfectionism aside! And listen to what Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal said:

He said: I heard Abdullah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal say: I heard my father say: I saw the Lord of Might, the Mighty and Majestic, in a dream, and I said: O Lord, what is the best thing by which those who seek nearness to You draw near? He said: My speech, O Ahmad. He said: I said: O Lord, with understanding or without understanding? He said: With understanding and without understanding.

(Manaqib al-Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn al-Jawzi)

Among the courtesies of listening to the Quran: after listening to poems, chants, and the like, the righteous predecessors would close with a recitation of the Quran, and some of them would recite both before and after; perhaps the wisdom in this is to clear away heedlessness, revive the heart, and bring it back to faith. (See al-Ghazali's Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din, the chapter on Listening)

Should my listening portion be different from my reading portion?

Whatever suits you!

It is better for your listening portion to differ from your reading portion, so that if you stop at a certain minute, you pick up from there the next day.

And keep your reading portion separate, so that you have two completions running: one by reading and one by listening.

And if you would rather have the one complete the other, there is room for that too!

There is a wonderful idea -though hard to put into practice- which is to listen while reading some tafsir at the same time. For that I recommend the Wahy app (31 encyclopedias of tafsir, working offline and free of ads. Look up how to download the books and the audio)

Don't set yourself ideal, perfect conditions, for they are exactly what keeps you putting off listening to the Quran: insisting, for instance, on listening only to the mujawwad mushaf, or only to a certain reciter whose tajweed is hard to follow, or only with full focus and attention, or only slowly at 1x speed. Every one of these has stood between you and finishing your listening portion. So start now, without these conditions. Put it into practice this very hour; don't say tomorrow, because you will forget, and a deed that follows on the heels of a moving word is the one that lasts!

For those who memorize: one tried-and-tested way of correcting your recitation is to listen and move your lips along without speaking aloud. The aim is to catch your own mistakes in memorization, recitation, and tajweed. If you recite aloud with the reciter, you may not hear him clearly, and you may not notice your own mistake, because your voice drowns out his. I have found this to be the best way yet, after a teacher, to catch mistakes in recitation and correct them.

If this has benefited you, then remember the writer of these words in your good prayers.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
