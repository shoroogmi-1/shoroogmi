Branches of the Relationship with the Quran

What Remains for a Person in His Darkest Hour?

  • 📅 August 16, 2026
  • ⏱️ 4 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

So I wanted to share it, along with a few thoughts of my own:

Dr. Muhammad Hamza had memorized the Quran before he was thrown into prison, where the prisoners were forbidden to speak. He says he kept the Quran and taught it to his fellow prisoners by writing out each page in soap on black clothing, where the white lather shows up clearly against the dark, as it would not on colored clothes. With him in the cell was the writer and television screenwriter Adnan al-Zira'i, who memorized ten parts of the Book of Allah this way before he was martyred.

He went on to tell how, in the last years of torture, his nerves were shattered and he hovered near death and sickness, so that reviewing the Quran in his head, from memory, grew slow: al-Baqarah, which used to take him half an hour, now took four or five. He felt his memory weakening, failing. And yet, he says, he was determined to keep his memory working and keep reviewing his daily portion, however much time and effort it cost him.

And when he reached the words of the Exalted ﴿And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits, but give good tidings to the patient, (155) Who, when disaster strikes them, say, "Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return." (156) Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is those who are the [rightly] guided. (157) [Al-Baqarah: 155-157]

the jailer came, called out his name, and gave him the good news of his release; and he walked out of prison after six lean years spent within its walls.

What amazes me is that this man was a surgeon, healing the sick and performing operations, and at the same time a teacher of the Quran who carried it by heart.

I don't know what to say as I listen to his story! Only that, while listening, I grasped one of the meanings of life and existence: when a person is cut off from everything, besieged by time and assailed from every side, denied his food and drink, his speech and his people, what remains with him!? Nothing but sorrows, and a memory that keeps replaying its pains. Nothing, unless he carries the Quran by heart. As he reviews his daily portion from memory, the words of the Quran crowd out the words of hatred, insult and abuse that are hurled at him, so he does not hurt, and does not much care what is aimed at him. That is why painful memories slip away from him easily, while his thoughts and mind are absorbed in going over the Quran. Memory, if you do not wear it out with memorizing and reviewing, will wear you out by memorizing sorrows and fears.

That is when I recognized the root of a very modern affliction (overthinking): it is a memory emptied of its daily review of the Quran, so it fills itself with replaying the past, fretting over the future, chewing the same thoughts over and over. I have even come across the stories of many who, after memorizing the Quran and tending it through review and recitation, reported an astonishing thing: the intrusive whispers, the thoughts, the negative feelings vanished completely!

Then I knew what remains for a person in his darkest hour: his own breast remains with him, and all he has entrusted to it of verses and recitations that heal him, tend to him, and bless his years. So what is left for the one who has not yet memorized it! And what is he waiting for!?

As for those who object (understanding matters more than memorizing), I say: there are mathematical laws you cannot understand until you have repeated them and learned them by heart; with memorizing comes understanding. And so it is with the Quran.

I remember a woman who wrote a post on Facebook: she had resolved to memorize the Quran, and a friend asked her, "Why memorize it? Reflecting on it matters more than memorizing it." And now, she wrote, the years have gone by, and I have neither memorized it nor reflected on it.

So, my friend, no companion will stay by your side in hardship but the Quran. It is the springtime and it is the rain; learn it by heart, and you will find in it companionship and joy. I ask Allah the Exalted to make the Quran the springtime of our hearts, and to make memorizing it easy for me and for you, in pardon and well-being, in peace and safety.

From the book (Ya Bushra Hadha Ghulam)

— Shoroog Almehmadi
