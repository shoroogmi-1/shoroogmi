Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

Surat al-Hadid Unfolded, for Those Who Seek More

  • 📅 August 21, 2026
  • ⏱️ 27 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

(Whatever is in the heavens and earth exalts Allah, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Wise (1) His is the dominion of the heavens and earth. He gives life and causes death, and He is over all things competent (2) He is the First and the Last, the Ascendant and the Intimate, and He is, of all things, Knowing (3) It is He who created the heavens and earth in six days and then established Himself above the Throne. He knows what penetrates into the earth and what emerges from it and what descends from the heaven and what ascends therein; and He is with you wherever you are. And Allah, of what you do, is Seeing (4) His is the dominion of the heavens and earth. And to Allah are returned [all] matters (5) He causes the night to enter the day and causes the day to enter the night, and he is Knowing of that within the breasts (6) (Al-Hadid)

These verses gather up, in their names and attributes, everything the rest of Surat al-Hadid will unfold!

Attributes of power: His is the dominion of the heavens and earth. He gives life and causes death, and He is over all things competent

Attributes of knowledge and full awareness: He is the First and the Last, the First and the Last, the Ascendant and the Intimate, and He is, of all things, Knowing

And His knowledge of the finest details of all that lies on the surface and in the depths of earth and sky

Once you know this, the theme of the surah takes shape and shines clear: being mindful that Allah watches, in private and in public, is what gives rise to awe and humility (and He is with you wherever you are).

And to Allah are returned [all] matters: to Allah are raised the decrees, the deeds, and the states, and He looks into their affairs and changes them; He guides those who have strayed, forgives those who have sinned, and brings relief to those in distress.

First come the verses of faith in Allah and His Messenger and of following the revealed Book, and only then the verses of the conquest! As if to say: there is no conquest and no victory without obedience to Allah, to His Messenger, and to those in authority, scholars and rulers alike (And why do you not believe in Allah while the Messenger invites you to believe in your Lord and He has taken your covenant, if you should [truly] be believers (8) It is He who sends down upon His Servant [Muhammad] verses of clear evidence that He may bring you out from darknesses into the light. And indeed, Allah is to you Kind and Merciful (9) And why do you not spend in the cause of Allah while to Allah belongs the heritage of the heavens and the earth? Not equal among you are those who spent before the conquest [of Makkah] and fought [and those who did so after it]. Those are greater in degree than they who spent afterwards and fought. But to all Allah has promised the best [reward]. And Allah, with what you do, is Acquainted (10) Who is it that would loan Allah a goodly loan so He will multiply it for him and he will have a noble reward (11) (Al-Hadid)

The verse makes clear that whoever spends in times of calamity and hardship stands firmer in his faith when the world later throws its doors open to him,

for protection from being seduced by the world comes only through spending, before the conquest and after it. And how many have failed this test: they pass in patience, and fail in gratitude.

For whoever spends before the conquest and after it, to him the two states are one; he spends certain that Allah will repay him with something better, and certain of victory and its spoils. The other, weaker in certainty, holds back and clutches tight, watching the news and the turn of events, and only when the conquest comes does he spend!

And so spending is the test of the ṣiddīqūn, the truthful ones

Indeed, the men who practice charity and the women who practice charity and [they who] have loaned Allah a goodly loan - it will be multiplied for them, and they will have a noble reward (18) And those who have believed in Allah and His messengers - those are [in the ranks of] the supporters of truth and the martyrs, with their Lord. For them is their reward and their light. But those who have disbelieved and denied Our verses - those are the companions of Hellfire (19) (Al-Hadid)

He said al-muṣṣaddiqīn, not al-mutaṣaddiqīn, and there is a subtle meaning in this: the one who gives charity is one who believes (muṣaddiq) that Allah will replace it for him with something better and multiply it for him, and it is this that raises him, in time, to the rank of the ṣiddīqūn; for the ṣiddīq is one who believes Allah and His Messenger and their promises, and acts accordingly.

(On the Day you see the believing men and believing women, their light proceeding before them and on their right, [it will be said], "Your good tidings today are [of] gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein you will abide eternally." That is what is the great attainment (12) On the [same] Day the hypocrite men and hypocrite women will say to those who believed, "Wait for us that we may acquire some of your light." It will be said, "Go back behind you and seek light." And a wall will be placed between them with a door, its interior containing mercy, but on the outside of it is torment (13) The hypocrites will call to the believers, "Were we not with you?" They will say, "Yes, but you afflicted yourselves and awaited [misfortune for us] and doubted, and wishful thinking deluded you until there came the command of Allah. And the Deceiver deceived you concerning Allah (14) So today no ransom will be taken from you or from those who disbelieved. Your refuge is the Fire. It is most worthy of you, and wretched is the destination (15) (Al-Hadid)

The exegetes say: when the believers are on the Path alongside the hypocrites, both groups have light, but the hypocrite's light goes out; for it was an outward light, all surface and no substance. He never made it real in this world, so it has no reality in the Hereafter. That is when the believers pray (they will say, "Our Lord, perfect for us our light") (At-Tahrim)

So whoever is not given light by Allah from the Quran and the Sunnah in this world will have neither light nor share in the Hereafter (And he to whom Allah has not granted light - for him there is no light (40) (An-Nur)

And those who hold fastest to the Quran and the Sunnah will have the greatest light on the Day of Resurrection.

You afflicted yourselves (fatantum anfusakum): like the trial (fitnah) of iron when it is put to the fire, which separates the dross from the pure; and so it is with hearts when they are put to the light of the Quran: ambiguities are laid before them, and some swerve, while others Allah guides; all this so that Allah may know who fears Him unseen.

You afflicted yourselves with doubts, then lay in wait with your desires, wandered bewildered in the darknesses and took them for many colors, when their color is one and only their paths are many; and wishful thinking deluded you until you were among the dead. And because you forgot the Hereafter, the Fire is your mawlā, your patron; it was their patron already in this world, for they took it as their ally through their desires (the Fire is surrounded by desires). And from the contrast we understand that Paradise took the believer as its ally (We [angels] were your allies in worldly life and [are so] in the Hereafter. And you will have therein whatever your souls desire, and you will have therein whatever you request [or wish] (31) (Fussilat)

For Paradise is surrounded by hardships: they fasted from their desires in this world, and in return were given whatever their souls desire in Paradise; reward from your Lord, [a generous] gift [made due by] account.

Should the believer not race against time to perfect his light, and never let the world seize him and make him forget the Hereafter?

(Has the time not come for those who have believed that their hearts should become humbly submissive at the remembrance of Allah and what has come down of the truth? And let them not be like those who were given the Scripture before, and a long period passed over them, so their hearts hardened; and many of them are defiantly disobedient (16) Know that Allah gives life to the earth after its lifelessness. We have made clear to you the signs; perhaps you will understand (17) (Al-Hadid)

(Has the time not come): as though the believer sat waiting, watching for humility to descend upon him from the sky, forever putting off his heart's ascent up the stairways of faith; and so his Lord addresses him with the gentlest words and the most tender companionship: Is now not the time for humility, now that the verse has fallen upon your ears, O My servant? And when that seemed hard to him and weighed on him — How can I reach a lofty station that belongs to the scholars, when I have no knowledge!? (Only those fear Allah, from among His servants, who have knowledge) — He answered him with ease and gentleness (that their hearts should become humbly submissive at the remembrance of Allah and what has come down of the truth): let his heart be softened by the Quran. For He has named both the disease and the cure: the disease is hardness, and its cure is the Quran, which softens the heart until it is humbled.

After gentleness and encouragement, He turned to warning, cautioning whoever slips back into postponing humility not to be like the People of the Book, over whom a long time passed without a Book, so their hearts hardened,

And I think the People of the Book meant here are the Jews, for theirs are the hardened hearts (Then your hearts became hardened after that, being like stones..)

At the close of Surat al-Hadid, He speaks of the opposite of hardness: the tenderness found in Christianity. Allah forbids resembling the People of the Book even in the states of their hearts: neither hardness of heart like the Jews, nor the excessive tenderness that kept the Christians from upholding the prescribed punishments -as will be explained in its place-.

(Know that Allah gives life to the earth after its lifelessness): so that you may come to know that He is able to revive dead hearts through His remembrance; so do not think it far-fetched, nor that the astray and the disbeliever may be guided until he becomes among the most God-fearing of people.

(We have made clear to you the signs; perhaps you will understand): here He has made plain to you the heart of the matter, the fear of Allah; that the fruit of knowledge is awe; and that there is nothing obscure or hard in it. So is there anyone of sense who will take these words to heart?

And the verse shows that Allah does not approve disbelief for His servants, nor does He approve for His believing servant abasement, or faith that is meager and weak; so He wished to stir his heart to seek more faith and climb its stairways, and not settle for its lower rank (And you will be superior if you are [true] believers) (Al Imran).

And on either side of the verse on the humbling of the heart He set two verses about the world! Above it and below it, on the right-hand page and on the left, like a flowing river that floods it and hems it in: (and wishful thinking deluded you…) on the right-hand page, (Know that the life of this world is but amusement and diversion and adornment..) on the left.

(Know that the life of this world is but amusement and diversion and adornment and boasting to one another and competition in increase of wealth and children - like the example of a rain whose [resulting] plant growth pleases the tillers; then it dries and you see it turned yellow; then it becomes [scattered] debris. And in the Hereafter is severe punishment and forgiveness from Allah and approval. And what is the worldly life except the enjoyment of delusion (20) Race toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden whose width is like the width of the heavens and earth, prepared for those who believed in Allah and His messengers. That is the bounty of Allah which He gives to whom He wills, and Allah is the possessor of great bounty (21) (Al-Hadid)

He turns from awe and humility, in which time is held still and slowed, to making clear why they fail to attain such states: their rush after the world and their race to keep up with it. The whole Quran describes the world in brief: amusement and diversion; except here in Surat al-Hadid, where He describes it at greater length (amusement and diversion and adornment and boasting to one another and competition in increase of wealth and children). He is telling us that what hardens the heart and drives it far from the remembrance of Allah is preoccupation with wealth and children, the very trial that turns the heart away from the Quran and remembrance (And know that your properties and your children are but a trial and that Allah has with Him a great reward (28) (Al-Anfal). So He added two more pieces of knowledge to their knowledge; and as for the competing for more, and the scattering of the heart, this is what distracted them from remembering the Hereafter and from worship (Competition in [worldly] increase diverts you until you visit the graveyards). Then came the counsel in full (O you who have believed, let not your wealth and your children divert you from remembrance of Allah. And whoever does that - then those are the losers (9) (Al-Munafiqun)

(O you who have believed, when you encounter a company [from the enemy forces], stand firm and remember Allah much that you may be successful (45)

If this fighter, amid war and blood and severed heads, was commanded to remember Allah, and not to let the sight of death distract him from remembrance! If the firmness of his footing and his victory lie in remembrance! Had Allah exempted anyone from remembrance, He would have exempted the fighter!

So what of one who is busy buying and trading?

(And when the prayer has been concluded, disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah often that you may succeed (10)

But when they saw a transaction or a diversion, [O Muhammad], they rushed to it and left you standing. Say, "What is with Allah is better than diversion and than a transaction, and Allah is the best of providers" (11) (Al-Jumu'ah) And He praised a people whom the world did not distract from His remembrance ([Are] men whom neither commerce nor sale distracts from the remembrance of Allah and performance of prayer and giving of zakah. They fear a Day in which the hearts and eyes will [fearfully] turn about (37) (An-Nur) Once again He shows us that what brings fear of Allah unseen, and awe of Him, is remembering Him with the heart, the tongue, and deeds.

There, when time stands still and all movement hushes: if a verse has stirred your heart and set longing loose in it, stop! Repeat it until the tears flow and the soul climbs the stairways of humility. And that last will never come to one who brushes past a verse that stirred his heart, his only concern reaching the end of the surah. What harm would it do you to fall behind in your daily portion, because a verse softened your heart? That is better for you than heaping up gold and silver in time-worn crucibles.

Part of the deep understanding of the righteous was that when their eyes wept from awe, they would wipe away their tears and spread them over the whole face, so that no part of the face would be touched by the Fire! (Sifat al-Safwah).

A dead heart brought to life by humility, and a heart once alive, killed by the world (like the example of a rain whose [resulting] plant growth pleases the tillers; then it dries and you see it turned yellow; then it becomes [scattered] debris). The Quran had been its springtime; then, when the world broke loose upon it, it made it forget the Quran and the night prayer, and it slid back into its first state of hardness and heedlessness, and the calamity returned to it.

(No disaster strikes upon the earth or among yourselves except that it is in a register before We bring it into being - indeed that, for Allah, is easy (22) In order that you not despair over what has eluded you and not exult [in pride] over what He has given you. And Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful (23) [Those] who are stingy and enjoin upon people stinginess. And whoever turns away - then indeed, Allah is the Free of need, the Praiseworthy (24) (Al-Hadid)

The one who reflects will notice that on the right-hand page, facing this verse on the left-hand page, stands (Know that Allah gives life to the earth after its lifelessness…)

(This Ayah indicates that He brings subtleness to hearts after they have become hard, guides the confused after they were led astray, and relieves hardships after they have intensified) (Ibn Kathir)

Decreeing the calamity is easy for Him, and lifting it is easy for Him. And having made clear that the hard is made easy, He warned of the pitfalls of relief!

Swaggering in one's gait and being deluded by the world, until it makes one forget that it is a trial; and delusion and boasting lead to stinginess in spending, so they withhold their wealth, and use their standing to bid people to be stingy! He has reached the very extreme of miserliness and greed

that he bids people to be stingy! And this is, beyond doubt, a trait of the hypocrite (They enjoin what is wrong).

Among the wisdoms of adversity is that it expels the dross and softens the hardened heart (Then why, when Our punishment came to them, did they not humble themselves? But their hearts became hardened) (Al-An'am). So whoever hardens his heart in calamity only adds to his own adversity; and whoever makes things hard, Allah makes them hard for him.

(We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people, and so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen. Indeed, Allah is Powerful and Exalted in Might (25) And We have already sent Noah and Abraham and placed in their descendants prophethood and scripture; and among them is he who is guided, but many of them are defiantly disobedient (26) Al-Hadid

Whoever reflects on the Quran will notice that verses commanding justice are followed at once by mention of the messengers and the scriptures, a sign that the Shariah is the surest way to justice in the self and in society. So in An-Nisa: (..be persistently standing firm in justice…) followed by (believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Book that He sent down upon His Messenger..); and in Al-An'am: (And when you testify, be just, even if [it concerns] a near relative…) followed by (And, [moreover], this is My path, which is straight, so follow it..); and the pattern recurs in Surat al-Hadid as well.

(and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice): He paired the Book with justice. Whoever holds fast to the Book upholds justice even against himself, and so becomes just in his character, balanced in his temperament, moderate in all his affairs, his inner self matching his outer, with nothing between them at odds: just! (O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives. Whether one is rich or poor, Allah is more worthy of both. So follow not [personal] inclination, lest you not be just. And if you distort [your testimony] or refuse [to give it], then indeed Allah is ever, with what you do, Acquainted (135) (An-Nisa) He called them by the name of faith, for nothing befits the believer but justice toward himself and his society; it does not befit him to lean with his whims, or to turn away from the truth once it has shown itself to him.

(that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice): He left the object unstated (maintain justice in what?) so that it would be unrestricted and all-embracing, reaching even their dealings with tiny ants and blades of grass. And Allah revealed to a prophet: “Wouldn't it have been sufficient to burn a single ant?” And in fiqh it is disliked to trample green meadows underfoot or under one's mount without need.

(And Allah is greater in might) (An-Nisa): severe in might in punishment and retribution; and it also includes that His might is overwhelming in reviving hearts that have rusted over. When the Chosen One, peace and blessings be upon him, saw hearts turned from disbelief to faith and back, his most frequent oath became “No, by Him Who turns the hearts”, for he had seen that power with his own eyes after knowing of it. His might is overwhelming even in guidance and mercy: so that all creation may turn to Him alone for guidance, for He alone is able to grant it, and He is the Subjugator above His servants; and so that they do not rely on their educational and psychological methods alone, even though these are means that ought to be taken.

(and so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen): if you uphold the religion, you have supported Allah and His messengers, and He tied this to the unseen! For truly upholding the religion happens in the unseen, in secret and within, where no one sees “O Allah, cause me to fear You in secret and in public”

And the greatest victory a believer can win in the unseen is to fear Allah and shed tears in solitude, far from people

(Has the time not come for those who have believed that their hearts should become humbly submissive at the remembrance of Allah)

And to command himself to do right and forbid himself from wrong, and only then his society; not the other way round!

(And We sent down iron): When hearts thought change beyond them, and souls could not imagine being turned from their natures, and some despaired of ever being set right and turned away, He reminded them with a fiery parable: the heart is like iron. Does iron not, when smelted in fire, melt and change its properties? Out of it come pots and vessels, after it had nearly rusted and lost its worth (And We certainly gave David from Us bounty. [We said], "O mountains, repeat [Our] praises with him, and the birds [as well]." And We made pliable for him iron (10) [Commanding him], "Make full coats of mail and calculate [precisely] the links, and work [all of you] righteousness. Indeed I, of what you do, am Seeing" (11) (Saba)

Truly, the One who softened iron is able, through the Quran, to soften hearts: guiding them after they have strayed, humbling them after they have grown coarse, uniting them after division, and setting right the heart of one who has despaired and lost all hope of setting himself right.

(And We made pliable): He attributed the softening of iron to Himself, the Exalted, using the “We” of majesty; for iron is fiercely hard, and none can soften it but its Creator. Just as He softened iron for David, peace be upon him, He is able to soften hearts and set souls right by His bounty, mercy, and generosity.

I had wondered why Allah did not mention David, peace be upon him, in Surat al-Hadid!? Why mention Noah and Abraham, peace be upon them, instead? Perhaps the answer is that the iron here is figurative, not material: the softening of the heart, faith, and humility through constant mindfulness (and He is with you wherever you are).

Spiritual struggle, discipline, and the purifying of hearts, if not guided by the Quran and the Sunnah, leave no mark.

That is why, right after this, He warned against innovation, extremism, and excessive rigor in matters of faith, as the Christians did: they had states of faith, and softness and tenderness in their hearts, but they went to extremes and innovated, and so drifted from the straight path

(And We have already sent Noah and Abraham and placed in their descendants prophethood and scripture; and among them is he who is guided, but many of them are defiantly disobedient (26) Then We sent following their footsteps Our messengers and followed [them] with Jesus, the son of Mary, and gave him the Gospel. And We placed in the hearts of those who followed him compassion and mercy and monasticism, which they innovated; We did not prescribe it for them except [that they did so] seeking the approval of Allah. But they did not observe it with due observance. So We gave the ones who believed among them their reward, but many of them are defiantly disobedient (27) (Al-Hadid)

So the believer does not resemble the People of the Book even in the states of their hearts: he is not hard-hearted like the Jews, nor a monk like the Christians, nor like them in a softness so excessive that they neglected the prescribed punishments and failed to uphold justice among people. And He said in the verse of the prescribed punishment (and do not be taken by pity for them in the religion of Allah, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day (2) (An-Nur)

Do not be like the Christians, for whom religion is love without fear, tolerance without limits, and a tenderness that led them to make the forbidden lawful, until wrongdoing spread among them because they had abandoned the prescribed limits.

(and monasticism, which they innovated; We did not prescribe it for them except [that they did so] seeking the approval of Allah. But they did not observe it with due observance): perhaps the meaning is: they did not observe what Allah had made binding on them, so how could they observe what they had made binding on themselves?

One whose soul does not stand upright on the command of Allah and His messengers can never make it stand upright by himself; he is far too weak and powerless for that.

But whoever surrenders himself to Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, obeying them without adding to or falling short of what he follows, it becomes a right upon Allah to guide him to what is most upright, to give him victory over his whims, and to lift him above himself.

And the Christians, even when they innovated, did not follow what they innovated! They neither followed Allah and the messengers, nor stood firm on what they had invented; so how would Allah guide the defiantly disobedient people? Nor did the Christians observe Allah's approval in His Oneness, so they disbelieved and said that Allah is Christ, the son of Mary (And He does not approve for His servants disbelief); had they observed His approval, they would not have disbelieved. And this is a rebuttal to the innovating Sufis who claimed love and carried it to extremes (Say, [O Muhammad], "If you should love Allah, then follow me, [so] Allah will love you") — if you truly claim love of the Divine, why have you turned aside from the guidance of Muhammad, and invented words of remembrance and sayings for which Allah sent down no authority, and forced yourselves into states that have no footing in Islam?!

(O you who have believed, fear Allah and believe in His Messenger; He will [then] give you a double portion of His mercy and make for you a light by which you will walk and forgive you; and Allah is Forgiving and Merciful (28) (Al-Hadid)

Throughout Surat al-Hadid it is (believe in Allah and His Messenger), except here, where He says (fear Allah): by being mindful of Him and in awe of Him in the unseen. (He will [then] give you a double portion of His mercy and make for you a light by which you will walk): the reward matches the deed. Because they were mindful of Allah in every state, He gave them a light like a companion walking beside them, going with them everywhere, setting them aright, granting them success, helping them and giving them victory by it. Mercy above the servant, and light at his sides, like a companion who never leaves the one who has kept company with the Quran. After such a fortress, how could the devils of mankind and jinn have any power over one guarded and cared for by his Lord like this?!

The believer who has fortified himself with the light of the Quran, and been mindful of Allah in private and in public, has toiled at that mindfulness and taken pains to hold that state: watching for Allah and knowing that He sees in every place (and He is with you wherever you are); and his reward is that a double portion of mercy goes with him, and light, wherever he is

And the verse from Surat Al-An'am explains it (And is one who was dead and We gave him life and made for him light by which to walk among the people like one who is in darkness, never to emerge therefrom? (122)

He was dead, and He gave him life through the Quran (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar); and reviving the heart from heedlessness is part of Allah's mercy (So observe the effects of the mercy of Allah - how He gives life to the earth after its lifelessness) (Ar-Rum), which is why it fits His words (He will [then] give you a double portion of His mercy)

(and made for him light by which to walk among the people) matches (and make for you a light by which you will walk)

So the meaning here in Al-Hadid is a light by which you walk within yourselves and among people

"He mentioned the granting of light to the one who was dead, and said: by which to walk among the people, that is, it stays with him however he turns; and He said: among the people, as an indication that it lights the way both for himself and for others among the people; so He made clear that the believer's benefit is not confined to himself" (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar - from the tafsir of Al-An'am)

[This is] so that the People of the Scripture may know that they are not able [to obtain] anything from the bounty of Allah and that [all] bounty is in the hand of Allah; He gives it to whom He wills. And Allah is the possessor of great bounty (29)

He opened the surah with the names and attributes of knowledge and power, and unfolded their details throughout it: that He is able to revive hearts after their death through the Quran, and to soften them with humility; that humility springs from being mindful of Allah and knowing that He is with His servant (and He is with you wherever you are), and also from not being deluded by the world, so that it does not distract him from the remembrance of Allah and his daily portion of the Quran. And He sealed it with this knowledge: that the bounty of guidance and of the Book is in the hand of Allah; He gives it to whom He wills and withholds it from whom He wills, for He is the All-Powerful, the All-Knowing, the possessor of great bounty.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
