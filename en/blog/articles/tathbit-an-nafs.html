Encouraging the Self

  • 📅 July 24, 2026
  • ⏱️ 2 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Encouraging the self: it appears in His words (And the example of those who spend their wealth seeking means to the approval of Allah and assuring [reward for] themselves)

Tathbīt al-nafs, making the self firm: encouraging it to give, and strengthening it.

And I leave you to dwell with the words of Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him:

(This is the parable of one whose spending springs from sincerity and truthfulness: seeking His pleasure, glory be to Him, is sincerity, and making firm from within is truthfulness in giving. For whoever spends meets two banes as he spends; if he escapes them both, his likeness is the one mentioned in this verse:

The first: that he seeks by his spending praise or acclaim, or some worldly aim of his, and this is the state of most who spend.

The second bane: the weakness of his soul in giving, its holding back and its wavering: should he do it or not?

The first bane is removed by seeking the pleasure of Allah,

and the second bane is removed by making firm; for making the soul firm means encouraging it, strengthening it, and urging it forward to give)

Righteous deeds, too, have inner motives, all turned toward a single aim: Allah the Exalted. And a person nurtures his own inner motives, and encourages himself by himself

"The merit of spending out of firmness from within oneself: such spending is driven by an inner motive, not by someone else's urging or advice, as the Exalted says: wa tathbītan min anfusihim (and assuring [reward for] themselves)" al-Tafsir al-Muharrar

And that he gives with a generous soul and a love of giving, not grudgingly; for the hypocrite's motives rest on others (istawqada nāran, “kindled a fire”: the hypocrite relies on others in his faith and his deeds, which is why He said istawqada, “sought to kindle,” and not awqada, “kindled”; for he looks to others to help him kindle his faith) (and that they do not spend except while they are unwilling)

— Shoroog Almehmadi
