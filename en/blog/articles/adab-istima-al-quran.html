Branches of the Relationship with the Quran

Taste and Courtesy in Listening to the Quran

  • 📅 August 30, 2026
  • ⏱️ 4 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

She tells me she was lying on a sickbed, and that the woman beside her turned up the Bluetooth volume with Surah Al-Baqarah and ruqyah, and that for two or three days she could not sleep because of it.

I told her: this woman has no sense of taste! And perhaps she never heard of his ﷺ way of lowering his voice with the Quran for fear of waking his family.

And like someone who turns up the Quran at a traffic light and opens the window, forgetting that among people are those sick with ear and eye infections and other ailments affected by sharp, loud sound, and those in need, and the elderly.

“And it has been narrated to us that the Prophet ﷺ heard a man reciting aloud, so he called out to him: O so-and-so, make Allah hear, and do not make me hear” (Qut al-Qulub)

For this Quran, no one is more in need of its admonitions and warnings than you, O listener and reciter. As for people, they have a Lord who guides them, so what have you to do with them? Make yourself hear, and set the volume to your own measure, not to the measure of the creation.

(And do not recite your prayer too loudly or too quietly, but seek a way between that (110) (Al-Isra)

The limit of reciting aloud is that you make your own ears hear. As for reciting silently, it is that you do not hear yourself, a mere murmur of the lips. And it used to be said: whatever your ear hears is not reciting quietly. “He has not recited quietly who makes his own ears hear” (al-Tabari)

“And I was told of a man from among the people of goodness who said: I was reciting Surah Ta-Ha before dawn in a room of mine that overlooked the street, and when I finished it I dozed off afterward, and I saw a figure come down from the sky with a white page in his hand, and he spread it out before me, and in it was Surah Ta-Ha, and under every word ten good deeds were recorded, except one word, for I saw in its place an erasure and saw nothing under it. That grieved me, so I said: By Allah, I did recite this word, and I see no reward for it, nor do I see it recorded. The figure said: You are right, you recited it and we wrote it for you, except that we heard a caller calling: Erase it and drop its reward, so we erased it. So I wept in my sleep and said: Why did you do that? They said: A man passed by, and you raised your voice with it for his sake, so we erased it”(Qut al-Qulub)

Linguists say: it is impossible for words alone to express everything in the mind.

So if I talk with you now and write, there are many feelings and thoughts that these words of mine cannot convey to you now; for language is not a pipe that carries everything.

Here; I paused the podcast and started reflecting on his ﷺ saying “When one of you gets up to pray at night, let him use the siwak, for when one of you recites in his prayer, an angel places its mouth on his mouth, and nothing comes out of his mouth except that it enters the mouth of the angel” (its narrators are trustworthy)

As if I understood why the angel places its mouth on the reciter's mouth when the Quran is recited? Why doesn't it sit away from him, for example?! Wouldn't that be enough?

I think, and the knowledge is with Allah; because along with the verses it takes the reciter's thoughts, feelings, intentions, supplications, and requests! The matter does not stop at the bare taking of letters, and if it were a bare taking of letters, how would the sincere be known from the show-off? So it takes everything from the reciter, his intentions, thoughts, and feelings during the recitation -Allah knows best-.

(And [by] a witness and what is witnessed) Among the meanings I reflected on in this verse is that the witness and what is witnessed may be meant as the Quran, for it is a witness for you or against you; witnessed in this world, the angels and the believers witness it, and witnessed in the Hereafter, on the day it is said to the reciter of the Quran: recite and rise.

So make it a witness for you, not against you, and do not be a cause of harming people and the sick with it. So observe public taste and courtesy, and let us hold to sincerity, for it is carried along with our recitation and our listening - and I am the first of those in need of this admonition. O Allah, forgive us, have mercy on us, and grant us sincerity from Yourself-.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
