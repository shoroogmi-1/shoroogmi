Good Tidings in Times of Hardship

  • 📅 September 10, 2026
  • ⏱️ 2 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

You see this idea everywhere: that good tidings will not come while your heart is full of fear (And when the fright had left Abraham and the good tidings had reached him)

How glad I am of anyone who reflects on the Quran! But sound reflection is built on a sound understanding of the Quran.

For the verse means this: Abraham (peace be upon him) felt apprehension toward his guests; then they told him they were angels sent to the people of Lot; then they gave him the good tidings of Isaac and Jacob (peace be upon them)

So the meaning is: the fright left him once they had given him the good tidings of Isaac and Jacob.

Besides, feelings are not something we are commanded or forbidden; the believer is not called to account for them.

In fact, good tidings come most often in times of hardship, fear, pain and grief. The Quran is full of this, like the good tidings given to the Messenger ﷺ and the believers in their hour of fear (the Battle of Badr), when they were reinforced with angels. For in times of fear a person needs good tidings to steady his heart far more than he does in times of ease!

Read these verses slowly; don't let your eye skim past them. You will see that good tidings come in the hour of fear, to make hearts steadfast and to plant feet firmly:

([Remember] when you asked help of your Lord, and He answered you, "Indeed, I will reinforce you with a thousand from the angels, following one another" (9) And Allah made it not but good tidings and so that your hearts would be assured thereby. And victory is not but from Allah. Indeed, Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise (10) [Remember] when He overwhelmed you with drowsiness [giving] security from Him and sent down upon you from the sky, rain by which to purify you and remove from you the evil [suggestions] of Satan and to make steadfast your hearts and plant firmly thereby your feet (11) (Al-Anfal)

If that understanding were sound, the good tidings would have been withheld from Abraham (peace be upon him), for he felt from them apprehension!

You were afraid? Then no good tidings for you!!

No, this understanding does not hold.

So once again: let us read a concise tafsir and understand the verses first, and only then write down our reflections and musings..

Of course, I am the one who needs this advice most of all. Our Lord, increase us in knowledge and in understanding of the Quran.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
