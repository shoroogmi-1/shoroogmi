Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

The Sun in (Al-A'la, At-Tin, Ash-Shams, Al-Layl, and Ad-Duha)

  • 📅 September 2, 2026
  • ⏱️ 18 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

There is a complete and perfect harmony between Surat Al-A'la, Ash-Shams and Al-Layl: each moves step by step from the creation of the heavens and the earth, to the creation of man, to the human soul, which then splits into two kinds according to how it strives to purify itself; then come the two paths, guidance and misguidance, laid out plainly, and where each path leads in this world and the Hereafter.

Except that Surat Al-A'la is broader than Ash-Shams, and Ash-Shams broader than Surat Al-Layl, while Surat Ad-Duha is narrower than both, and At-Tin narrower still.

So look with me — may Allah let you long enjoy your sight:

(Surat Al-A'la)

Exalt the name of your Lord, the Most High (1) Who created and proportioned (2) And who destined and [then] guided (3)

(Surat Ash-Shams)

By the sun and its brightness (1) And [by] the moon when it follows it (2) And [by] the day when it displays it (3) And [by] the night when it covers it (4) And [by] the sky and He who constructed it (5) And [by] the earth and He who spread it (6) And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it (7) And inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness (8) He has succeeded who purifies it (9) And he has failed who instills it [with corruption] (10).

(Surat Al-Layl)

By the night when it covers (1) And [by] the day when it appears (2) And [by] He who created the male and female (3) Indeed, your efforts are diverse (4)

In Surat Al-A'la He shows us the Creator behind all creation, and so it opens by glorifying and exalting Him. Then comes His creating of everything — heavens and earth, animals, humans and plants — in that He proportioned each in the finest form, then destined for it its measures and provisions, or set them in the very nature of its making, and then guided it. So it is the most general of them, embracing every creation and every kind of guidance — so much so that He guides the ant as it carries a grain on its back.

And in Surat Ash-Shams He begins with the creation of the day and the night, the sun and the moon, the sky and the earth, then the creation of the soul and its kinds, and tells us that its purpose is to strive to perfect it and to attain its righteousness.

And Surat Al-Layl follows the very same pattern: in these surahs there is a marvelous order, even in time and in history — the creation of the heavens and the earth, then the creation of man and his soul, and then the purpose of creation: his striving toward guidance, and the truth that the soul can reach no perfection except by bringing its guidance to perfection.

Taghshiyah (covering) and tasjiyah (shrouding):

In Ash-Shams and Al-Layl, He describes the night as covering (And [by] the night when it covers it) (By the night when it covers).

And in Ad-Duha He uses tasjiyah (And [by] the night when it covers with darkness — idhā sajā).

And the reason — and Allah knows best — is that ghishā' is used for a complete and total covering, while tasjiyah is used for intensity, and it was borrowed here for the night to show how intense that hour is.

Astronomers tell us that when evening falls, the sun has not fully set! It does not fully set until 8 p.m., and in some countries not until eleven at night, and this varies with winter and summer and from one country to another.

So the meaning may be: when the night covers, as the sun vanishes from it entirely — not merely its apparent setting to the naked eye.

And when the day appears, as the moon vanishes entirely from the daytime sky. So it speaks of the day in its fullness and the night in its fullness, each set truly apart from the other, not as the eye mistakenly sees them.

As for tasjiyah: it comes from binding the shroud tightly around the dead, wrapping him in cloth and covering. For the dead may be covered and fully shrouded, and yet not tightly bound and fastened. And so (And [by] the night when it covers with darkness) is the night at its deepest, near its end, in the hour before dawn.

Here the marvelous astronomical order of the surahs becomes clear to you! They move within an orderly, precise orbit of time: Surat Ash-Shams and Al-Layl open with night and day fully covered — say it is eleven o'clock — and Surat Ad-Duha with the late hours of the night, around three in the morning, for example.

Except that the meaning of Surat Ad-Duha is that it reveals the most intense hours for purifying and refining the soul, as though Surat Ash-Shams poses a riddle: how is the soul purified, and when?

And Surat Ad-Duha answers: when the hour is at its most intense! These are the voluntary prayers: the voluntary prayer of the day is the Duha prayer, and the voluntary prayer of the night is more effective for concurrence [of heart and tongue] and more suitable for words.

A heavy load can only be moved by something as heavy or heavier; it makes no sense to tow a broken-down truck with a flimsy bicycle! That is sheer folly. No — true wisdom is to bring a truck like it, or a heavier one, to tow it, get it moving and bring it back to life; and so it is with the soul, weighed down by desires — the very things that stall a person and keep him from moving swiftly and lightly, and shut him off from goodness and blessing. So something heavier than the soul had to lead it, and that was the two revelations, the Quran and the Sunnah (Allah wants to accept your repentance, but those who follow [their] passions want you to digress [into] a great deviation (27) And Allah wants to lighten for you [your difficulties]; and mankind was created weak (28) (An-Nisa)

Lighten what for us? The very passions you read about in the verse before it!

And it is absurd to rely on the self alone to straighten and correct it, with man-made Western methods and programs; that is a bicycle towing a truck! Either the rope between them snaps, or the cyclist's back gives way and breaks.

The ways of the Quran and the Sunnah are indispensable for changing the human soul, alongside human research and methods, Western or Eastern, when their ideas are balanced and sound — they complement one another — except that the two revelations are heavier, and better.

We said that the night and the day are meant in their fullness, as they arrive and in their own time; (And [by] He who created the male and female) is the same: just as He told us of the day and the night in their fullness, He goes on to tell us of the creation of male and female in its fullness — each made wholly distinct from the other.

Tazkiyah (purifying) and tadsiyah (corrupting)

(And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it (7) And inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness (8) He has succeeded who purifies it (9) And he has failed who instills it [with corruption] (10)

We said that He perfected the creation of the night and the day, and the bodily make-up of male and female; and He also perfected the creation of the human soul, granting it the gift of thought and feeling, proportioning it in the finest proportion, wronging it in nothing. Yet it remains for man to complete his soul and set it right, until its darkness stands apart from its light — and that is by leading it, along the path of purification, out of the darknesses and into the clear light!

By earnest, wholehearted striving toward guidance (Indeed, your efforts are diverse) — and sa'y, striving, is faster than walking, as if He were saying: if you could gain guidance in a single day and night, then hurry to it! Of course that is impossible; it is only because guidance and righteousness matter so much, so that his creation may be made complete by the completeness of his character — and this is the trust man is bound to fulfill.

Just as He perfected the creation of the heavens and the earth for you, and perfected your own creation in the finest form, the repayment for this divine goodness is that you complete the purification of your soul and bring your righteousness to perfection (Is the reward for good [anything] but good?).

The wretched soul and the happy soul

Thamud denied [their prophet] by reason of their transgression (11) When the most wretched of them was sent forth (12) And the messenger of Allah [Salih] said to them, "[Do not harm] the she-camel of Allah or [prevent her from] her drink" (13) But they denied him and hamstrung her. So their Lord brought down upon them destruction for their sin and made it equal [upon all of them] (14) And He does not fear the consequence thereof (15).

Having set out the divisions of the happy and the wretched soul (And he has failed who instills it [with corruption]), He gives a real example of one particular wretched soul: the most wretched of the people of Thamud — showing that this is the end of whoever does not strive for guidance and purify himself: his own soul leads him astray, until it trips him, seduces him, and drives him to transgress.

And if you ask me: why does Surat Ash-Shams tell the story of Thamud, and not, say, of Pharaoh, who was more wretched than him?

The surah is brief, so brevity suits it, and one example suffices of the wretched soul that would not be purified — and of how whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger meets such an end in this world, before the Hereafter.

And why does He not give an example of the happy soul that was purified?

It is in the nature of the human mind that when we hold two opposite ideas, an example of the worse lets us grasp the example of the better on our own — and this is intelligence, eloquence, and the art of saying much in little.

The happy soul and the wretched soul: if I give you an example of the wretched soul and where it ends up, you will understand the happy soul and its end without being told.

And the meaning is: if this is the end of the wretched soul in this world, before the Hereafter, then likewise the happy soul — the one that purified itself, strove for guidance, and obeyed Allah and His Messenger — finds happiness in this world before the Hereafter.

And if you ask me: if He has shown the worldly punishment of wretchedness, where is the punishment of the Hereafter!?

We say that He has shown it in Surat Al-A'la:

But the wretched one will avoid it (11) [He] who will [enter and] burn in the greatest Fire (12) Neither dying therein nor living (13) He has certainly succeeded who purifies himself (14) And mentions the name of his Lord and prays (15).

So the traits of the wretched soul are set out in detail in Ash-Shams (When the most wretched of them was sent forth), and his recompense in the Hereafter is made plain in Surat Al-A'la (But the wretched one will avoid it). That is why Surat Al-A'la is broader than Ash-Shams.

That whoever does not put his wretched self to death here, by striving and struggle, and bring the good self to life through purification, giving it a noble and goodly life, and does not believe in the best [reward], will have a Fire in which he neither dies nor lives; and whoever brings his soul to life by keeping the night alive with the Quran and voluntary prayer will have two lives = which together make one good life, in this world and the Hereafter, and his life is worth the life of a whole nation, (And whoever saves one - it is as if he had saved mankind entirely); and what gives the soul life but the Quran and standing in prayer at night?

So He promises success in this world and the Hereafter to the one who purifies his soul; and zakāh includes the meaning of purifying souls, as Ibn Kathir mentioned in his exegesis of verse 156 of Surat Al-A'raf).

From this we may glean that ṣadaqah (charity) — and Allah knows best — shares in the meaning of taṣdīq (believing) and ṣiddīq (the truthful one).

But you prefer the worldly life (16) While the Hereafter is better and more enduring (17) Indeed, this is in the former scriptures (18) The scriptures of Abraham and Moses (19) (Al-A'la)

And since some souls doubt these words — that Allah has a sure and unchanging way of correcting, straightening and guiding the soul — He sealed the surah with what the soul trusts most firmly: writing, scriptures, history. These are Allah's ways of straightening and balancing souls and making life good — so much so that He mentioned them in the scriptures of Abraham (peace be upon him) and the Torah of Moses (peace be upon him); and just as it is told there, it is told again here in the Quran, to affirm this truth and principle, so that you may be certain that the truest way of changing a human being toward the highest and most complete is the Quran and the Sunnah and all they hold (So if you are in doubt, [O Muhammad], about that which We have revealed to you, then ask those who have been reading the Scripture before you. The truth has certainly come to you from your Lord, so never be among the doubters (94)

So the scriptures were not mentioned for the sake of rhyme — far be it — but for whoever doubts these ways and laws, just as you would say to someone who doubts your word: if you don't believe me, ask so-and-so; he can bear witness to what I say.

(Surat At-Tin)

By the fig and the olive (1) And [by] Mount Sinai (2) And [by] this secure city [Makkah] (3) We have certainly created man in the best of stature (4) Then We return him to the lowest of the low (5) Except for those who believe and do righteous deeds, for they will have a reward uninterrupted (6) So what yet causes you to deny the Recompense (7) Is not Allah the most just of judges (8).

Like the pairs of night and day, male and female, the wretched soul and the happy one, here too He pairs the fig with the olive; and what is the link!?

If the reach of man's senses toward the sun and the moon is sight alone, the fig and the olive lie within reach of every sense, even the two most intimate: smell and taste — and that may be the meaning intended here!

The fig tastes sweet, and the olive tastes bitter.

And so the pair is complete: bitterness and sweetness — the human soul tastes its straying as bitterness, pain and misery, and tastes its guidance as the sweetness of faith, joy and delight.

And Mount Sinai: because there He revealed the Torah to Moses (peace be upon him). And this secure city: because revelation came down in it, and because Abraham (peace be upon him) built the Kaaba there, may Allah exalt it.

These two places were honored only because revelation came down in them — so what of a heart into which the Quran came down, and which held it in memory, lived by it and understood it! Would it not be the most honored of hearts and the most complete of souls!

We have certainly created man in the best of stature: in body and in soul,

yet there are flaws and stains in it (Then We return him to the lowest of the low) — except for whoever purifies it and cleanses it of them.

The most just of judges: the real question of divine decree is not why Allah enriched this one and gave that one sufficiency — much of that we can understand — but why He guided this one and let that one go astray. So He pointed us to the truth that decree belongs to Him (And who destined and [then] guided), for all judgment and all knowledge are His; and just as we have accepted the other questions of decree, we must accept this one too. Do you not see that Noah (peace be upon him) called upon his Lord by this very name (And Noah called to his Lord and said, "My Lord, indeed my son is of my family; and indeed, Your promise is true; and You are the most just of judges" (45) (Hud)

So the Most Just of Judges guides whom He wills and leaves astray whom He wills.

The secret of perfect contentment

[He] who gives [from] his wealth to purify himself (18) And not [giving] for anyone who has [done him] a favor to be rewarded (19) But only seeking the countenance of his Lord, Most High (20) And he is going to be satisfied (21).(Al-Layl)

And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied (5) (Ad-Duha)

Sincerity in private worship is easy, but in worship that involves others, and above all in giving, it is very hard — except for those Allah makes it easy for. So there must be sincerity in giving: give for Allah, and hold back for Allah; whoever chases people's approval with his giving burns himself out and dies of sorrow. We have found nothing more restful to the soul than giving for Allah, because that is what brings you contentment.

And the Quran says this again and again:

And the example of those who spend their wealth seeking means to the approval of Allah … (265) Al-Baqarah

No good is there in much of their private conversation, except for those who enjoin charity or that which is right or conciliation between people. And whoever does that seeking means to the approval of Allah - then We are going to give him a great reward (114) (An-Nisa)

All of these are good deeds done toward people, and so He made clear that whoever seeks to please Allah, not people, will have a great reward: Allah's pleasure with him, and provision and gifts poured upon him until he is content; for the recompense matches the deed — he gave only to seek the pleasure of Allah, so his recompense is that Allah gives to him until He has made him content, and his soul is at peace.

(And who brings out the pasture (4) And [then] makes it black stubble (5) We will make you recite, [O Muhammad], and you will not forget (6) Except what Allah should will. Indeed, He knows what is declared and what is hidden (7) And We will ease you toward ease (8).(Al-A'la)

(And who brings out the pasture and [then] makes it black stubble) fits this world and its passing states, but that is not the state of one who recites the Quran: he lives in an endless spring — which is why it is followed by (We will make you recite, [O Muhammad], and you will not forget)

And this is the secret behind the words of the Chosen One ﷺ: "O Allah, make the Quran the spring of my heart" — he did not say the plant of my heart, or its flower! For these wither, while spring is a season that lasts through days and months.

And whoever keeps carrying the Quran is always in this state: in a joy that never breaks off and a gladness that never fades.

Then He makes clear His knowledge of what is declared and what is hidden of the soul's secret schemings; and so Surat Al-A'la is general, and Surat Ash-Shams narrower than it, and so on with the rest of the surahs.

(But as for the favor of your Lord, report [it])

They are all favors, except the Quran: (the favor of your Lord) — an attribution of honor and glory to the Mighty Lord.

You are not, [O Muhammad], by the favor of your Lord, a madman (2)

So remind [O Muhammad], for you are not, by the favor of your Lord, a soothsayer or a madman (29)

And the difference between the favor of the Lord and the favor of Allah:

Ask the Children of Israel how many a sign of evidence We have given them. And whoever exchanges the favor of Allah [for disbelief] after it has come to him - then indeed, Allah is severe in penalty (211)

This was revealed about the Children of Israel

And whoever exchanges the favor of Allah: those who altered and distorted the Torah and the Gospel and turned away from them, abandoning this favor for the little of this world (and all that is in this world is little); but He said "the favor of Allah," using the Name of Majesty to threaten, to warn and to rebuke.

And the favor of your Lord: to show tenderness and to put the heart at ease.

And the favor of your Lord: for the paths to salvation, guidance, light and happiness in this world and the Hereafter that it holds.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
