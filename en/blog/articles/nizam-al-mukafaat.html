Islam and the System of Rewards and Motivation

  • 📅 June 5, 2026
  • ⏱️ 2 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Islam has a remarkable system of rewards, motivation, encouragement, and optimism; among it is the feast, and handing out food, even a little, at every joy that is completed or begun. Of this we mention: the wedding feast (walīmat al-ʿurs, an emphasized Sunnah), the ʿaqīqah at a birth, male or female (Sunnah), the shundukhiyyah: at the engagement, the contract, and the marriage (the start of marriage), the wakīrah: food for a new building, the naqīʿah: food at the return of someone who was away, and the mishdākh: when a reciter completes the Quran.

And a feast for funerals and condolences is disliked, except what is made for the family of the deceased only; so that feasts do not become linked with sorrows. And so when we look at the rulings on feasts in Islam, we find them linked with joys.

And at every stage of a person's transition and change for the better

Food is handed out, even a little; and let them intend it as charity and as thanks to Allah, for thanks brings more, and as joy in Allah's bounty and blessing. {Say: In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy, in that let them rejoice} that he rejoice that the blessing came from Allah, not from himself; and so do not make the reward for yourself, but make it charity, joy, and thanks to Allah, for this gives it more meaning. For “The Messenger of Allah ﷺ one day divided dates among his Companions, and gave each person seven dates” (narrated by al-Bukhari). And I do not agree with the one who says: “Graduation parties, and study, and feasts for jobs, engagements, and a new home are innovations and newly invented things foreign to society!”

For the deep reader of the Shari'ah of Islam knows that it considers such things recommended; that is because they fall under charity, feeding people, and gratitude. And I do not find him (the one who says this, that they are new and an innovation) to be anything but stingy, or someone who has not noticed the occasions for charity and feeding, and does not know the occasions for gratitude, or a gloomy person who has not valued the blessing as it deserves.

So if he becomes skilled at and masters a skill, he gives something in charity, even if a little: Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal handed out walnuts to the boys, five each, when his son al-Hasan became skilled.

And upon completing a stage of learning (even if other stages remain): as Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) and his son did when they slaughtered a camel upon learning Surah Al-Baqarah.

And whoever memorizes 114 surahs, there is no harm in him holding 114 celebrations! And the same goes for the rest of the crafts, sciences, and arts.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
