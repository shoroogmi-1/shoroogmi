The Art of Answering Blackmail Over the Past (As in the Quran)

  • 📅 September 10, 2026
  • ⏱️ 8 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

The past does not cling to a person, not by religious law, not by reason, and not by present state: your day now, and what you live in its new light, is you.

But it is people who chase you with it.

It happened to Moses (peace be upon him) when he killed the Copt: Moses left Egypt, then returned to it laden with the message and the divine light, proclaiming in it to the whole universe that he had changed toward the light and would change the world with him, from harshness and cruelty to the light of prophethood, forbearance, and gentleness {And speak to him with gentle speech}. Then Pharaoh chases him with his past before he chases him with his soldiers {And [then] you did your deed which you did, and you are of the disbelievers (19) ! You claim light, a message, and guidance, and forget that you killed one of our people!

Whoever reflects on what Moses (peace be upon him) said sees that he admitted it in a very brief sentence, because the past does not deserve lengthy argument in answering it {[Moses] said, "I did it, then, while I was of those astray} To correct you, Pharaoh! First, I am not one of the disbelievers; I was a believer, but that act was out of being astray. Facing blackmail over the past means correcting the other person's error and injustice in how he describes you, for he may magnify something small because of the great rancor and resentment he holds against you, so he is unjust, his descriptions overstep, and his words and insults become obscene: “Abusing a Muslim is wickedness.” So the first thing to do is correct those descriptions and not accept them, then make clear the truth of what happened and what he did, without denial, for the scout does not lie to his people. And finally, a very brief statement to answer all blackmail over the past, because Moses (peace be upon him) had before him tasks far greater and bigger than a case that had died and was over. What has passed and gone does not deserve elaboration.

And I picture Moses (peace be upon him), when he answered it, answering with calm, tranquility, and confidence; the proof is that he immediately confronted Pharaoh with his own past: {And is this a favor of which you remind me — that you have enslaved the Children of Israel?}

Tell me, by your Lord, which crime is greater: killing a soul by mistake, or slaughtering the male children of the Children of Israel while they were little, without any sin!? And is sowing division among them greater, or killing a soul by mistake? {And fitnah is greater than killing} {and made its people into factions} Pharaoh divided the Children of Israel into factions so they would quarrel among themselves, fearing they would rule Egypt. And which crime is greater: killing a soul while one's own soul is full of regret, humility, and brokenness {He said, "My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, so forgive me," and He forgave him} and a lasting sense of guilt that drives him to seek forgiveness and to pray intimately in the hours before dawn {He said, "My Lord, indeed I killed from among them a man}! Or the one who killed souls and lives and seized homes in arrogance and haughtiness {And indeed, Pharaoh was haughty within the land, and indeed, he was of the transgressors}? Is the one who went to excess in killing, deliberately, like one who killed a single soul by mistake! There is no comparison! And then, leave all this aside: is the sin of one who was upon light from his Lord equal to one who sinned upon disbelief and polytheism!

And is one who sinned and then came out of the darknesses of misguidance into the light of guidance equal to one who abides forever in his darknesses {and be given to drink scalding water that will sever their intestines!}

Moses did not stay silent at Pharaoh's blackmail over his past; rather he answered, silenced him, refuted his claims, and got his due from him {[Moses] said, "I did it, then, while I was of those astray. So I fled from you when I feared you. Then my Lord granted me wisdom and prophethood and appointed me [as one] of the messengers. And is this a favor of which you remind me — that you have enslaved the Children of Israel?} When he had corrected his descriptions and admitted it, he went on to mention Allah's favor upon him, to get his due from him and from his false claims: that he is a believing messenger, and Allah's grace upon him with the message and the light, proclaiming in it that he had changed and was not the man he was before. And he went on telling him about Allah's favor and bounty upon him, greater than your favor, Pharaoh; it is Allah's favor, not yours for you to remind me of, and you have no right to remind me {[Pharaoh] said, "Did we not raise you among us as a child, and you remained among us for years of your life?}! And what favor is this that you remind me of, enslaving the Children of Israel? Do you consider it a favor that you enslave the Children of Israel and kill them? Where is this favor of yours next to Allah's favor upon me with light and revelation? Moses (peace be upon him) met Pharaoh's reminders of favor with weightier arguments that silenced him, so he did not answer them and fell silent! And he went off opening another topic, hoping he might find a way out! {Said Pharaoh, "And what is the Lord of the worlds?"}.

And among the prophets is one who stayed silent at his people's blackmail over his past and left off answering, out of nobility and patience {They said, "If he steals — a brother of his has stolen before." But Joseph kept it within himself and did not reveal it to them} until the moment of complete victory arrived; for that is a greater response and a more silencing answer than short replies and side battles {They said, "By Allah, certainly has Allah preferred you over us, and indeed, we have been sinners."} We erred twice: the first when we threw you into the well, and the second when we said you were a thief, to your face, while you were supplying us with charity. And by Allah you are not a thief, for a thief is not honored: “He whom You befriend is not humiliated, and he whom You take as an enemy is not honored”

{My Lord, You have given me [something] of sovereignty and taught me of the interpretation of dreams. Creator of the heavens and earth, You are my protector in this world and in the Hereafter}

But the self is strained by words like these, and there has to be a reply, so he delayed the reply a long while and concealed it a short while; as for the short delay, he answered them within himself {He said, "You are worse in position, and Allah is most knowing of what you describe."} And a reply within the self is a reply and an answer, and whoever answers an opponent within himself has given himself his due; for Allah knows best who is the thief, who the kidnapper, and who the wrongdoer. The one who was given his father's belt while he was little and understood nothing is not equal to the one who kidnapped while an adult and threw a small child to the bottom of the well without mercy; and the one who plotted murder is not like one accused of theft who never stole at all {Kill Joseph}; murder is the greater crime, and throwing him into the well was only an effort to be rid of him.

(The theft Joseph's brothers attributed to him, according to Ibn Kathir: that he stole an idol and broke it. And it is said rather that he never stole at all; rather, when his aunt, the daughter of Isaac, wanted to keep Joseph with her, out of love for him, she put on him the belt of Isaac (peace be upon him), which they used to inherit, then she said search for it, and they found it on Joseph, so they said Joseph stole. And he is innocent of this)

As for delaying the reply a long while, that is because he returned it to them in the next-to-last scene of the story {He said, "Do you know what you did with Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?"}!?

Yes; the past for the past, blackmail for blackmail, and for wounds is legal retribution! But it is the intelligence that delays the reply to the time when the opponent has no answer and no book, so he is silenced and finished, and he apologizes and confesses {They said, "By Allah, certainly has Allah preferred you over us, and indeed, we have been sinners."} We are the ones who erred, not you. The small crime vanishes and is swallowed in the great crime, so the one with the greater crime apologizes, and the noble son of the noble forgives, and Allah honors him and raises his standing.

And let no one's provocation over the past drive you to abandon the insight and message you are upon, for it is one of the trials for a servant to be exposed to people's ill thoughts about him (And We have made some of you [people] as trial for others — will you have patience?)

So be patient. Indeed, the promise of Allah is truth. And let them not disquiet you who are not certain [in faith] (60)

So beware: do not let their provocation drive you to foolishness, flightiness, and recklessness (wa lā yastakhiffannaka, “let them not disquiet you”: let them not provoke you) but be confident in the guidance and proof you are upon.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
