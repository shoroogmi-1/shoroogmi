The Art of Answering Blackmail Over the Past (As in the Quran)

  • 📅 September 10, 2026
  • ⏱️ 9 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

The past does not cling to a person — not by sacred law, not by reason, not by his present state: this day of yours, and all you live in its new light, is you.

It is people who keep chasing you with it.

So it was with Moses, peace be upon him, after he killed the Copt. He left Egypt, then returned to it laden with the message and the divine light, proclaiming to the whole universe that he had changed and turned toward the light, and that he would change the world along with him — from harshness and cruelty to the light of prophethood, to forbearance and compassion {And speak to him with gentle speech}. Then Pharaoh pursues him with his past before ever pursuing him with his soldiers {And [then] you did your deed which you did, and you are of the disbelievers (19) ! You lay claim to light, to a message, to guidance — and forget that you killed one of us!

Reflect on Moses' reply, peace be upon him, and you see that he admitted it in one very short sentence, for the past does not deserve long-winded argument in answer {[Moses] said, "I did it, then, while I was of those astray} — Let me correct you, Pharaoh! First of all, I am not one of the disbelievers; I was a believer, but that deed was a straying. To face blackmail over your past means correcting the other's error and injustice in the way he describes you; for he may blow a small thing out of all proportion because of the great rancor and malice he bears you, so he wrongs you, his descriptions run riot, and his words and insults turn vile: “Abusing a Muslim is Fusuq (an evil doing).” So the first thing to do is correct those descriptions and refuse to accept them; then make plain the truth of what happened and what your own part was, without denial — for the scout does not lie to his people. And last of all, one very brief sentence to answer every blackmail over the past; for Moses, peace be upon him, had before him tasks far greater and weightier than a case long dead and done. What has passed and gone does not deserve elaboration.

And I picture Moses, peace be upon him, giving his answer with calm, serenity and confidence; the proof is that he at once confronted Pharaoh with Pharaoh's own past: {And is this a favor of which you remind me — that you have enslaved the Children of Israel?}

Tell me, by your Lord: which crime is greater — killing a soul by mistake, or slaughtering the little boys of the Children of Israel, guilty of nothing!? And which is greater: sowing division among them, or killing a soul by mistake? {And fitnah is greater than killing} {and made its people into factions} — Pharaoh split the Children of Israel into factions so that they would quarrel among themselves, for fear they might come to rule Egypt. And which crime is graver: killing a soul, and then a soul filled with remorse, lowliness and brokenness {He said, "My Lord, forgive me," and He forgave him}, with an abiding sense of guilt that drives it to seek forgiveness and to pour itself out in prayer in the hours before dawn {He said, "My Lord, indeed, I killed from among them someone}! Or killing souls and lives and seizing homes in arrogance and haughtiness {And indeed, Pharaoh was haughty within the land, and indeed, he was of the transgressors}? Is the one who killed without restraint, and on purpose, like one who killed a single soul by mistake! There is no comparison! And then, set all this aside: is the sin of one who stood upon light from his Lord the same as the sin of one steeped in disbelief and polytheism!

And is one who sinned and then came out of the darkness of misguidance into the light of guidance the same as one who abides forever in his darkness {and are given to drink scalding water that will sever their intestines!}

Moses did not keep silent before Pharaoh's blackmail over his past; he answered, silenced him, demolished his claims and took back his due from him {[Moses] said, "I did it, then, while I was of those astray. So I fled from you when I feared you. Then my Lord granted me wisdom and prophethood and appointed me [as one] of the messengers. And is this a favor of which you remind me — that you have enslaved the Children of Israel?} Once he had corrected Pharaoh's descriptions and made his admission, he went on to speak of Allah's favor upon him, to take back his due from Pharaoh and his lying claims: that he was a believing messenger, graced by Allah with the message and the light — proclaiming that he had changed and was no longer the man he had been. And he went on telling him of Allah's favor and bounty upon him, far greater than your favor, Pharaoh! It is Allah's favor, not yours to hold over me, and you have no right to hold it over me {[Pharaoh] said, "Did we not raise you among us as a child, and you remained among us for years of your life?}! And what favor is this that you hold over me — enslaving the Children of Israel? Do you truly see it as a favor, that you enslave the Children of Israel and kill them? What is this favor of yours beside Allah's favor upon me of light and revelation? Moses, peace be upon him, met Pharaoh's reproaches with weightier arguments that left him speechless; he could not answer them, and fell silent! Then off he went, opening another subject, hoping to find a way out! {Said Pharaoh, "And what is the Lord of the worlds?"}.

And among the prophets is one who kept silent before his people's blackmail over his past and left it unanswered, out of nobility and patient endurance {They said, "If he steals — a brother of his has stolen before." But Joseph kept it within himself and did not reveal it to them} until the moment of complete victory should come; for that is a mightier reply and a more crushing answer than quick retorts and side battles {They said, "By Allah, certainly has Allah preferred you over us, and indeed, we have been sinners."} — We sinned twice: first when we threw you into the well, and again when we called you a thief, to your face, while you were providing for us out of charity. By Allah, you are no thief, for a thief is not honored: “He whom Thou takest as an enemy is not honored, and he whom Thou befriendest is not humbled”

{My Lord, You have given me [something] of sovereignty and taught me of the interpretation of dreams. Creator of the heavens and earth, You are my protector in this world and in the Hereafter}

But the soul chafes at words like these, and some reply there must be; so he deferred his reply for a long while, and kept it hidden for a short while. As for the short delay: he answered them within himself {He said, "You are worse in position, and Allah is most knowing of what you describe."} — and a reply within the soul is still a reply and an answer; whoever answers an opponent within himself has given himself his due. Allah knows best who is the thief, who the abductor, and who the wrongdoer. One who was dressed in his father's belt as a little child who understood nothing is not the equal of those who, as grown men, snatched a small boy and flung him to the bottom of a well without mercy; and those who plotted murder are not like one accused of theft who never stole at all {Kill Joseph}. Murder is the graver crime, and throwing him into the well was nothing but a way to be rid of him.

(The theft that Joseph's brothers attributed to him, according to Ibn Kathir: that he stole an idol and broke it. It is also said that he never stole at all: rather, when his aunt, the daughter of Isaac, wanted to keep Joseph with her out of love for him, she fastened around him the girdle (belt) of Isaac, peace be upon him, which they used to pass down as an heirloom; then she said, “Search for it,” and they found it on Joseph, and so they said Joseph had stolen. And he is innocent of this)

As for deferring the reply a long while: he returned it to them in the next-to-last scene of the story {He said, "Do you know what you did with Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?"}!?

Yes: the past for the past, blackmail for blackmail, and for wounds is legal retribution! But it takes intelligence to hold back the reply until a moment when the opponent has neither answer nor argument — so he is silenced, finished, and he apologizes and confesses {They said, "By Allah, certainly has Allah preferred you over us, and indeed, we have been sinners."} We are the sinners, not you. The small offense fades and is swallowed up in the great one, so the one with the greater crime apologizes, and the honorable son of the honorable forgives, and Allah honors him and raises his standing.

Let no one's provocation over your past drive you to abandon the insight and the message you carry; for it is one of life's trials that a servant be exposed to people's ill thoughts of him (And We have made some of you [people] as trial for others — will you have patience?)

So be patient. Indeed, the promise of Allah is truth. And let them not disquiet you who are not certain [in faith] (60)

So take care: do not let their provocation push you into folly, flightiness and recklessness (wa lā yastakhiffannaka, “let them not disquiet you”: let them not provoke you); rather, stand confident upon the guidance and proof you hold.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
