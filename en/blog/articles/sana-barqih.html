Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

(The flash of its lightning almost takes away the eyesight)

  • 📅 August 19, 2026
  • ⏱️ 5 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

The lightning in Surah Al-Baqarah stands for the proofs of the Quran, in His words (The lightning almost snatches away their sight) (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

So the meaning of this verse in Surah An-Nur may be that the light of the Quran almost takes away the eyesight.

The Quran, when it is recited, is not a single light! It is the light of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, upon the light of Jibril, peace be upon him, upon the light of Muhammad ﷺ, from whose blessed lips we received the Quran, upon the light of the angels who come to witness the Quran when it is recited, upon the light of the believer who recites it and listens to it (even if untouched by fire. Light upon light).

The Quran's way is concision and omission, so it will not say: light upon light upon light upon light upon light! That would tire the reader. It is content to name two lights, and from them the reader grasps all the rest of those perfect lights.

Even if untouched by fire: even if he had received no knowledge before it except the knowledge of the Quran, light would come to him, understanding would be complete in him, and every step of comprehension and ascent would be his. Have you not seen your Prophet Muhammad ﷺ? He received no learning, no books of religion or culture, before the Quran, and yet his knowledge reached beyond every horizon!?

(And, [O Muhammad], you are not [engaged] in any matter or recite any of the Qur'an and you [people] do not do any deed except that We are witness over you when you are involved in it (61) (Yunus)

He speaks generally of every matter and every deed: the angels guard it, write it and are present for it. But the Quran He singles out by name. And by Allah, He did not speak generally of every deed and matter and then single out the Quran from among them except for the greatness of its rank and station, and for the majesty of the Most Merciful and His angels bearing witness to it. Every matter and every deed is mentioned to warn, and to wake the sense of being watched; the Quran is mentioned to inspire longing and to exalt its station, for it stands above every matter. Working with it and being busy with it (reciting, listening, memorizing, explaining, pondering) is the greatest of the witnessed deeds kept in the record. It is what makes the believer's face shine and his whole countenance light up when he is handed his book in his right hand: his joy at finding his time with the Quran written in that record is greater than his joy at all his other deeds. It is the tremendous news that, for its greatness, the creatures of the heavens come to attend! Does this happen with the evening news? Does it happen with ordinary talk? Not at all. Those ordinary events are written by angels appointed to them (Allah knows best how many they are; in the Tafsir of Ibn Kathir they are two, perhaps four, and some said thirteen). But the Quran's station is greater: the angels come to witness it, calling to one another from every place, and spread their wings around the reciter, to his right and to his left, perhaps over his shoulder, while one angel places his lips upon the reciter's lips. Nothing so wondrous happens with ordinary things or with the other comings and goings of talk

“When one of you gets up to pray at night, let him use the tooth-stick, for when one of you recites in his prayer, an angel places his mouth over his mouth, and nothing comes out of his mouth but it enters the angel's mouth” (its narrators are trustworthy)

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: “The speech most beloved to Allah is the recitation of the Quran. Whoever sits down to recite it, the angels surround him with their wings, mercy covers them, and they are the guests of the Most Merciful until they plunge into some other talk” (weak)

And reciting the Quran in a place brings blessing to it, brings down mercy upon it, and drives Satan out of it.

“Al-Nawawi said: He urged that voluntary prayers be prayed at home only because that is more hidden and further from showing off, and so that the house may be blessed by it, mercy may descend in it, and Satan may flee from it.”

That is the blessing of the Quran in a place; so tell me, by your Lord, what of the one who carries the Quran in the most hidden place of all: the heart!

And this light is stronger than you imagine. Its power to drive away devils and to master the self and its flaws almost takes away the very breath and soul of those who recite, from the majesty of Allah the Almighty and the light of the angels.

“What is wrong with those people who look towards the sky during the prayer? … They should stop (looking towards the sky during the prayer); otherwise their eyesight would be taken away”

Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, may Allah have mercy on him, explained this hadith, saying:

“And it was said that the meaning is: there is fear for their eyes from the lights with which the angels descend upon those who pray”

The light of the Quran almost takes away the sight of the hypocrites and of the devils among men and jinn; and it almost takes away the sight of the believers.

So if the light of the Quran is strong enough almost to take away keen, physical eyesight, what then of the darknesses and sins that every means has failed to erase, every stratagem has failed to remove or to guard against? And how could its meaning not shine out in the heart of the one who recites it, almost taking away eyes and all things of sense, until nothing is left in the place but light (Everyone upon the earth will perish).

Yes indeed, the light of the Quran is fierce against the devils, fierce upon the heart, so fierce that it sweeps away the darknesses and comes down as light upon the servant: his heart is radiant, his deeds and words are light, and all around him, and his face, is a shining moon.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
