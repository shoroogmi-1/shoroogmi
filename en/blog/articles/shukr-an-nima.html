Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

Ingratitude for a Blessing

  • 📅 August 3, 2026
  • ⏱️ 3 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

It is ingratitude for a blessing to forbid it to yourself when Allah has made it lawful.

Like someone who cuts out a whole kind of food, say dairy or bread, without any medical advice from a trustworthy, reputable doctor, and with no food allergy or anything of the sort; he is simply following the latest social media craze, with no logic and no reason behind it.

Or like the one who banned eggplant, tomatoes, onions, watermelon and every fruit there is -except berries and cherries- and banned bread, rice, meat, poultry, eggs and dairy -a self-made doctor- Good grief, man, what have you left!? What do you actually eat?

Food that does your body no harm, that you have no trouble with, when you are not on a diet and no doctor has told you to avoid it: why on earth do you forbid it!?

Dr. Mustafa Abu Saad noted -in a podcast episode- that this generation faces three great phenomena and challenges in our time:

1- Atheism 2- sexual deviance 3- and eating disorders born of poor nutrition.

That last problem, he says, comes from a generation of doctors that will arise forbidding everything! Don't eat this, or that, or the other, or, or,,,

(Say, "Have you seen what Allah has sent down to you of provision of which you have made [some] lawful and [some] unlawful?" Say, "Has Allah permitted you [to do so], or do you invent [something] about Allah?" And what will be the supposition of those who invent falsehood about Allah on the Day of Resurrection? Indeed, Allah is full of bounty to the people, but most of them are not grateful)

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

"And His saying: {Indeed, Allah is full of bounty to the people) …it may mean that He is full of bounty to the people in what He has made lawful for them of the good things He created in this world, and that He has forbidden them nothing except what harms them in their worldly life or their religion. (but most of them are not grateful) rather, they forbid what Allah has blessed them with,

and they hem themselves in, making some things lawful and some unlawful."

And Muhammad al-Maqdisi cited a statement of Taqi al-Din: "As for refraining from permissible things altogether, like one who refrains from eating meat, or eating bread, or drinking water … and thinks this is part of commendable asceticism, such a person is ignorant and astray — until he mentioned that Allah commanded eating of the good things, and giving thanks to Him"

And the rule says: Whoever is harmed by honey, it is forbidden to him: that is, if honey harms this person's body, let him keep away from it until he recovers. But these people forbid with no harm, no logic, no reason and no justification!

If only they forbade one thing or two; no, they forbid everything but a handful!

Just like the people of the Jahiliyyah, who used to forbid kinds of food with no basis in revelation, reason, cause, medicine or harm — such as forbidding the slaughter and eating of a she-camel that had borne a string of females one after another, and forbidding milk to their women, and other strange things that we see today, only in a new and different guise, in the name of science and medicine!

— Shoroog Almehmadi
