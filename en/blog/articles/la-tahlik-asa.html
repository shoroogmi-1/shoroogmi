Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

“They say, ‘Perish not of grief; be steadfast’”| Reflections on the Command Not to Kill Oneself with Grief

  • 📅 September 13, 2026
  • ⏱️ 11 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

The Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful opens Surah Ash-Shu'ara in the most dazzling way:

(Ta, Seen, Meem (1) These are the verses of the clear Book (2) Perhaps, [O Muhammad], you would kill yourself with grief that they will not be believers (3)

He makes clear that the disbelievers drew no benefit from the Book, so do not be like them, killing yourself with sorrow because they will not believe; even grief brings no benefit. Just as they gained nothing from the shining truth, you gain nothing by killing yourself with sorrow. Bākhiʿ means one who kills. It comes from bakhaʿa: to carry the slaughter all the way to the bikhāʿ, a vein at the nape of the neck. That is the furthest point a slaughter can reach, and when it is reached, the soul departs. (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

And Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, says: (And do not kill yourselves [or one another]. Indeed, Allah is to you ever Merciful (29) (An-Nisa) Perhaps one way of killing the self is to crush it under gloom and grief until it can no longer step back into the joy of life, or return to the things that once made it glad.

So if He has forbidden you to kill it with grief, then you must rouse it with every permissible delight until gladness and joy come back to it!

There are only two roads. Either you kill yourself with grief, weighed down by sorrow over life's worries and over people, or you bring yourself joy, giving back to your soul its lost right to delight and its missing share of everything sweet and gladdening.

And since we are still in Surah Ash-Shu'ara, The Poets, the poets have matchless gems on this very meaning, such as the one who chanted:

O you whose soul is being slain by longing

for a thing the Fates have taken from his hands

He calls out to him with the vaguest of addresses: O you there, killing yourself with grief over something the Fates naḥathu, turned away, from him.

(Then perhaps you would kill yourself through grief over them [ʿalā āthārihim], [O Muhammad], if they do not believe in this message, [and] out of sorrow) (Al-Kahf)

What is asked of you is this: do what you were commanded, fulfil your duties, and take the means as you strive toward your goals. Then if the ends prove out of reach, if your efforts fall short of them and the Fates turn them away, there is no call to burden yourself with grieving over them

“In this verse and those like it there is a lesson: the one charged with calling people to Allah must convey the message and strive by every means that leads to guidance, and close off the paths of misguidance and error, to the utmost of his ability, relying on Allah in all of it. If they are guided, well and good; if not, let him neither grieve nor sorrow, for that weakens the soul and wrecks one's strength, and there is no benefit in it. Rather, he should carry on with the task he was charged with and set out to do; whatever lies beyond that is outside his power” (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

The point is this: there is no sense in a grief so heavy that it kills you, buries your thoughts and your creativity alive, and ends your life over what has passed and is gone. To destroy yourself with excessive gloom, to deny yourself life's joys and pleasures for the sake of this sorrow, has no meaning and no use, except to breed more of the same sorrow! Sorrow begets sorrow, round and round in a circle that never ends.

So forcing yourself to be patient is better for you than forcing yourself to grieve! Pressing your soul toward the joy of life and leading it to its pleasures, even when it is unwilling, is better than leaving it in a sorrow that brings nothing you wish for and settles nothing you seek. Going forward in joy, busying yourself with a worthwhile hobby or useful knowledge, with work, with talk among people, unfurls sails of happiness that carry the ships along, and perhaps they will bring you to an island you never imagined.

For joy strengthens the soul and the will, so it grows strong enough to work, moves with zeal and energy, and the sails of opportunity open before it

And sorrow, what does it do?! It produces: nothing. Helplessness and sloth. It is the killing of life and the slaughter of the soul. Dead land is brought back to life by human hands; and your own soul, you are the one responsible for bringing it to life, and you, too, are the one who slaughters it with crushing grief.

And Imru' al-Qais says:

Halting their mounts over me there, my companions ... say: “Perish not of grief; bear it with grace”

And I picture it as though Jibril, peace be upon him, had stood over his head, consoling him, urging him not to kill his soul with worry (Then perhaps you would kill yourself..)

“He says: they halted over me, that is, for my sake, or standing over my head while I sat beside their camels and mounts, saying to me: Do not perish from excess of grief and depth of anguish; adorn yourself with patience. In short: they halted their mounts over him, bidding him be patient and forbidding him to give way to anguish.” (Sharh al-Zawzani)

And look at the word he chose: tajammal! It holds bearing up, and it also holds making yourself beautiful, reaching for everything that gladdens the soul and lets joy in. He must push himself to it even if he does not want to open his shut-up soul, even if it can see nothing but its own closed doors.

And people say in everyday speech: “Tajammal, there's nothing left!” They say it when the effort is spent, the mind has been driven to exhaustion, and fatigue and weariness have climbed over the walls. They say it to cheer you on: You've reached your goal! You're right there, so why turn back now? Can't you hold yourself together for the last step? Do anything at all, just get there!

Put on a brave face, be patient, endure, anything! Just get it done!

The English have something like it, a lovely expression; they say:

“Burn the midnight oil”

That is: when your work is nearly finished, stay up with it late into the night and see it through to the end, whatever it costs you in sleep and fatigue.

And our own fellow says: “Lift the curtain of night and undo its braid, and take no rest but on the tops of the high peaks”

Tarafa ibn al-Abd says:

Halting their mounts over me there, my companions ... say: “Perish not of grief; be steadfast”

“Tajallud: making oneself hardy, that is, holding oneself to patience.” (al-Zawzani)

It seems the poets say (perish not) at the parting of loved ones. The Quran is different. It says (bākhiʿ), and it opens with a word of reasoning; it shields the soul of the Prophet ﷺ from being made common, so it does not aim the perishing at his very self as the poets do (perish not of grief). Instead it guards his noble soul ﷺ, choosing the language of reasoning (perhaps you) over a direct prohibition. And it uses bākhiʿ, an active participle, naming the one to whom the act of bakhʿ, killing oneself with grief, belongs. It uses the active participle to bring the good news swiftly and to dispel the illusion!

Yes, because crushing sorrow is born of illusion, not reality. The extra sorrow in it does not match the facts or what actually happened. Such grief carries an exaggerated, overblown reading of events; it is an extremist reading of what happened!

So let us fight extremism in thought and in feeling!

As for bringing good news swiftly, it is like saying: Blessing has come! The companion has come! Here the active participle (bākhiʿ) is brought forward to forbid the soul and steer it away from harmful sorrow, to lay bare what that sorrow really is, and to strip away the deceit of Iblis, who saddens the believer's heart with what is not real, what brings no benefit and is in fact deeply harmful, unless Allah permits (Private conversation is only from Satan that he may grieve those who have believed, but he will not harm them at all except by permission of Allah. And upon Allah let the believers rely (10) (Al-Mujadila)

Iblis: the name comes from iblās, utter despair, for he despaired of Allah's mercy forever. And we have been forbidden (and You know best) to resemble him or follow him. So keep yourself far from crushing worry, for it is Iblis's first step toward other steps.

And know that Satan has many roads to saddening the believers, because bringing grief into the believer's heart is a goal in itself for him: it saps the believer's strength for worship and his drive to do good. To reach that goal, he will use any means

Nor is it limited to two people whispering together. Al-Mawardi said, explaining what is meant by private conversation from Satan: “it is the dreams a person sees in his sleep.” “And whoever brings grief upon the believers bears a likeness to Satan” (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

So the believer takes the Quran as his model in being good news for the believers: he brings gladness into the hearts of believers and spreads hope, while whoever resembles Satan spreads gloom and brings grief into people's hearts.

“He, Glorified is He, likened this to a man whose loved ones and dear ones have parted from him, so that he is falling apart with regret over their traces, killing himself with longing for them and yearning at their parting. He brought this singular image to guard His comparison from being made common; for Allah the Exalted wished to console His Prophet ﷺ and to lighten the worry and pain that had come upon him, so He presented the situation in the form of hopeful expectation, then perhaps you, though what is meant is prohibition, that is: do not kill yourself, do not destroy yourself, out of sorrow that they do not believe” (al-Tafsir al-Muharrar)

Because killing yourself with deep sorrow weakens the soul, and a weakened soul is of no use: it neither worships Allah gladly and with contentment, but only grudgingly and in pain, nor does it benefit itself or others! “Al-Kisa'i said: bakhaʿtu al-arḍ by farming, when you have made the land weak by plowing it over and over; and a man bakhaʿa himself when he wore himself down. On this basis, the meaning of ﴿bākhiʿun nafsaka﴾ is: wearing it down and straining it until you destroy it” al-Razi.

For crushing grief (may the Most Kind, the Most Merciful watch over you) weakens your soul even for worship, dampens your resolve, and stands between you and the earning of good deeds and higher ranks.

Over their traces:

Āthār, traces, belong to the past; and these two poets stood over the ruins of the past, weeping.

And people commonly speak of someone who died ʿalā athar, on the traces, of a loved one! That is: when the first one died, his traces and signs remained, his clothes, his perfume, and the other stood grieving over those traces for a while, until he followed him into death and perished too!

“It is said: so-and-so died ʿalā athar so-and-so, meaning after him. The origin of this is that when a person dies, his signs and traces remain for a while after his death, then they fade and vanish entirely. So if his death comes close upon the death of the first, it happens while the traces of the first still remain, and so it is right to say: so-and-so died ʿalā athar so-and-so.” (al-Razi)

The Quran urges you to leave a trace, instead of weeping over one!

For in the first lies your life, and in the second your death. Have you not heard what the Lord of Might has said?!

(Indeed, it is We who bring the dead to life and record what they have put forth and what they left behind [āthārahum], and all things We have enumerated in a clear register (12) (Ya-Sin)

Their traces: We record what they did and the practices, good or bad, that they set going in their lives and that people followed after them; so it is either a reward for them or a burden upon them.

So always set this choice before yourself (may the Most Merciful watch over you) whenever a deadly grief confronts you:

1- Either I waste my time weeping over a dead trace (of no use whatsoever)

2- Or I make the most of my life by building up the earth and filling it with what benefits me and others, and I leave good traces after my death that flow back to me as rivers of reward and the richest of good deeds, and they bring me joy.

So choose for yourself. You are free now. I commend you to Allah.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
