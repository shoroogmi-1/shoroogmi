Lemons and Yūsufī (“Mandarins”): On the Wisdoms in the Story of Yusuf

  • 📅 August 28, 2026
  • ⏱️ 6 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Throughout Surah Yusuf: All-Knowing, All-Wise.

He knew beforehand with knowledge, so He brought about for him rulings and decrees that end in His knowledge, and the furthest point His judgment reached, and in which His wisdom culminated.

And among the wisdoms in the story of Yusuf (peace be upon him) that I reflected on:

Allah wanted to guide a number of the prisoners at his hands; and had he not been among them, they would not have known tawhid.

That Allah wanted for Ya'qub (peace be upon him) an attachment to the Truth rather than to the creation, and wanted for Yusuf an estrangement and isolation in which he would be more cut off from the creation and more attached to the Truth; so this is one of the purposes of isolation, estrangement, and the departure of loved ones: the completion of the blessing of tawhid upon the servant (and complete His favor upon you and upon the family of Ya'qub, as He completed it before upon your two forefathers, Ibrahim and Ishaq. Your Lord is All-Knowing, All-Wise (6) Yusuf (Today I have perfected your religion for you, completed My favor upon you, and chosen Islam as your religion ۚ (3) (Al-Ma'idah), then once the blessing of tawhid is complete, He returns to the servant the beloved things he was used to -if He wills-; because the wisdom has run its course, the benefit has been achieved, and the blessing has been completed.

Prison by its nature has inmates with quarrels among them; so He removed these from them through tawhid and calling to Allah, and as training for Yusuf (peace be upon him) in solving dilemmas when he would later become the Aziz of Egypt and cases would be brought before him.

That a woman in the trial of chastity wishes for death, like Maryam (peace be upon her) (I wish I had died before this and been a thing utterly forgotten), while a man wishes for prison! (He said: My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they invite me to (33) And perhaps a woman, in the nature of her soul, is quicker in haste than a man, and her solution to problems is to end them on the spot, while a man is steadier, slower, and calmer than her; and that is why the Lawgiver placed divorce in his hand, not hers, and had it been in her hand, it would be like death, something she craves at every moment! -this does not apply to men and women in general -

That Allah wanted to show the despairing and hopeless that He prevails over His affair, however the enemies plot and do what they do; if He wills a matter, He overcomes them.

He cast him into the well as a preparation for prison for a longer time! So that he would know that just as he came out of the well before, with no might or power of his own, while he was weak and small, He is able to bring him out of prison while he is a strong man.

He gave him a means of getting out of prison, which is what He granted him of interpreting dreams; and likewise the beneficial talents Allah has blessed a person with may be a means of getting out of what he is in, if he makes good use of them and benefits people with them.

He did not ask for anything in return for interpreting the dream; and a person, at the beginning of getting out of the crises he is in, is like this: little pay in return for the benefit of his talent, then doors and provisions open for him after that, as Yusuf came to rule.

And had he asked for money, perhaps he would have angered the king, and Yusuf (peace be upon him) knew better the king's nature and the situation he was in. But you see him postpone the benefit and the request until after that (He said: Set me over the storehouses of the land).

So a person, at the start of his affair, postpones benefits so they grow great with time, and does not rush recklessly to ask for them all at once and so miss all of them. Meaning, had Yusuf (peace be upon him) asked for a fee for interpreting it, he might have been given it and then had nothing after it, and no rule! But he postponed the benefit and the taking, so that it would be greater with time.

And his patience with the trial of prison and the well and his brothers' plotting is what gave him this deliberateness, the wisdom of gradual steps, and leaving off impulsiveness and haste.

Perhaps Yusuf's brothers had not experienced severe trial, and that was the reason for their lack of wisdom.

And whoever reads the characters of his brothers finds in their nature impulsiveness, haste, and turmoil before the reconciliation that took place. Among that is their saying (Kill Yusuf or cast him out to some land, so your father's attention will be yours alone, and after that you can be a righteous people (9) (And they sold him for a paltry price, a few counted dirhams, and they were indifferent to him (20) And had there been an atom of patience in them, they would have asked for more money for him! But their aim was only to get rid of him by any means, even for the cheapest price (So when they returned to their father, they said: O our father, measure has been denied to us, so send our brother with us so we may get measure; and we will surely guard him (63)

And the fā' is for immediate sequence (So when they returned) just like that! Without any gradualness in the words and the address: send our brother Binyamin with us, come on! Forgetting what they did to Yusuf, as if they had not yet regretted their deed! And the criminal mind never knows regret.

And among the haste in their nature is their saying before the Aziz, without any consideration or covering for their brothers or saving of face (They said: If he steals, a brother of his has stolen before ۚ(77)

And had there been any modesty in them and some covering for family, they would not have said such words before a stranger they did not know! And here, too, they cared only about themselves, with no concern for the image of brothers and family before people, or protecting reputation and the like! Absent in those of bad nature.

And likewise, had there been some deliberateness in them, they would have sought to clarify the ruling and law of the land on taking the thief! (They said: Its penalty is that he in whose bag it is found, he himself shall be its penalty ۚ(75) So they did not seek clarification or ask for some time to consult, and it appears their answer was one, in one voice. May Allah forgive them and pardon them.

If the ignorant one does not respond to reason and good character, because emotion and desire have covered over him; then it is better to withdraw, leave, and flee, as Yusuf admonished the wife of the Aziz and she did not desist, so he fled from her.

For continuing the conversation with the likes of these opens the doors of evil, and there is no benefit in it.

Praise be to Allah, and I seek forgiveness from Allah the Magnificent

— Shoroog Almehmadi
