Lemons and Yūsufī (“Mandarins”): On the Wisdoms in the Story of Yusuf

  • 📅 August 28, 2026
  • ⏱️ 6 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

All through Surah Yusuf, one refrain: Knowing, Wise.

He knew, long before, with a perfect knowledge; and out of it He brought forth for Yusuf rulings and decrees that lead back to His knowledge, to the furthest end His judgment reached, the very point where His wisdom found its fulfillment.

Among the wisdoms I have pondered in the story of Yusuf (peace be upon him):

Allah willed to guide a number of the prisoners at his hands; had he not been there among them, they would never have known tawhid.

Allah willed for Ya'qub (peace be upon him) a heart attached to the Truth rather than to creation; and He willed for Yusuf an exile and a solitude in which he would be cut off from creation and bound ever closer to the Truth. This is one of the purposes of solitude, of exile, of the departure of loved ones: to complete the blessing of tawhid upon the servant (and complete His favor upon you and upon the family of Jacob, as He completed it upon your fathers before, Abraham and Isaac. Indeed, your Lord is Knowing and Wise (6) Yusuf (This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion ۚ (3) (Al-Ma'idah). And once the blessing of tawhid is complete, He returns to the servant the beloved things he once knew, if He wills; for the wisdom has run its course, the good has been realized, and the blessing is complete.

Prison, by its nature, is full of inmates quarreling among themselves; Allah dissolved those quarrels through tawhid and the call to Allah. It was also a training ground for Yusuf (peace be upon him) in untangling hard cases, for the day he would become the Aziz of Egypt and disputes would be brought before him.

In the trial of chastity, a woman wishes for death, like Maryam (peace be upon her) (Oh, I wish I had died before this and was in oblivion, forgotten), while a man wishes for prison! (He said, "My Lord, prison is more to my liking than that to which they invite me." (33) Perhaps it is that a woman, by her very nature, is quicker to rush than a man, and her answer to a problem is to end it on the spot, while a man is steadier, slower and calmer. That is why the Lawgiver placed divorce in his hand and not in hers; had it been in hers, it would have been like death, something she longs for at every turn! (This does not hold for all men and all women.)

Allah willed to show the despairing and the hopeless that He prevails over His affair, however the enemies scheme and whatever they do; when He wills a thing, He overpowers them.

He cast him into the well to prepare him for a far longer stay in prison! So that he would know: just as He brought him out of the well before, with no might or power of his own, when he was small and weak, so He is able to bring him out of prison now that he is a grown and strong man.

He gave him a way out of prison: the gift He had granted him of interpreting dreams. In the same way, the useful talents Allah blesses a person with may become his way out of whatever he is in, if he uses them well and benefits people through them.

He asked nothing in return for interpreting the dream. And so it is with a person in the first days of climbing out of a crisis: small reward for the good his talent brings; then, afterward, the doors and the provisions swing open, as kingship came to Yusuf.

Had he asked for money, he might well have angered the king; Yusuf (peace be upon him) knew better the king's temperament and his own position. But see how he put off the benefit, and the asking, until later ([Joseph] said, "Appoint me over the storehouses of the land.").

So at the start of his path a person defers his gains, so they may grow great with time, and does not rush recklessly to claim them all at once and lose them all. Had Yusuf (peace be upon him) asked for a fee for the interpretation, he might have been given it, and then had nothing after it, no kingdom at all! But he deferred the gain and the taking, so that it would be greater with time.

It was his patience through the trial of the prison, the well and his brothers' scheming that gave him this unhurried calm, the wisdom of moving step by step, and freedom from impulse and haste.

Perhaps Yusuf's brothers had never tasted severe trial, and that is why they had so little wisdom.

Whoever reads the characters of his brothers finds in them impulsiveness, haste and turmoil before the reconciliation came. Among this is their saying (Kill Joseph or cast him out to [another] land; the countenance of your father will [then] be only for you, and you will be after that a righteous people (9) (And they sold him for a reduced price - a few dirhams - and they were, concerning him, of those content with little (20) Had there been an atom of patience in them, they would have asked more money for him! But their only aim was to be rid of him, by any means, at even the paltriest price (So when they returned to their father, they said, "O our father, [further] measure has been denied to us, so send with us our brother [that] we will be given measure. And indeed, we will be his guardians" (63)

The fā' signals immediate sequence (So when they returned): just like that! No easing into it, no gentle approach: send our brother Binyamin with us, come on! As though they had forgotten what they did to Yusuf, as though they had not yet regretted their deed! The criminal mind never knows regret.

Their hasty nature shows, too, in what they said before the Aziz, with no regard for their brothers, no covering for them, no saving of face (They said, "If he steals - a brother of his has stolen before." ۚ(77)

Had there been any modesty in them, any instinct to cover their own family, they would never have said such a thing before a stranger they did not know! Here, too, they cared for nothing but themselves: no concern for how their brothers and family looked to others, for their good name or anything else! Such concern is simply absent in those of base nature.

Likewise, had they been at all deliberate, they would have asked what the ruling and law of the land were on taking a thief! ([The brothers] said, "Its recompense is that he in whose bag it is found - he [himself] will be its recompense." ۚ(75) They asked for no clarification, no time to consult; it seems their answer came all at once, in one voice. May Allah forgive them and pardon them.

When an ignorant person will not answer to reason or good character, because emotion and desire have smothered him, it is better to withdraw, to leave, to flee; just as Yusuf admonished the wife of the Aziz, and when she would not stop, he fled from her.

To carry on the conversation with people like these only opens the doors of evil, and there is no good in it.

Praise be to Allah, and I seek forgiveness from Allah the Magnificent

— Shoroog Almehmadi
