Subtleties and Reflections on Hajj: A Comparison of Surahs Al-Baqarah and Al-Hajj

  • 📅 August 8, 2026
  • ⏱️ 2 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

Reflections on the links between the Hajj passages in Surah Al-Baqarah and Surah Al-Hajj, and their reminders of resurrection, trials, and submission to Allah.

One of the wisdoms behind the rites of Hajj is to remind people of the Day of Gathering. For this reason, the Hajj verses in Surah Al-Baqarah conclude by reminding us that we will be gathered before Allah, while Surah Al-Hajj opens by drawing attention to the Hour and its tremendous upheaval.

Surah Al-Baqarah presents some of the meanings of Hajj in summary, while Surah Al-Hajj elaborates on them. Surah Al-Hajj gives more detail about the terrors of the Gathering, emphasizes the rulings of Hajj, and responds to those who deny resurrection.

Facing trials

The verses draw attention to the person who worships Allah on an edge: if good comes, they feel reassured, but if a trial comes, they turn away. In contrast, Surah Al-Hajj describes the humble-hearted, whose hearts tremble at the remembrance of Allah, who remain patient through hardship, establish prayer, and spend from what Allah has provided them.

The Hajj verses in Surah Al-Baqarah prohibit indecent speech, disobedience, and argument during Hajj. This trains pilgrims to leave quarrelling, insults, cursing, and anger behind. Removing blameworthy traits comes before adorning oneself with good ones. Whoever abandons these habits becomes better prepared for a humble, reverent heart; reverence does not sit alongside needless argument and cursing.

Whoever restrains anger and leaves arguing with a fellow believer for Allah's sake may be granted greater light in the heart, understanding, and wisdom. Patience is tested during trials and hardship, when a person must not turn back.

Hajj and striving

Surah Al-Baqarah mentions striving before discussing Hajj, while Surah Al-Hajj begins with scenes of the Hour and Hajj before mentioning striving. This invites reflection on the succession of tests, submission, and victory, and on how circumstances change throughout the life of a community.

The land of Hajj and the Gathering

The open spaces of the sacred sites evoke the image of the land of resurrection. Mention of ruined towns reminds the pilgrim of the homes, lands, palaces, and loved ones left behind. A person comes to the rites alone and stripped of worldly display, just as they will be resurrected.

As pilgrims travel between Mina, Arafat, and Muzdalifah, they can remember humanity's journey toward Allah on the Day of Gathering. Let them have hearts that understand and remember their final destination.

These reflections on the connections between verses and the purposes of Hajj call us to mindfulness of Allah, patience through trials, abandoning quarrels, humility before Him, and preparation for the Day of Gathering.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
