Branches of the Relationship with the Quran

The Many Branches of Our Bond with the Quran, and the Degrees of Holding the Book with Strength

  • 📅 July 15, 2026
  • ⏱️ 8 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

In an earlier clip I spoke about branching out our bond with the Noble Quran, and I promised to say more about it later.

By branching out your bond with the Noble Quran, I mean that your relationship with it should take many forms, not just one. It is not right to settle for recitation alone, or for memorization alone, or for tafsir and understanding alone. These are all paths, and you must take up each one and walk it, but step by step. We will see how this path unfolds, and what the evidence for it is.

The bond with the Noble Quran begins with recitation, as much of it as Allah opens up for you, even if it is just one verse, or two, or three a day. Then you build up gradually: half a juz', then a whole juz', then three, then five.

Then, as Allah the Exalted opens the way for you beyond recitation, it is best to move on to memorization, and then to understanding the Quran: reading tafsir and drawing on its books and sciences. And at every one of these stages, you must live by the Noble Quran from the very first letter of it you read.

Where did we take this from? From the supplication of Ibrahim (peace be upon him) in Surat al-Baqarah: (Our Lord, and send among them a messenger from themselves who will recite to them Your verses and teach them the Book and wisdom and purify them. Indeed, You are the Exalted in Might, the Wise).

Fakhr al-Din al-Razi said that the point is to begin in this order: first with recitation (who will recite to them Your verses), and then (and teach them the Book), that is, explain to them the meanings of the Noble Quran; by "the Book" is meant the Quran, and by "wisdom," the Sunnah. (Who will recite to them Your verses): he begins with recitation, and after it come tafsir and understanding (and teach them the Book and wisdom).

And so one branch of our bond with the Noble Quran is our bond with the Prophetic Sunnah, which can never be separated from the Noble Quran. That is why you must read the Prophetic Sunnah and come to understand it and know it well; this too is part of your bond with the Noble Quran. Your bond with the Noble Quran is not with the Quran alone, for the Quran is inseparable from the Prophetic Sunnah; and that is why He said: (and teach them the Book and wisdom).

Allah the Exalted also says: (So when We have recited it [through Gabriel], then follow its recitation. Then upon Us is its clarification [to you]); so one begins with reading, in this order: recitation and memorization, devoting oneself to them; then explanation and tafsir; then contemplation.

Allah the Exalted rebuked the People of the Book, saying: (And among them are unlettered ones who do not know the Scripture except in wishful thinking, but they are only assuming). That is, the bond these People of the Book had with it (meaning here the Jews) was one of reading and nothing more: they pronounced its words correctly, but neither lived by it nor understood its meanings. And why did they not understand its meanings? Because they never devoted themselves to its tafsir and to grasping what it meant, never asked the people of knowledge and learned from them; and so they neither understood it nor lived by it.

Why do the People of the Book not live by it? Because they are ignorant of its meanings and its tafsir. Had they studied the tafsir and the meanings and understood them, they could have lived by them, and gained from them, and profited; but they stopped at reciting the words.

That is why Ibn Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him) said: In this verse Allah the Exalted rebukes those who read the Book as mere words without understanding its meanings, and it shows how important it is to understand the Noble Quran and its tafsir.

Allah the Exalted also says: (Every time it lights [the way] for them, they walk therein). This parable, the "water parable," as some exegetes called it, is one Allah the Exalted struck about the Noble Quran: the lightning stands for the light of the Noble Quran and its proofs. Every time this lightning, the Quran, flashes, the hypocrites are able to make use of it, walking only as far as its light reaches. So they do gain something from the Noble Quran, but only a little, through the faith they put on display: with it they deceive the believers outwardly, while in truth they deceive only themselves. They walk as far as the Quran lights the way for them, and life stirs in them again, and they benefit; but when that light goes out as they drift away from the Noble Quran, (but when darkness comes over them, they stand [still]): they come to a halt, unable to move, unable to benefit.

And here is a reflection that came to me on this verse: a person's deeds are measured by his understanding of the Noble Quran. The more his understanding grows, the wider his reading in tafsir, the richer the meanings and knowledge he gains through studying it, the wider his practice of the Noble Quran becomes, and the better his way of life, down to his very thoughts and leanings. And the narrower his understanding of the Noble Quran, the slighter his reading in tafsir, the narrower and scantier his deeds, and the narrower his understanding even of life itself.

For to understand the Noble Quran is to understand life. The more you understand the Noble Quran, the better you understand yourself, your life, the people around you, the Hereafter, and so on.

Another branch of our bond with the Noble Quran is contemplation. But is contemplation possible without understanding? No, understanding is essential. And so: the wider it grows (every time it lights the way for them), the wider your understanding of the Quran and its meanings, not only do your deeds increase, but Allah the Exalted also opens the doors of contemplation for you. So whenever you find someone to whom contemplation has been opened, know that he reads, studies, and listens to the tafsir of the Noble Quran a great deal.

Now I come to holding the Book with strength. When Allah the Exalted raised the mountain, at-Tur, above the Children of Israel to strike awe into them, He took a covenant from them, and part of it was: (Take what We have given you with determination), bi-quwwah: what We have given you of the Torah, of the Book, take it in earnest, with zeal, with energy, and with a firm resolve to preserve it.

And this resolve, this strength, is for what? For the branches of this bond: understanding, memorization, and recitation. Take it with resolve, energy, and zeal, not with listlessness and sloth.

When I memorized the Noble Quran, all praise be to Allah, I thought, I truly believed, that once I had it by heart I had reached the end and was done. But I discovered that memorizing the Quran is half the strength, not the whole of it. It matters enormously, but it is only part of the strength, not all of it.

Then I came to know the strength that comes from studying tafsir, reading it, and reading and listening to it a great deal, and that this is perhaps what completes the second half of the strength. I might even say, to drive the point home: half the strength is memorization, and the other half is understanding.

That is why, when someone asked a scholar: What should I do after memorizing the Noble Quran? Should I devote myself to the qira'at or to tafsir? He told him: Devote yourself to tafsir; it comes before the qira'at.

This will differ for each person, according to their goals; but this is what Allah, Glorified and Exalted, commanded: that we not busy ourselves with words alone. That is why He rebuked the People of the Book for being taken up with words. The qira'at are beautiful and good, but let a person read, even a little, from the concise, accessible books of tafsir so that he understands; and once he understands, he can set off for the ten qira'at and the other sciences of the Noble Quran.

So half the strength is memorization, and the other half is understanding. And when a person is granted the strength of understanding, he is granted, by Allah the Exalted's leave, contemplation and deeds: contemplation and deeds are the fruit of these two strengths.

And when contemplation and deeds have been opened up to someone, know that contemplation was opened to him only because he acted. Indeed, I would set them in this order: deeds first, then contemplation; for how could contemplation be opened to someone who does not live by what is in the Book?

But living by it begins from the very first letter of the Quran you read. Every time understanding is opened to you, you act on it, and you go on living by the Book all the time. There is no day, no hour, in which we do not live by the Book; no holiday, no break from living by the Quran.

Holidays are for worldly work. As for the Quran, it fills every breath and every particle of a person's life, and he lives that life by the Noble Quran.
