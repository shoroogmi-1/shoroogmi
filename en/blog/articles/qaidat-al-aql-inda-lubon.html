The Rule of the Human Mind According to Gustave Le Bon

  • 📅 August 30, 2026
  • ⏱️ 2 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

The rule of the human mind according to Gustave Le Bon is moving it from the compound to the abstract.

So in his view, the education of a child must be built on what is tangible, not abstract.

My critique of it: this is not true!

Because a child is capable, by his innate nature, of grasping the most abstract of concepts, like his innate faith in Allah; and even if the child does not understand them now, repeating them to him until he comes of age makes it easy for him to understand and recall them.

If he learned the rules of grammar he might not understand them as a child, but at least the abstract concepts would have passed through him: subject and object, declinable and indeclinable.. so when he learns them again as an adult he is able to understand them clearly and easily, and he thinks age plays a role, which is true from one angle; from another angle, repetition from childhood into adulthood plays a role.

“O boy, I will teach you some words: be mindful of Allah and He will protect you; be mindful of Allah and you will find Him before you; when you ask, ask Allah; and when you seek help, seek help from Allah..”

Dr. Said al-Kamali commented on this hadith, saying: "And what would a child know of these great meanings? We do not think that Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him), as a boy, understood them, but this does not prevent teaching a child big concepts; he will understand them later when he comes of age."

And if we relied on Le Bon's method in teaching children, it would be a disaster! Because in a hidden way it raises him on what is experimental and tangible, so with this method the child would not be able to plant faith and values in his spirit and soul.

The child must be taught what is abstract in a simple, easy way, using similes and metaphors if possible.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
