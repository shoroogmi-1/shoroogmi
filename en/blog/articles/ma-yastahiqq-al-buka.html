What Deserves Tears and What Does Not: Reflections on the Weeping of the Prophet ﷺ

  • 📅 October 8, 2026
  • ⏱️ 7 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

1- The forbidding of tears over what lies ahead: what Ibn al-Qayyim called “the weeping of fear” (Zad al-Ma’ad).

Weeping over something you fear may happen in the future comes from Satan, and such weeping does not become a believer. Let him seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed. For this belongs to the unseen: what do you know of whether the thing you dread will happen or not? And suppose it does happen, what do you know of the gentleness and mercy Allah will show you! And if what you fear does come, it will be something you are given the strength to bear.

Look back at your past, at the things you dreaded that did come to pass: did you not marvel at Allah's gentleness and mercy toward you! And here you are today, alive and provided for, among us, laughing! So why weep over what lies ahead? It is nothing but time thrown away.

This fear-mongering is Satan's work (That is only Satan who frightens [you] of his supporters)

“When the Prophet ﷺ sent him to Yemen, he (peace be upon him) went out with him, Mu’adh riding and the Messenger of Allah walking beside his mount. He said: ‘O Mu’adh, it may be that you will not meet me after this year of mine, and that you will pass by my grave and my mosque.’ And Mu’adh wept, stricken at parting from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He said: ‘Do not weep, O Mu’adh, for weeping is from Satan.’” (Sahih, with a break in its chain)

He forbade him only because it concerned something yet to come.

So hold back your tears from whatever lies ahead of you, and come: on this journey of ours, let us learn what they are worth shedding for.

2- Weeping over what is lost of this world

“She said: By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, I could tell the weeping of Abu Bakr from the weeping of Umar while I was in my room. And they were, as He said, merciful among themselves. He said: So I said: Then what did the Messenger of Allah do? She said: His eye would not shed tears over anyone; but when he was grieved, he would simply take hold of his beard”

I do not know the chain of this report, but it has come in another wording whose narrators are trustworthy, though some of them are disputed: I said to Aisha: Did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ weep? She said: No, but he would grip his beard ﷺ.

Reflect on the hadith:

a- He wept over no one, because his heart was wholly bound to Allah, because he wanted nothing of this world, and because he had given himself to the Hereafter as a yearning lover gives himself to the one he longs for. Whatever is lost of this world is not worth a tear.

b- Restraint, then redirection: first he masters his tears and holds himself back, so that they do not fall but stay held in his eyes; then he turns this bodily expression away from tears and into gripping his beard or chin. This means he had reached the summit of mastery over his emotions. And do not be surprised that a righteous person can reach such a level: whoever has let go of his cravings, found his solace in the Quran, and stripped himself of this world will find this restraint easy

When a young man is used to wading into death,

the easiest thing he crosses is the mire;

and he who commands the fortresses, and they never defy him,

the rugged highlands and the plains obey.

(Al-Mutanabbi)

c- Weeping over someone is mercy: it is established that he ﷺ wept over his son Ibrahim (peace be upon him), and this weeping was born of mercy and tenderness, as Ibn al-Qayyim says in Zad al-Ma’ad. So behind the believer's sorrow there is an awareness that steers it: he weeps out of mercy, not out of regret for what is gone; his tears are the tears of one who seeks his reward with Allah, not the tears of resentment. And how far apart the two outlooks are.

3- Weeping over the past

I divide it into two kinds:

1- Weeping over the distant past: weeping over something that years have passed over. This is not to be encouraged.

It is not established that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, years after the death of Khadijah or of Ibrahim (peace be upon him), used to weep! Rather, he grieved for them, felt tenderness and longing for them, and remained loyal to the dead.

It is in the very way the human mind works to look back on the distant past as beautiful, with all its sweetness and all its bitterness. Do you weep over your childhood? No, you call it the good old days.

This is what is known as rosy retrospection: we let the bad of the past fade and remember what was good and lovely. And the further the past recedes, the more time goes by, the stronger this bias becomes. This very hour you see in a rather negative light; but once it is a day old, then a week, its shadows fade and it turns rosy.

The optimist leans toward remembering the blessings of the past, and clears his mind to recall how lovely a past it was; the pessimist leans toward remembering its darkness, its afflictions rather than its blessings.

This does not mean we must never look at the past in a negative light; we need to, but without drama! Without tears, without brooding, standing apart from the feelings of that time, and looking fairly at the lessons we took from it.

That is why the soundest course is that, as the years go by, a person comes to accept Allah's decree, makes peace with the distant past, and gives himself to his present.

2- Weeping over the recent past: something close to your present, within the span of months and weeks. There is no harm in this.

4- Weeping out of anguish, resentment and regret: anguish at Allah's decrees and refusal to accept them, or regret over the fleeting portions of this world. All of this ought to be given up.

How he ﷺ wept:

Not with noise and wailing, but quietly, with tears that welled and ran down without sobbing; and when his weeping grew strong, they would hear a sound from his chest like the simmering of a pot.

Finally, these ideas can be lived; they are not ideals out of reach. Begin with what is easiest and put it into practice. Each time, practise one more thing.

For example, begin with what is easiest for you: not weeping over the future. Then the next easiest: not weeping over what years have passed over (the distant past). Then the hardest: not weeping over this world and what you have lost of it (the recent past).

That said, there is nothing wrong with weeping! We are not talking about obligations! These are only reflections; but when you put them into practice, try them out and test them, you will be amazed at how your inner wellbeing has grown happier and steadier than before.

And changing this behaviour is simply a decision you make; it can be changed

“From Aisha, who said: We were returning from Hajj or Umrah and were met at Dhul-Hulayfah. The young men of the Ansar were coming out to meet their families. They met Usayd ibn al-Hudayr and gave him the news of his wife's death, and he covered his head and began to weep. I said: May Allah forgive you! You are the companion of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, with all your precedence and standing, and you weep over a woman? She said: He uncovered his head and said: You are right. By my life, I have every reason to weep over no one after Sa’d ibn Mu’adh, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said of him what he said. She said: I said: And what did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say of him? He said: The Throne shook at the death of Sa’d ibn Mu’adh” (its chain is hasan)

See how Usayd ibn al-Hudayr was weeping over his wife, and then resolved to weep over no one after Sa’d ibn Mu’adh, may Allah be pleased with them both

And experience and the years play their part.

This, and Allah knows best; and success comes only from Allah.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
