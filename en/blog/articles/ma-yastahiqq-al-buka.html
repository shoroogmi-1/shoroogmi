What Deserves Tears and What Does Not: Reflections on the Weeping of the Prophet ﷺ

  • 📅 October 8, 2026
  • ⏱️ 7 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

1- The prohibition of weeping over what lies ahead: this is what Ibn al-Qayyim called “the weeping of fear” (Zad al-Ma’ad).

Weeping over something you fear will happen in the future is from Satan, and this weeping does not befit the believer. Let him seek refuge in Allah from Satan, the accursed. For this belongs to knowledge of the unseen, and what do you know about whether what you fear will happen or not? And suppose it did happen, what do you know of Allah’s kindness to you and His mercy! And if what you dread does happen, it is only something you will be helped through.

And look at your past and the things you feared that did happen: were you not amazed at Allah’s kindness to you and His mercy! And here you are today, alive, provided for, among us and laughing! So why weep over what lies ahead? It is a waste of time.

And this frightening is from Satan (That is only Satan who frightens [you] of his supporters)

“When the Prophet ﷺ sent him to Yemen, he (peace be upon him) went out with him, Mu’adh riding while the Messenger of Allah walked beside his mount. He said: ‘O Mu’adh, perhaps you will not meet me after this year of mine, and you will pass by my grave and my mosque.’ Mu’adh wept in grief at parting from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and he said: ‘Do not weep, O Mu’adh, for weeping is from Satan.’” (Sahih, with a break in its chain)

He only forbade him this because it was about something that lay ahead.

So hold back your tears from what lies ahead of you, and let us set off on this journey of ours to learn what to shed them for.

2- Weeping over what has passed of the world

“She said: By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, I could tell the weeping of Abu Bakr from the weeping of Umar while I was in my room. And they were, as He said, merciful among themselves. He said: So I said: Then what did the Messenger of Allah do? She said: His eye would not shed tears over anyone, but when he was grieved, he would only take hold of his beard”

I do not know about the chain of this, but it has been narrated in another wording whose narrators are trustworthy, with disagreement over some of them, which is: I said to Aisha: Did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ weep? She said: No, but he would grip his beard ﷺ.

Reflect on the hadith:

a- He did not weep over anyone, and that is because of how completely his heart was attached to Allah, and his complete detachment from the world and devotion to the Hereafter, the devotion of a longing lover. So whatever has passed of the world does not deserve weeping over.

b- Control, then emotional redirection: first he controls his tears and holds himself back so they do not flow, and the tears are held in his eyes, and he redirects this bodily expression from tears to expressing it by gripping his beard or chin. This means he reached the peak of controlling emotions. And do not be surprised that a righteous person reaches this level, for whoever turns away from desires, finds his comfort in the Quran, and strips himself of the world, finds this emotional control easy

When a young man grows used to plunging into death

the least of what he passes through is mud

And whoever commands fortresses, and they never defy him,

the rugged lands and the plains obey him

(Al-Mutanabbi)

c- Weeping over someone is mercy: it is established that he ﷺ wept over his son Ibrahim (peace be upon him), and this weeping of his was out of mercy and compassion, as Ibn al-Qayyim said in Zad al-Ma’ad. So behind the believer’s sorrow there is a guiding awareness: weeping out of mercy, not weeping in regret over what has passed; the weeping of one who seeks reward with Allah, not the weeping of discontent. And there is a difference between the two perspectives.

3- Weeping over the past

And I divide it into two parts:

1- Weeping over the distant past: that you weep over something years have passed over, and this is not recommended.

It is not established from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that years after the death of Khadijah or Ibrahim (peace be upon him) he would weep! Rather, he would grieve, feel tenderness, and long for them, out of loyalty to the deceased.

And the cognitive nature of a human being is that he looks at the distant past as beautiful, with all its sweetness and bitterness. Do you weep over childhood? No, you call it the beautiful time.

This is what is called rosy retrospection bias (Rosy Retrospection): we neglect the bad things of the past and remember what is positive and beautiful. And the further away the past and the more time passes, the more this bias grows. You see your hour right now more negatively, then when it becomes a day, then a week, its negativity lessens and it becomes rosy.

The optimist is biased toward remembering the blessings of the past and freeing his mind to remember how beautiful a past it was, and the pessimist is biased toward remembering the darkness of the past and the afflictions, not the blessings.

This does not mean we should never look at the past negatively; rather, we need that, but without drama! Without weeping and rumination, detached from the feelings of that period, and with a fair look at the lessons we took from it.

That is why the safest course is that, with the years, a person becomes content with Allah’s decree, makes peace with the distant past, and busies himself with his present.

2- Weeping over the recent past: this is something close to your present, within the range of months and weeks. There is no harm in this.

4- Weeping out of anguish, discontent, and regret: anguish at Allah’s decrees and not being content with them, or regret over the shares of this passing world; all of this should be given up.

The manner of his weeping ﷺ:

It was not with clamor and screaming, but quietly, with tears that are shed and flow down without sobbing, and if his weeping intensified, they would hear a buzzing sound from his chest.

Finally, these meanings can be applied and are not idealistic ideas. Start with what is easy in them and apply it. Each time, apply something from it.

For example, start with the easiest thing for you: not to weep over the future. Then the easier: not to weep over what years have passed over (the distant past). Then the hardest: not to weep over the world and what you have missed of it (the recent past).

That said, there is no harm in weeping! We are not talking about obligations! These are merely reflections, and when you apply them, try them out, and test their soundness, you will be amazed at how your mental health has become happier and more stable than before.

And changing this behavior is only a decision you make, and it can be changed

“From Aisha, who said: We came back from Hajj or Umrah and were met at Dhul-Hulayfah. The young men of the Ansar were meeting their families. They met Usayd ibn al-Hudayr and gave him news of his wife’s death, so he covered his head and began to weep. I said: May Allah forgive you, you are the companion of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and you have the precedence and standing that you have, and you are weeping over a woman? She said: So he uncovered his head and said: You have spoken the truth. By my life, it is more fitting that I not weep over anyone after Sa’d ibn Mu’adh, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said about him what he said. She said: I said: And what did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say about him? He said: The Throne shook at the death of Sa’d ibn Mu’adh” (its chain is hasan)

So reflect that Usayd ibn al-Hudayr was weeping over his wife, then decided not to weep over anyone after Sa’d ibn Mu’adh, may Allah be pleased with them both

And experience and the years have their role.

This, and Allah knows best, and success is from Allah.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
