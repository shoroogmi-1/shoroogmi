Reflections on the Quran & Sunnah

(And What Is That in Your Right Hand, O Moses?)

  • 📅 October 9, 2026
  • ⏱️ 5 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

(And what is that in your right hand, O Moses?) He asks, though He, Glorified and Exalted is He, knows it better than Moses himself — yet He asks in order to put him at ease! The Lord, the Exalted, wanted Moses, peace be upon him, to feel at home in the conversation before surprising him with the weighty mission (Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed)! Imagine if He had opened the conversation with that command! Moses would have sensed apprehension within himself and felt estranged! But He wished to set him at ease first, so He asked him about the staff — a staff He knew, and could see in his right hand.

It's like saying to a child: Ooh! What's this toy in your hand?

And you know perfectly well what it is! You can see it — but you want to put him at ease and be gentle with him, or to prepare him for being asked to play with his brother.

Now imagine you had said to him first: Give me the ball! (hāti, with a kasra, is said to a boy and a girl alike.) He would have bristled, shied away from you, and run off.

(And what is that in your right hand, O Moses? (17) He said, "It is my staff; I lean upon it, and I bring down leaves for my sheep and I have therein other uses." (18) [Allah] said, "Throw it down, O Moses." (19) So he threw it down, and thereupon it was a snake, moving swiftly. (20) [Allah] said, "Seize it and fear not; We will return it to its former condition. (21) And draw in your hand to your side; it will come out white without disease — another sign, (22) that We may show you [some] of Our greater signs." (23).

Moses, peace be upon him, felt at ease, and so he began listing the staff's uses, when he could have stopped at: It is my staff.

But so deeply at ease was he with Allah that he went on to count off its other uses, which means the question had achieved its aim — the knower's feigned ignorance: (And what is that in your right hand, O Moses?).

Then He commanded him to throw down the staff, and when it turned into a snake he was afraid of it; so his Lord reassured him: just as He has the power to turn it from a staff into a snake, He has the power to turn it from a snake back into a staff. So after ease came fear, and then his soul was soothed and felt safe. Then He gave him still more strength, confidence and firmness, commanding him to draw his hand in beneath his other arm, and it would come out white — a sign more reassuring still, and stronger proof that his Lord was with him and had complete power.

A reading of the inner state of Moses, peace be upon him: so far he has felt ease, then fear, then safety, then strength and ever greater firmness

And what came next? His Lord charged him with the heaviest mission and the hardest command, and all those exchanges had been nothing but a preparation of heart and mind for accepting it: (Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed)

Notice that Moses, peace be upon him, never once objected. It is something strange and wondrous, and to this day it astonishes me!

Let's imagine a different scenario:

The moment Moses came before his Lord, He commanded him at once: Go to Pharaoh!

The reaction might well have been: Why?! But Pharaoh is a tyrant, and, and, and…

But He had prepared him for this command beforehand, in many ways:

putting him at ease, and letting him witness the power of Allah the Exalted (That We may show you [some] of Our greater signs) — so that you may believe, so that your soul may settle, grow calm and be at peace; just as it found peace in His commands and in His power to turn the staff into a snake and then back into a staff, so that you may know that I am able to do all things, that I am Strong — and this means that I am with you!

Moses, peace be upon him, felt that peace, and so he accepted the hard command the instant it was laid upon him, meeting it with no objection — not even a question about the details: How? Who? When?

And from the depth of his heart's tranquillity and his soul's contentment with the command of Allah the Exalted, he turned to Him in prayer:

(Go to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed. (24) [Moses] said, "My Lord, expand for me my breast [with assurance] (25) and ease for me my task (26) and untie the knot from my tongue (27) that they may understand my speech. (28) And appoint for me a minister from my family — (29) Aaron, my brother. (30) Increase through him my strength (31) and let him share my task (32) that we may exalt You much (33) and remember You much. (34) Indeed, You are of us ever Seeing." (35) [Allah] said, "You have been granted your request, O Moses." (36).

From this we come to see that language is a social art, not mere words and musical rhythm.

So if you want to be socially intelligent, be linguistically intelligent first. Language teaches you social tact and good manners: gentleness, preparing the listener's heart before a direct command or prohibition, taking things step by step, and knowing how to engage with others.

If someone were to build thousands of relationships yet lacked care and precision in choosing his words, those relationships would fall apart, and he would fail with people and with his own family. Language is the root of social life.

I recommend al-Maydani's al-Balāghah al-ʿArabiyyah; it is good on this subject.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
