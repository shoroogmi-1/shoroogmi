(And What Is That in Your Right Hand, O Moses?)

  • 📅 October 9, 2026
  • ⏱️ 5 min read
  • 📖 … completed reads
  • ❤️ …

(And what is that in your right hand, O Moses?) He asks him, though He, Glorified and Exalted is He, knows it better than Moses does, but He asks him for the purpose of putting him at ease! The Lord the Exalted wanted to put Moses (peace be upon him) at ease in talk and conversation before surprising him with the heavy mission (Go to Pharaoh; he has exceeded all bounds)! Imagine if He had begun with this command from the very start of the conversation! He would have felt fear and been unsettled! But He wanted to put him at ease first, so He asked him about the staff while knowing it and seeing it in his right hand.

It's like saying to a child: Wow! What's this toy in your hand?

And you know what it is! And you see it, but you want to put him at ease and be gentle with him, or to prepare him for the request to play with his brother.

And imagine if you had said to him first: Give me the ball! (hāti, with a kasra, is said to a boy and a girl alike) He would have felt alienated, shied away from you and run off.

(And what is that in your right hand, O Moses? (17) He said: It is my staff; I lean on it, and with it I knock down leaves for my sheep, and I have other purposes for it. (18) He said: Throw it, O Moses. (19) So he threw it, and behold, it was a serpent, slithering. (20) He said: Take hold of it and do not be afraid; We shall restore it to its first state. (21) And press your hand to your side; it will emerge white, without blemish, as another sign, (22) so that We show you of Our greatest signs. (23).

Moses (peace be upon him) felt at ease, so he began listing the uses of the staff, when he could have stopped at saying: It is my staff.

But out of how much at ease he felt with Allah, he began listing the other uses, which means the purpose of the question, a knowing feigned ignorance, had been fulfilled: (And what is that in your right hand, O Moses?).

Then He commanded him to throw down the staff, and he felt afraid of it when it turned into a snake, so his Lord reassured him that just as He has the power to turn it from a staff into a snake, He has the power to turn it from a snake back into a staff. So after ease he felt fear, then his soul was reassured and felt safe, then He gave him more strength, confidence and empowerment by commanding him to press his hand under his other arm so that it would come out white, and this is a more reassuring sign and stronger proof that his Lord is with him and has complete power.

A reading of the psychology of Moses (peace be upon him): so far he has felt ease, then fear, then safety, then strength and greater empowerment

Then what came after? His Lord gave him the heaviest mission and the hardest command, and those exchanges were nothing but psychological and mental preparation for accepting this command: (Go to Pharaoh; he has exceeded all bounds)

Notice that Moses (peace be upon him) never objected at all, and this is something strange and wondrous; to this day I am amazed by it!

Let's imagine a different scenario:

The moment Moses came before his Lord, He immediately commanded him: Go to Pharaoh!

The reaction might have been: Why?! But Pharaoh is a tyrant, and so on and so on.

But He had prepared him beforehand for this command with many things:

putting him at ease, and letting him see the power of Allah the Exalted (so that We show you of Our greatest signs) so that you may believe, and your soul may settle, be calm and reassured; just as it was reassured by His commands and His power to turn the staff into a snake and then return it to a staff, so that you know that I am able to do all things, Strong, and this means I am with you!

Moses (peace be upon him) felt peace, so he accepted the hard command immediately when it was given to him, meeting it with no objection, not even a question about the details: How? Who? When?

And out of the depth of his heart's tranquility and his soul's contentment with the command of Allah the Exalted, he began to call on Him:

(Go to Pharaoh; he has exceeded all bounds. (24) He said: My Lord, open my chest for me, (25) and make my task easy for me, (26) and loosen a knot from my tongue (27) so they may understand what I say. (28) And give me a helper from my family, (29) Aaron, my brother. (30) Reinforce my strength through him (31) and make him a partner in my task, (32) so that we may exalt You much (33) and remember You much. (34) You have always been watching over us. (35) He said: You have been given what you asked for, O Moses. (36).

From this we realize that language is a social manner, not just words and musical rhythm.

So if you want to be socially intelligent, be linguistically intelligent first. Language teaches you taste and social etiquette, such as gentleness and psychologically preparing the listener before a direct command or prohibition, gradualness in matters, and how to interact socially.

And if someone built thousands of relationships but did not choose his words with care and precision, his relationships would fall apart and he would fail socially and within his family. Language is the root of social life.

I recommend reading al-Balāghah al-ʿArabiyyah by al-Maydani; it is good on this subject.

— Shoroog Almehmadi
